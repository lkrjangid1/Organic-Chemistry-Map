Why a reaction map works better than a list

Organic chemistry questions in JEE and NEET rarely ask for one reaction in isolation. They ask you to convert one compound into another, identify the product of a two-step sequence, or pick the right reagent. That is a path-finding problem, and paths are easiest to learn on a map.

Organic Chemistry Map puts every compound on a single canvas and draws each reaction as an arrow from reactant to product. Follow arrows to build conversion chains, compare reagents that lead to the same product, and see at a glance which functional groups connect to which.

How it helps you in each exam

JEE Main organic chemistry

JEE Main rewards quick, accurate recall of standard NCERT conversions and reagents. Revise them by functional group instead of by chapter.

  • Alcohol, aldehyde, ketone and acid interconversions in one view
  • Aldol, Cannizzaro, Reimer–Tiemann, Kolbe and Friedel–Crafts reactions
  • Diazonium salt reactions: Sandmeyer, Gattermann, Balz–Schiemann, azo coupling
  • Search any compound to see every reaction going in and out

JEE Advanced organic chemistry

JEE Advanced tests multi-step conversions, mechanisms and stereochemistry. Trace full reaction sequences and read the mechanism for each step.

  • Rearrangements: Beckmann, Pinacol–Pinacolone, Hofmann, Curtius, Schmidt, Wolff, Claisen
  • Stereochemistry: SN2 inversion, anti-E2, Sharpless and CBS reactions
  • Kinetic vs thermodynamic control in sulfonation
  • Pericyclic reactions: Diels–Alder, electrocyclic ring opening, Oxy-Cope

NEET organic chemistry

NEET organic chemistry is NCERT-based and leans on named reactions, distinguishing tests and biomolecules. Revise them visually in minutes.

  • Named reactions with reagents and conditions for every arrow
  • Tests for amines: Carbylamine (isocyanide) and Hinsberg tests
  • Biomolecules: osazone formation, mutarotation, Kiliani–Fischer, Ruff degradation
  • Polymers: condensation polymerisation and copolymerisation
