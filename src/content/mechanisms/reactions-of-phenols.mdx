## What are the reactions of phenols?

Phenol, C₆H₅OH, reacts in two places. The **O–H bond** behaves as a weak acid and as a nucleophile (salt formation, ethers, esters). The **ring** is strongly activated by the –OH group, so it undergoes **electrophilic aromatic substitution** at the **ortho and para** positions far more easily than benzene, often without a catalyst. A few reactions touch the C–O bond or the whole ring: zinc dust reduction, oxidation to quinones and catalytic hydrogenation. How phenol is made is covered on [Preparation of phenols](/preparation/preparation-of-phenols/).

## 1. Acidity: reactions of the O–H group

Phenol (pKa about 10.0) is about a million times more acidic than ethanol (pKa about 15.9) because the **phenoxide ion is stabilised by resonance** into the ring. It dissolves in NaOH but is weaker than carbonic acid, so it does not liberate CO₂ from NaHCO₃. Electron-withdrawing groups at ortho/para (–NO₂) increase acidity; electron-releasing alkyl groups (cresols) decrease it. Details: [Acidity of phenols](/comparisons/acidity-of-phenols/).

<Eq ce="C6H5OH + NaOH -> C6H5O^- Na^+ + H2O" />

Reactions that use phenoxide or the O–H group:

| Reagent | Product | Page |
|---|---|---|
| NaOH, then R–X (SN2) | Aryl alkyl ether, Ar–O–R | [Williamson ether synthesis](/reactions/williamson-ether-synthesis/) |
| RCOCl or (RCO)₂O (base or acid) | Phenyl ester | Esterification |
| C₆H₅COCl + aq. NaOH | Phenyl benzoate | [Schotten–Baumann reaction](/reactions/schotten-baumann-reaction/) |
| Allyl bromide, base; then heat | Allyl phenyl ether → o-allylphenol | [Claisen rearrangement](/reactions/claisen-rearrangement/) |
| Phenyl ester + AlCl₃ | o- and p-Hydroxyaryl ketones | [Fries rearrangement](/reactions/fries-rearrangement/) |

<KeyPoint>
Aryl halides cannot be used to make the Ar–O bond by SN2, so in a Williamson synthesis the phenol must supply the oxygen: **phenoxide + alkyl halide**, never alkoxide + aryl halide.
</KeyPoint>

## 2. Electrophilic substitution on the ring

The –OH group is a **strongly activating, ortho/para-directing** substituent; the phenoxide ion is even more strongly activating.

| Reaction | Conditions | Product |
|---|---|---|
| Nitration | Dilute HNO₃, 298 K | o- + p-nitrophenol (separated by steam distillation) |
| Nitration | Conc. HNO₃ | 2,4,6-Trinitrophenol (picric acid), poor yield |
| Bromination | Br₂ in CS₂ or CHCl₃, low temperature | o- + p-bromophenol |
| Bromination | Bromine water | 2,4,6-Tribromophenol (white precipitate) |
| Sulphonation | Conc. H₂SO₄ | Phenolsulphonic acids |
| Kolbe (carboxylation) | NaOH, then CO₂ under pressure, then H⁺ | Salicylic acid |
| Reimer–Tiemann | CHCl₃ + aq. NaOH, then H⁺ | Salicylaldehyde |
| Reimer–Tiemann (CCl₄) | CCl₄ + aq. NaOH, then H⁺ | Salicylic acid |
| Azo coupling | C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻, mildly alkaline, cold | p-Hydroxyazobenzene (orange) |
| Hydroxymethylation | HCHO, acid or base | o- and p-Hydroxymethylphenol → Bakelite |
| Elbs oxidation | K₂S₂O₈, aqueous alkali | Hydroquinone (p-dihydroxybenzene) |

<Eq ce="C6H5OH + 3Br2 ->[H2O] 2,4,6-Br3C6H2OH v + 3HBr" />

**Notes on the key ones**

- **Nitration:** o-nitrophenol is steam-volatile because of **intramolecular** hydrogen bonding; p-nitrophenol is held back by **intermolecular** hydrogen bonding. Picric acid is now made by sulphonating phenol to phenol-2,4-disulphonic acid first and then nitrating.
- **Bromination:** no FeBr₃ is needed because the activated ring polarises Br₂ by itself. See [Bromine water test](/tests/bromine-water-test/).
- **[Kolbe–Schmitt reaction](/reactions/kolbe-schmitt-reaction/):** sodium phenoxide is reactive enough to attack the weak electrophile CO₂ (classically about 125 °C and 100 atm); acid work-up gives salicylic acid, mainly ortho. Acetylating salicylic acid gives **aspirin** (acetylsalicylic acid).
- **[Reimer–Tiemann reaction](/reactions/reimer-tiemann-reaction/):** dichlorocarbene from CHCl₃ attacks phenoxide ortho; hydrolysis gives –CHO.
- **[Diazo coupling](/reactions/diazo-coupling-reaction/):** the diazonium ion is a weak electrophile, so only phenoxide-type rings react.
- **Formaldehyde:** phenol adds HCHO at the ortho and para sites to give hydroxymethylphenols, which condense through –CH₂– bridges to phenol–formaldehyde resins. See [Bakelite and formaldehyde resins](/concepts/bakelite-and-formaldehyde-resins/).
- **[Elbs persulphate oxidation](/reactions/elbs-persulphate-oxidation/)** introduces a second –OH para to the first; the related [Dakin reaction](/reactions/dakin-reaction/) turns o- or p-hydroxybenzaldehyde into catechol or hydroquinone.

## 3. Reactions of the C–O bond and the ring as a whole

- **Zinc dust:** heating phenol with zinc dust gives **benzene** and ZnO.
- **Oxidation:** chromic acid (for example Na₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄) oxidises phenol to **p-benzoquinone**, a conjugated cyclohexadienedione. In air phenol slowly darkens as coloured quinone mixtures form. Quinones are easily reduced back to hydroquinones.
- **Catalytic hydrogenation:** H₂ over Ni (heat) reduces the ring to give **cyclohexanol**.

<Eq ce="C6H5OH + Zn ->[\Delta] C6H6 + ZnO" />
<Eq ce="C6H5OH ->[Na2Cr2O7, H2SO4] O=C6H4=O" />
<Eq ce="C6H5OH + 3H2 ->[Ni][\Delta] C6H11OH" />

<ExamTip>
For "phenol → benzene" the answer is **Zn dust, heat**. For "phenol → salicylic acid" the answer is **Kolbe** (NaOH, CO₂, H⁺) or Reimer–Tiemann with **CCl₄**. For "phenol → salicylaldehyde" it is Reimer–Tiemann with **CHCl₃**.
</ExamTip>

## 4. Tests for phenols

| Test | Reagent | Positive result |
|---|---|---|
| [Ferric chloride test](/tests/ferric-chloride-test/) | Neutral FeCl₃ | Violet colour (iron phenoxide complex) |
| [Liebermann nitroso test](/tests/liebermann-nitroso-test/) | NaNO₂ + conc. H₂SO₄, then dilute, then add NaOH | Deep green/blue, red on dilution, blue again in alkali |
| Phthalein test | Phthalic anhydride + conc. H₂SO₄, heat; then NaOH | Phenolphthalein forms: pink in alkali |
| Bromine water | Br₂(aq) | Decolourised; white precipitate |
| [Ceric ammonium nitrate test](/tests/ceric-ammonium-nitrate-test/) | CAN | Dark brown precipitate (alcohols give red) |

## Reaction-type vocabulary

These terms appear again and again in phenol chemistry:

| Term | Meaning | Phenol example |
|---|---|---|
| Condensation | Two molecules join with loss of a small molecule such as H₂O | Phenol + HCHO → Bakelite; phenol + phthalic anhydride → phenolphthalein |
| Carboxylation | Introduction of a –COOH group | Kolbe reaction |
| Decarboxylation | Removal of –COOH as CO₂ | Salicylic acid → phenol on heating |
| Dehydration | Removal of H₂O | Formation of phenolphthalein |
