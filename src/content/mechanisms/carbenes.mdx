## What is a carbene?

A **carbene** is a neutral species containing a **divalent carbon with only six valence electrons**: two bonds and two non-bonding electrons, written R₂C:. The simplest is **methylene, :CH₂**. Carbenes are short-lived, highly reactive intermediates; their main reactions are **addition to C=C bonds (cyclopropanation)** and **insertion into σ bonds**. In the JEE syllabus they appear as the hidden intermediate in the Reimer–Tiemann and carbylamine reactions.

## How carbenes are generated

- **α-Elimination from chloroform:** a strong base removes the proton, and CCl₃⁻ loses Cl⁻. Two groups leave from the same carbon, hence "α".
- **Loss of N₂ from diazomethane:** light, heat or copper decomposes CH₂N₂ into :CH₂ and nitrogen gas.
- **Carbenoids:** zinc and CH₂I₂ give ICH₂ZnI, which behaves like a carbene ([Simmons–Smith reaction](/reactions/simmons-smith-reaction/)).

<Eq ce="CHCl3 + OH- -> CCl3^- + H2O" />

<Eq ce="CCl3^- -> :CCl2 + Cl-" />

<Eq ce="CH2N2 ->[h\nu or \Delta] :CH2 + N2" />

## Singlet vs triplet carbene

| Property | Singlet carbene | Triplet carbene |
|---|---|---|
| Electrons | Paired (↑↓) in one orbital | Two unpaired (↑ ↑) in different orbitals |
| Hybridisation and shape | sp², bent (about 102° for :CH₂) | Often drawn as sp and linear in exam answers; measured angle 125–140° for :CH₂ |
| Magnetism | Diamagnetic | Paramagnetic |
| Hund's rule | Not followed | Followed |
| Character | Electrophile; empty p orbital gives Lewis-acid behaviour | Reacts as a diradical |
| Stability | Less stable for :CH₂ | Ground state for :CH₂ (about 33 kJ/mol lower) |
| Addition to alkenes | Concerted, syn, stereospecific | Stepwise, non-stereospecific (cis/trans mixture) |

<KeyPoint title="Phase and substituents">
Triplet carbenes are more stable in the gas phase; singlets are found more often in solution. Substituents with lone pairs (Cl, O, N) push electrons into the empty p orbital and stabilise the singlet, so dichlorocarbene is a bent, diamagnetic singlet.
</KeyPoint>

## Addition to alkenes: cyclopropanation

A singlet carbene adds to both alkene carbons at once, so **cis-but-2-ene gives only cis-1,2-dimethylcyclopropane** and trans gives trans. A triplet carbene first forms one bond, giving a diradical that can rotate before the ring closes, so both isomers form. This difference is used to tell which spin state reacted.

<Eq ce="CH2=CH2 + :CH2 -> C3H6" />

<ExamTip title="Common mistake">
Calling triplet addition "anti". It is non-stereospecific (a mixture), not a clean anti addition. Write: singlet → syn, stereospecific; triplet → non-stereospecific.
</ExamTip>

Dichlorocarbene adds the same way to give 1,1-dichlorocyclopropanes. With pyrrole, :CCl₂ adds to the ring and it expands to 3-chloropyridine (the "abnormal Reimer–Tiemann" or Ciamician–Dennstedt reaction).

## Insertion into σ bonds

Free :CH₂ can push itself into an existing bond. The preference is **X–H (X = O, N) first, then C–H, then C–C**. Methylene is so reactive that it hardly distinguishes primary, secondary and tertiary C–H bonds.

<Eq ce="R-OH + :CH2 -> R-O-CH3" />

<Eq ce="R2N-H + :CH2 -> R2N-CH3" />

<Eq ce="R-H + :CH2 -> R-CH3" />

## Diazomethane and sulphur ylides with carbonyl compounds

These are often grouped with carbene reactions; strictly, they are **nucleophilic additions** of a ylide carbon to C=O, not carbene insertions.

- **Alcohols + CH₂N₂:** methyl ethers form only with a Lewis acid catalyst such as **BF₃**. Without it, diazomethane methylates the more acidic carboxylic acids and phenols.
- **Ketones + CH₂N₂ (Buchner–Curtius–Schlotterbeck reaction):** the diazomethane carbon adds to C=O, N₂ leaves, and either an alkyl group shifts to give the **homologated ketone** R–CO–CH₂–R (major) or the oxygen closes onto the carbon to give an **epoxide**.
- **Ketones + sulphur ylide, CH₂⁻–S⁺R₂ (Corey–Chaykovsky reaction):** the ylide adds to C=O and the oxygen displaces the sulphide, giving the **epoxide** as the main product.

<Eq ce="R2C=O + CH2N2 -> R-CO-CH2-R + N2" />

<Eq ce="R2C=O + CH2=S(CH3)2 -> R2C(O)CH2 + (CH3)2S" />

## Factors that decide carbene behaviour

- **Spin state:** singlet (concerted, stereospecific) vs triplet (stepwise, radical-like).
- **Substituents:** halogens and heteroatoms stabilise the singlet and make the carbene more selective; :CH₂ is the least selective.
- **Metal:** zinc or copper carbenoids react more cleanly than free carbenes and can be directed by nearby OH groups.

## Member reactions

- [Reimer–Tiemann reaction](/reactions/reimer-tiemann-reaction/): :CCl₂ with phenoxide gives salicylaldehyde.
- [Carbylamine reaction](/reactions/carbylamine-reaction/): :CCl₂ with a primary amine gives an isocyanide.
- [Simmons–Smith reaction](/reactions/simmons-smith-reaction/): zinc carbenoid cyclopropanation.
