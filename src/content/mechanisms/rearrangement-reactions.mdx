## What is a rearrangement reaction?

In a **rearrangement**, an atom or group moves from one atom to another within the same molecule, so the carbon skeleton or the position of a functional group changes. Most exam rearrangements are **1,2-shifts**: a hydride, alkyl or aryl group moves with its bonding electrons to a neighbouring **electron-deficient** atom: a carbocation, a nitrenium-like nitrogen or an electron-poor oxygen.

## Common patterns

- **To an electron-deficient carbon:** carbocation rearrangements, pinacol–pinacolone, dienone–phenol, Demjanov ring expansion.
- **To an electron-deficient nitrogen:** Beckmann, Hofmann bromamide, Schmidt, Curtius, Lossen.
- **To an electron-deficient oxygen:** Baeyer–Villiger oxidation.

<KeyPoint title="Migratory aptitude">
The group that best stabilises positive charge migrates first: aryl &gt; H &gt; alkyl (with methyl among the poorest). The migrating group keeps its configuration.
</KeyPoint>
