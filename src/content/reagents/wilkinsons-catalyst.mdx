## What is Wilkinson's catalyst?

**Wilkinson's catalyst** is the rhodium complex **[RhCl(PPh₃)₃]**, chloridotris(triphenylphosphine)rhodium(I), where Ph is a phenyl group. It is a red-brown solid that dissolves in organic solvents and acts as a **homogeneous catalyst** for the **hydrogenation of alkenes** with H₂. Like the metal-surface catalysts, it adds both hydrogens to the same face (**syn addition**), but it is far more **selective**: it reduces only unhindered C=C bonds and leaves most polar groups alone. It is named after Sir Geoffrey Wilkinson (Nobel Prize, 1973), who made its use popular in the 1960s.

## Composition and preparation

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Formula | RhCl(PPh₃)₃, C₅₄H₄₅ClP₃Rh |
| Oxidation state of Rh | +1 (d⁸) |
| Geometry | Slightly distorted square planar |
| Electron count | 16 (coordinatively unsaturated) |
| Appearance | Red-brown solid, insoluble in water |

It is made by refluxing rhodium(III) chloride hydrate with excess triphenylphosphine in ethanol. Three PPh₃ become ligands; a fourth reduces Rh(III) to Rh(I) and is itself oxidised to triphenylphosphine oxide.

<Eq ce="RhCl3(H2O)3 + 4PPh3 -> RhCl(PPh3)3 + OPPh3 + 2HCl + 2H2O" />

## What it reduces

| Substrate | Product with H₂ / RhCl(PPh₃)₃ |
|---|---|
| Terminal alkene, RCH=CH₂ | Alkane, RCH₂CH₃ (fast) |
| Disubstituted alkene | Alkane, syn addition |
| Diene with one unhindered C=C | Only the less hindered C=C reduced |
| Carvone (isopropenyl C=C and ring C=C) | Isopropenyl group reduced; ring enone C=C kept |
| Alkyne | Usually all the way to the alkane (via the cis-alkene); hard to stop |

<Eq ce="R-CH=CH2 + H2 ->[RhCl(PPh3)3][benzene, 25 ^\circ C] R-CH2-CH3" />

## What it does not touch

- **Tri- and tetrasubstituted alkenes**: too crowded to bind to the bulky rhodium centre, so they react very slowly or not at all.
- **C=O, C≡N, NO₂, esters, carboxylic acids**: like Pd/C, Wilkinson's catalyst is good with C=C but leaves polar multiple bonds alone.
- **Benzene rings**: not reduced.

<ExamTip title="Common mistake">
The formula is **RhCl(PPh₃)₃**, not [Rh(PPh₃)₃]Cl₃. Rhodium carries only one chloride and is in the +1 state. Also, do not use Wilkinson's catalyst to reduce a tetrasubstituted alkene in an answer: such a double bond is too hindered and survives.
</ExamTip>

## Mechanism (short)

1. **Ligand loss.** One PPh₃ dissociates, giving a 14-electron RhCl(PPh₃)₂ species with a vacant site.
2. **Oxidative addition of H₂.** H₂ adds to rhodium, giving a Rh(III) dihydride.
3. **Alkene binding.** The alkene coordinates through its π bond.
4. **Migratory insertion.** One hydride moves to an alkene carbon, forming a Rh–alkyl bond. This step is slowed by steric crowding, which explains the selectivity for unhindered alkenes.
5. **Reductive elimination.** The second hydride joins the alkyl group; the alkane leaves and Rh(I) is regenerated.

Because both hydrogens come from the same metal atom, the addition is syn.

<KeyPoint>
Rh(I) → Rh(III) → Rh(I): **oxidative addition, insertion, reductive elimination**. The catalyst is homogeneous, 16-electron, square planar and sensitive to steric hindrance.
</KeyPoint>

## Other reactions

- With aldehydes, a stoichiometric amount of the complex removes the CHO carbon as CO (decarbonylation): RCHO gives RH and RhCl(CO)(PPh₃)₂.
- It also catalyses hydroboration with catecholborane and hydrosilylation of alkenes.

## Comparison with similar catalysts

| Catalyst | Type | Selectivity | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ni, Pd/C, PtO₂ | Heterogeneous | Reduce almost all C=C and C≡C | [Catalytic hydrogenation](/reactions/catalytic-hydrogenation/) |
| Wilkinson's, RhCl(PPh₃)₃ | Homogeneous | Only mono- and disubstituted C=C | Selective reduction of the least hindered C=C |
| Crabtree's catalyst (Ir) | Homogeneous, cationic | Reduces even tri- and tetrasubstituted C=C | Hindered alkenes |
| [Lindlar catalyst](/reagents/lindlar-catalyst/) | Heterogeneous, poisoned Pd | C≡C to cis C=C only | Alkyne to cis-alkene |
| Diimide, HN=NH | Metal-free reagent | Non-polar C=C and C≡C | [Diimide reduction](/reactions/diimide-reduction/) |

## Exam tips

- All hydrogenations (metal surface, Wilkinson's, Lindlar, diimide) are **syn additions**.
- If a question shows two C=C bonds and asks for selective reduction of the **less substituted** one, Wilkinson's catalyst is the answer.
