## What is pyridinium dichromate (PDC)?

**Pyridinium dichromate (PDC)**, also called the **Cornforth reagent**, is the orange pyridinium salt of dichromate, **(C₅H₅NH⁺)₂Cr₂O₇²⁻**. It is a chromium(VI) oxidising agent that converts **secondary alcohols to ketones** and **primary alcohols to aldehydes or carboxylic acids**, depending on the solvent. In **dichloromethane** it stops at the aldehyde; in **DMF** it oxidises saturated primary alcohols to **carboxylic acids**. Cornforth introduced it in 1962, and Corey and Schmidt developed its use for alcohols in 1979.

## Preparation and composition

PDC is made by slowly adding a concentrated aqueous solution of **chromium trioxide** to **pyridine** with ice cooling (the mixing is strongly exothermic). The orange solid is filtered, washed with acetone and dried.

<Eq ce="2C5H5N + 2CrO3 + H2O -> (C5H5NH)2Cr2O7" />

In water, CrO₃ forms dichromic acid, whose two protons are taken up by two pyridine molecules. Key properties:

- stable in air, **not very hygroscopic**, easy to store;
- **nearly neutral** (only slightly acidic from the pyridinium ion), so it is **less acidic than PCC**;
- soluble in water, DMF and DMSO; only sparingly soluble in CH₂Cl₂, where it is used as a suspension.

## What PDC oxidises: the solvent decides

| Substrate | PDC in CH₂Cl₂ | PDC in DMF |
|---|---|---|
| Saturated 1° alcohol, RCH₂OH | aldehyde, RCHO | **carboxylic acid, RCOOH** |
| Allylic or benzylic 1° alcohol | α,β-unsaturated aldehyde / ArCHO | still stops at aldehyde |
| 2° alcohol, R₂CHOH | ketone | ketone |
| 3° alcohol | no oxidation | no oxidation |

<Eq ce="CH3(CH2)6CH2OH ->[PDC][DMF] CH3(CH2)6COOH" />

<Eq ce="C6H5CH2OH ->[PDC][DMF] C6H5CHO" />

<KeyPoint>
PDC is mild in CH₂Cl₂ (like PCC) but strong in DMF. The more DMF in the solvent mixture, the stronger the oxidation. Allylic and benzylic primary alcohols are not taken to acids even in DMF.
</KeyPoint>

Slow oxidations can be sped up with powdered 3 Å molecular sieves, acetic acid or acetic anhydride, at the cost of a less neutral medium and some loss of selectivity.

## What PDC does not touch

- **Tertiary alcohols**: no α-hydrogen, so no carbonyl can form.
- **Acid-sensitive groups** (acetals, silyl ethers, many protecting groups): because PDC is almost neutral, it is the preferred Cr(VI) reagent for such substrates.
- Isolated C=C bonds are not cleaved under the usual room-temperature conditions.

## Mechanism (short)

As with other Cr(VI) oxidants, the alcohol first forms a **chromate ester** with the dichromate. A base then removes the hydrogen on the carbinol carbon while the O–Cr bond breaks, giving the C=O group and reduced chromium. A tertiary alcohol has no such hydrogen, which is why it is not oxidised. In exam answers, the acid formed in DMF is explained as a second oxidation of the aldehyde (through its hydrate) under the stronger conditions.

## Comparison with similar reagents

| Reagent | Formula | Acidity | 1° alcohol gives |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PDC** | (C₅H₅NH⁺)₂Cr₂O₇²⁻ | nearly neutral | aldehyde (CH₂Cl₂) / acid (DMF) |
| [PCC](/reagents/pyridinium-chlorochromate/) | C₅H₅NH⁺CrO₃Cl⁻ | slightly acidic | aldehyde (CH₂Cl₂) |
| Collins reagent | CrO₃·2C₅H₅N in CH₂Cl₂ | neutral to basic | aldehyde |
| [Jones reagent](/reagents/jones-reagent/) | CrO₃ + dil. H₂SO₄ in acetone | strongly acidic | carboxylic acid |
| [K₂Cr₂O₇ + H₂SO₄](/reagents/potassium-dichromate/) | Cr₂O₇²⁻ / H⁺ | strongly acidic | carboxylic acid |

PCC and PDC share most properties: both are stable, commercially available, and convert alcohols to aldehydes and ketones in CH₂Cl₂ at room temperature. The deciding difference is acidity: choose **PDC for acid-sensitive substrates**.

## Exam tips

- Read the solvent. "PDC, CH₂Cl₂" means aldehyde; "PDC, DMF" with a saturated primary alcohol means carboxylic acid.
- PDC and PCC are both pyridinium salts of Cr(VI): PCC has **one Cr and a Cl**, PDC has **two Cr bridged by O**.
- If a question calls PDC a "strong" or "hard" oxidant, it is thinking of DMF conditions; in CH₂Cl₂ it is as selective as PCC.
- For oxidising only allylic or benzylic alcohols, [MnO₂](/reagents/manganese-dioxide/) is more selective still.

All chromium(VI) reagents are toxic and must be handled with care; modern labs often replace them with Swern or Dess–Martin oxidations. Overview: [Oxidation reactions](/mechanisms/oxidation-reactions/).
