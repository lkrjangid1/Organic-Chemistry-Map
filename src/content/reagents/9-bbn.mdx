## What is 9-BBN?

**9-BBN (9-borabicyclo[3.3.1]nonane)** is a **dialkylborane, R₂BH**, in which the boron atom bridges a bicyclic eight-carbon framework and carries a **single B–H bond**. It is a colourless solid used as a **hydroboration reagent**: because it is so bulky, it adds boron almost exclusively to the **less hindered carbon** of an alkene, giving very clean **anti-Markovnikov** products after oxidation. It is also a **mild reducing agent** for carbonyl compounds. In the solid and in solution it exists as a **hydride-bridged dimer**, which splits easily when a reactive substrate is present.

## Preparation and structure

9-BBN is made by **double hydroboration of cycloocta-1,5-diene** with borane (BH₃·THF or B₂H₆) in an ether solvent. The first C=C bond adds a B–H; the second C=C adds intramolecularly to the same boron, which locks the two halves of the ring into the bicyclo[3.3.1] skeleton.

## What it does: hydroboration and reduction

| Substrate | Reagents | Product |
|---|---|---|
| Terminal alkene, RCH=CH₂ | (i) 9-BBN, THF; (ii) H₂O₂, NaOH or KOH | Primary alcohol, RCH₂CH₂OH |
| Unsymmetrical internal alkene | (i) 9-BBN; (ii) H₂O₂, OH⁻ | OH on the less substituted carbon |
| Alkene, then aryl halide + Pd catalyst | 9-BBN, then Suzuki coupling | C–C coupled product |
| Aldehydes, ketones | 9-BBN | Alcohols (mild reduction) |
| Acid chlorides | 9-BBN | Reduced products (mild) |
| Amides | 9-BBN (stoichiometric) | Amines |

<Eq ce="CH3(CH2)3CH=CH2 ->[(i) 9-BBN, THF][(ii) H2O2, NaOH] CH3(CH2)5OH" />

Hex-1-ene gives **hexan-1-ol**. With plain BH₃ a little hexan-2-ol also forms; the steric demand of 9-BBN suppresses this 2-substituted isomer.

## Mechanism (short)

9-BBN reacts **like BH₃**, only with one B–H bond:

1. **Four-centred addition.** The B–H bond adds across the C=C in one concerted step. Boron (the electrophile) bonds to the less hindered carbon while H goes to the other carbon. H and B add to the **same face**: syn addition. No carbocation forms, so there is no rearrangement.
2. **Oxidation.** Hydroperoxide ion (HOO⁻) attacks boron; the alkyl group migrates from B to O with **retention of configuration**.
3. **Hydrolysis.** The B–O bond is cleaved by base, releasing the alcohol.

<KeyPoint>
Boron goes to the **less substituted** carbon, H and B add **syn**, and OH replaces B with **retention**. The bigger the borane, the higher the regioselectivity, and 9-BBN is one of the bulkiest common boranes.
</KeyPoint>

## What it does not touch (or reacts with slowly)

- **Crowded alkenes** react more slowly, because the bulk around boron hinders its approach.
- Since it has only one B–H, it does **not** go on to form trialkylboranes from three alkenes the way BH₃ does.
- It is a much gentler reducing agent than [LiAlH₄](/reagents/lithium-aluminium-hydride/), which is why it is described as mild.

- When a question asks for the **terminal alcohol** from a terminal alkene with the **highest selectivity**, choose 9-BBN followed by H₂O₂/NaOH.
