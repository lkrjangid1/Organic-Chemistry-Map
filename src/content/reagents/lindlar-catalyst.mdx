## What is Lindlar catalyst?

**Lindlar catalyst** is a **poisoned palladium catalyst**: palladium deposited on **calcium carbonate** (or barium sulphate) and deactivated with **lead acetate** and **quinoline**. With H₂ it reduces an **alkyne to a cis-alkene** and stops there, because the weakened catalyst cannot reduce the alkene that forms. The addition is **syn**. It was introduced by Herbert Lindlar in 1952.

<Eq ce="R-C#C-R' + H2 ->[Pd/CaCO3, Pb(OAc)2, quinoline] cis-RCH=CHR'" />

## Composition

| Component | Role |
|---|---|
| Palladium (about 5% by weight) | Active hydrogenation metal |
| CaCO₃ (or BaCO₃, BaSO₄) | Solid support with low surface activity |
| Lead acetate or lead oxide | First poison; deactivates the most active Pd sites |
| Quinoline (or another amine) | Second poison; improves selectivity so no alkane forms |

The catalyst is made by reducing palladium chloride in a slurry of the carbonate and then adding lead acetate. The common Indian textbook variant, **Pd/BaSO₄ with quinoline**, behaves the same way for alkynes; the same poisoned system on BaSO₄ is also used in the [Rosenmund reduction](/reactions/rosenmund-reduction/).

## What it reduces

| Substrate | Product |
|---|---|
| Internal alkyne, R–C≡C–R′ | cis (Z) alkene |
| But-2-yne | cis-But-2-ene |
| 5-Decyne | cis-5-Decene |
| Phenylacetylene, C₆H₅C≡CH | Styrene, C₆H₅CH=CH₂ |
| Enyne (C=C and C≡C in one molecule) | Only the C≡C is reduced |

<Eq ce="CH3-C#C-CH3 + H2 ->[Lindlar] cis-CH3CH=CHCH3" />

## What it does not touch

- **Alkenes**: an isolated C=C, or the C=C formed from the alkyne, is not hydrogenated. This is the whole point of the poison.
- **C=O and other polar groups**: not reduced under these mild conditions.
- **Benzene rings**: untouched.

<KeyPoint>
Lindlar catalyst works **only on alkynes**. It gives a **cis-alkene** by **syn addition**, and it cannot reduce an alkene.
</KeyPoint>

## Mechanism (short)

The alkyne and H₂ are both adsorbed on the palladium surface, exactly as in ordinary [catalytic hydrogenation](/reactions/catalytic-hydrogenation/). Two surface hydrogens are delivered to the same face of the triple bond, which gives the cis geometry. The lead and quinoline leave just enough activity for the more reactive triple bond, so the alkene that forms is released without taking up a second H₂.

## P-2 catalyst (nickel boride)

**P-2 nickel** is a nickel boride catalyst made in situ by reducing nickel(II) acetate with **sodium borohydride in ethanol**. It is very sensitive to steric hindrance and is used for **partial reduction of alkynes to alkenes**; with added ethylenediamine it gives very pure **cis-alkenes**. In exam questions "Lindlar or P-2 catalyst" means the same result: alkyne to cis-alkene.

<ExamTip title="Common mistake">
The metal in Lindlar catalyst is **palladium**, not nickel. Nickel appears only in the separate P-2 catalyst (Ni₂B). Also remember that ordinary Pd/C, Pt or Ni without a poison takes an alkyne all the way to the alkane.
</ExamTip>

## Comparison with similar reagents

| Reagent | Alkyne gives | Mechanism | Stereochemistry |
|---|---|---|---|
| H₂, Pd/C or Pt or Ni | Alkane | Surface addition | Not relevant |
| H₂, Lindlar catalyst | Alkene | Surface syn addition | cis (Z) |
| H₂, P-2 nickel boride | Alkene | Surface syn addition | cis (Z) |
| Na or Li in liquid NH₃ | Alkene | Electron transfer, then protonation | trans (E) |
| Diimide, HN=NH | Mostly alkane (hard to stop) | Concerted syn transfer | cis if stopped |

The Na/liquid NH₃ route is described on the [Birch reduction](/reactions/birch-reduction/) page. The two methods are complementary: choose Lindlar for cis and sodium in ammonia for trans.

## Exam tips

- "Alkyne → cis-alkene" = H₂/Lindlar (or P-2). "Alkyne → trans-alkene" = Na/liq. NH₃.
- A terminal alkyne with Lindlar gives the terminal alkene (no cis/trans isomerism).
- If one mole of H₂ is taken up by an enyne over Lindlar catalyst, only the triple bond has reacted.
- For other reducing reagents, see the [reduction reactions hub](/mechanisms/reduction-reactions/).
