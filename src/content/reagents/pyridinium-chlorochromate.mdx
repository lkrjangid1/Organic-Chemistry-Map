## What is pyridinium chlorochromate (PCC)?

**Pyridinium chlorochromate (PCC)**, also called the **Corey–Suggs reagent**, is the orange-yellow salt **C₅H₅NH⁺ CrO₃Cl⁻**, made from pyridine, chromium trioxide and hydrochloric acid. It is a **mild chromium(VI) oxidising agent** that converts **primary alcohols to aldehydes** and **secondary alcohols to ketones**. Because it is used in dry dichloromethane, the aldehyde is not over-oxidised to a carboxylic acid. NCERT calls it a better reagent than plain CrO₃ for getting aldehydes from primary alcohols in good yield.

## Preparation and composition

Pyridine is added to a cold solution of CrO₃ (chromic anhydride) in concentrated HCl, and the salt crystallises out. Adding a cold solution of pyridine in HCl to solid CrO₃ instead cuts down the toxic chromyl chloride (CrO₂Cl₂) fumes.

<Eq ce="C5H5N + HCl + CrO3 -> C5H5NH+ CrO3Cl-" />

Two things happen in this step:

1. The lone pair on the pyridine nitrogen picks up H⁺ from HCl, giving the **pyridinium ion** C₅H₅NH⁺.
2. Chloride adds to the chromium of CrO₃, giving the tetrahedral **chlorochromate ion** [CrO₃Cl]⁻. Chromium stays in the +6 state.

The reagent is stable, not very hygroscopic, sold commercially and can be stored.

## What PCC oxidises

| Substrate | Product with PCC (CH₂Cl₂, room temperature) |
|---|---|
| 1° alcohol, RCH₂OH | aldehyde, RCHO (stops here) |
| 2° alcohol, R₂CHOH | ketone, R₂C=O |
| Allylic alcohol, e.g. CH₂=CH–CH₂OH | α,β-unsaturated aldehyde (acrolein); C=C kept |
| Benzyl alcohol, C₆H₅CH₂OH | benzaldehyde |
| 3° alcohol, R₃COH | no oxidation |

<Eq ce="CH3CH2CH2OH ->[PCC][CH2Cl2] CH3CH2CHO" />

<Eq ce="(CH3)2CHOH ->[PCC][CH2Cl2] (CH3)2C=O" />

Overall, two chlorochromate ions oxidise three alcohol molecules, and chromium ends up as Cr₂O₃ (Cr(III)):

<Eq ce="2C5H5NH+ CrO3Cl- + 3R2CHOH -> 2C5H5NH+ Cl- + Cr2O3 + 3R2C=O + 3H2O" />

## What PCC does not touch

<KeyPoint>
PCC is a mild oxidant (मध्यम ऑक्सीकारक). It does not oxidise isolated alkenes or alkynes, does not take an aldehyde on to the acid in dry CH₂Cl₂, and leaves ketones, esters and 3° alcohols alone.
</KeyPoint>

- **Carboxylic acid formation needs water.** An aldehyde is over-oxidised only through its hydrate, RCH(OH)₂. In anhydrous CH₂Cl₂ no hydrate forms. In DMF, however, PCC can over-oxidise primary alcohols to acids.
- **Acidity.** PCC is slightly acidic (more acidic than PDC). Acid-sensitive substrates are oxidised with sodium acetate added as a buffer.
- Tar-like chromium by-products are absorbed onto added Celite or molecular sieves and filtered off.

## Mechanism (short)

1. The alcohol oxygen attacks chromium and a **chromate ester**, R₂CH–O–CrO₂X, forms.
2. A base (pyridine or chloride) removes the **α-hydrogen** on the carbinol carbon while the O–Cr bond breaks. The C=O forms and chromium drops from Cr(VI) to Cr(IV).
3. Cr(IV) species react further and end up as Cr(III).

Breaking the α-C–H bond is the slow step (deuterated alcohols react several times more slowly). A tertiary alcohol has no α-H, so it can form a chromate ester but cannot give a carbonyl compound.

## Comparison with Collins, Sarett and other Cr(VI) reagents

| Reagent | Composition | Solvent | 1° alcohol gives | Remarks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **PCC** | C₅H₅NH⁺ CrO₃Cl⁻ (pyridine + CrO₃ + HCl) | CH₂Cl₂ | aldehyde | mildly acidic, stable solid |
| **Collins reagent** | CrO₃·2C₅H₅N (pyridine + CrO₃) | CH₂Cl₂ | aldehyde | no water, suits acid-sensitive substrates; used in large excess |
| **Sarett reagent** | CrO₃·2C₅H₅N (pyridine + CrO₃) | pyridine | aldehyde (less efficient), ketones well | basic medium; hygroscopic, dangerous to prepare |
| [PDC](/reagents/pyridinium-dichromate/) | (C₅H₅NH⁺)₂Cr₂O₇²⁻ | CH₂Cl₂ / DMF | aldehyde / acid | less acidic than PCC |
| [Jones reagent](/reagents/jones-reagent/) | CrO₃ + dil. H₂SO₄ | acetone (aqueous) | carboxylic acid | water present, so over-oxidises |
| [K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄](/reagents/potassium-dichromate/) | Cr₂O₇²⁻ + H⁺ | water | acid (aldehyde only if distilled out) | orange to green colour change |

Collins and Sarett reagents contain the **same active complex**, CrO₃·2py; they differ only in solvent. Collins reagent is easier and safer to prepare (CrO₃ added to pyridine in CH₂Cl₂), less basic, and less hygroscopic. All three of PCC, Collins and Sarett work without water, which is why they stop at the aldehyde.

<ExamTip title="Common mistake">
Writing Sarett reagent as "pyridine + CrO₃ + CH₂Cl₂ + HCl". Adding HCl to pyridine and CrO₃ gives PCC, a different compound. Sarett reagent is CrO₃·2py dissolved in pyridine; Collins reagent is the same complex in CH₂Cl₂. Neither contains HCl.
</ExamTip>

## Exam tips

- "Primary alcohol to aldehyde, no further" points to **PCC** (or PDC/Collins in CH₂Cl₂). "Primary alcohol to acid" points to KMnO₄, Jones reagent or acidified dichromate.
- PCC keeps a C=C intact: CH₂=CH–CH₂OH gives CH₂=CH–CHO, while hot KMnO₄ would also attack the double bond.
- If a question gives a 3° alcohol with PCC, the answer is usually "no reaction".
- For allylic and benzylic alcohols only, [MnO₂](/reagents/manganese-dioxide/) is the more selective choice; PCC oxidises all 1° and 2° alcohols.
- For an alcohol-only oxidation without chromium, compare the [Oppenauer oxidation](/reactions/oppenauer-oxidation/).
