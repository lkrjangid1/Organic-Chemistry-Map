## What is DIBAL-H?

**DIBAL-H (diisobutylaluminium hydride)**, (i-Bu)₂AlH, is an organoaluminium reducing agent with **one Al–H bond** and two bulky isobutyl groups on aluminium. Its key use is **partial reduction**: it converts **esters into aldehydes** at −78 °C and **nitriles into aldehydes** after aqueous hydrolysis, stopping one step short of the alcohol or amine that [LiAlH₄](/reagents/lithium-aluminium-hydride/) would give.

Structure: (CH₃)₂CH–CH₂–Al(H)–CH₂–CH(CH₃)₂. In the pure state it exists as dimers and trimers held together by Al–H–Al bridges.

## Preparation and handling

DIBAL-H is made by heating triisobutylaluminium, which loses isobutene by β-hydride elimination:

<Eq ce="(i-Bu)3Al ->[\Delta] (i-Bu)2AlH + CH2=C(CH3)2" />

It is sold as a solution in toluene or hexane (it also dissolves in THF and ether). Like other alkylaluminium compounds it **catches fire in air** and reacts violently with water, so it is handled under dry nitrogen or argon.

## What DIBAL-H reduces

| Substrate | Conditions | Product |
|---|---|---|
| Ester, R–COOR′ | 1 equiv, −78 °C, toluene; then H₃O⁺ | Aldehyde, R–CHO (+ R′OH) |
| Nitrile, R–C≡N | 1 equiv, low temperature; then H₃O⁺ | Aldehyde, R–CHO |
| Lactone (cyclic ester) | Controlled amount | Lactol (cyclic hemiacetal) |
| α,β-Unsaturated ester | Full reduction | Allylic alcohol (C=C kept) |
| Ester | Excess, higher temperature | 1° alcohol, R–CH₂OH |
| Aldehyde, ketone | Any | Alcohol |

<Eq ce="CH3(CH2)10COOCH3 ->[1. DIBAL-H, toluene, -78 ^\circ C][2. H3O+] CH3(CH2)10CHO + CH3OH" />

<Eq ce="C6H5C#N ->[1. DIBAL-H][2. H3O+] C6H5CHO" />

<KeyPoint>
DIBAL-H is the standard answer for **ester → aldehyde** and **nitrile → aldehyde** in one step. Temperature control is essential: the ester-to-aldehyde reduction is known for giving large amounts of alcohol if the mixture warms up or excess reagent is used.
</KeyPoint>

## What it does not do (under the usual conditions)

- At −78 °C with one equivalent, it does **not** reduce the aldehyde it has just formed.
- It does **not** reduce isolated C=C bonds; α,β-unsaturated esters keep their C=C and become allylic alcohols.
- Nitriles do **not** go on to amines, because only one hydride is delivered.

## Mechanism (short)

DIBAL-H is an **electrophilic** reducing agent, unlike the nucleophilic [AlH₄]⁻ of LAH. Aluminium in (i-Bu)₂AlH has only six valence electrons, so it is a Lewis acid.

1. **Coordination.** The carbonyl oxygen of the ester (or the nitrogen of the nitrile) donates a lone pair to aluminium.
2. **Hydride transfer.** The single Al–H hydride moves to the carbonyl (or nitrile) carbon.
3. **Ester case.** At −78 °C the reagent is not reactive enough to attack the aldehyde, so after work-up the aldehyde is isolated.
4. **Nitrile case.** One hydride gives an imine anion bound to aluminium. Because DIBAL-H has no second hydride, no further addition occurs, and acidic aqueous work-up hydrolyses the imine (R–CH=NH) to the aldehyde.

<Eq ce="R-C#N ->[(i-Bu)2AlH] R-CH=N-Al(i-Bu)2 ->[H3O+] R-CHO + NH4+" />

## Exam tips

- "Ester to aldehyde in one step" → DIBAL-H at −78 °C. LiAlH₄ cannot do it; it always gives the alcohol.
- "Nitrile to aldehyde" has two classic answers: DIBAL-H then H₃O⁺, or the Stephen reduction. Both pass through an imine that is hydrolysed.
- DIBAL-H also counts as a route in the [preparation of aldehydes and ketones](/preparation/preparation-of-aldehydes-and-ketones/).
- Remember the structure: two isobutyl groups and **one** H on Al. "One H" is why it is a partial reducing agent.
