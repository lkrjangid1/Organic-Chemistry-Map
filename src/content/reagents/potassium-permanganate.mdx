## What is potassium permanganate?

**Potassium permanganate (KMnO₄)** is a deep purple, strong oxidising agent in which manganese is in the +7 state. It oxidises in **acidic, neutral and alkaline** media, but the manganese product and the strength of oxidation change with the medium. In organic chemistry, **cold dilute KMnO₄ (Baeyer's reagent)** turns alkenes into **syn 1,2-diols**, while **hot or acidified KMnO₄** cleaves C=C and C≡C bonds and oxidises alkyl side chains of benzene to **benzoic acid**.

## Behaviour in different media

The permanganate ion, MnO₄⁻, is the active species. How many electrons it accepts depends on the pH:

| Medium | Half-reaction | Mn product | Colour seen |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acidic (dil. H₂SO₄) | MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O | Mn²⁺ (+2) | Purple fades to almost colourless |
| Neutral or weakly alkaline | MnO₄⁻ + 2H₂O + 3e⁻ → MnO₂ + 4OH⁻ | MnO₂ (+4) | Brown-black precipitate |
| Strongly alkaline (pH about 14) | MnO₄⁻ + e⁻ → MnO₄²⁻ | Manganate (+6) | Green solution |

<Eq ce="MnO4- + 8H+ + 5e- -> Mn^2+ + 4H2O" />

<KeyPoint>
MnO₄⁻ is **permanganate** (Mn +7, purple); MnO₄²⁻ is **manganate** (Mn +6, green). In Baeyer's test the purple colour disappears and a brown MnO₂ precipitate forms.
</KeyPoint>

## What it oxidises

| Substrate | Conditions | Product |
|---|---|---|
| Alkene R₂C=CR₂ | Cold, dilute, alkaline (Baeyer's reagent) | Vicinal diol, syn addition |
| Alkene R–CH=CR′₂ | Hot, acidic or concentrated | R–COOH + R′₂C=O (C=C cleaved) |
| Terminal alkene R–CH=CH₂ | Hot, acidic | R–COOH + CO₂ |
| Alkyne R–C≡C–R′ | Hot, vigorous | R–COOH + R′–COOH |
| Alkylbenzene (benzylic C–H present) | Hot, acidic or alkaline | Benzoic acid, whatever the chain length |
| Tertiary C–H, e.g. (CH₃)₃CH | KMnO₄ | Tertiary alcohol, (CH₃)₃COH |
| 1° alcohol R–CH₂OH | Acidic, neutral or alkaline | R–COOH (the aldehyde does not survive) |
| 2° alcohol R₂CH–OH | KMnO₄ | Ketone |
| Aldehyde R–CHO | KMnO₄ | R–COOH |
| Ketone | Hot, vigorous | Shorter carboxylic acids (Popoff's rule) |

**Syn dihydroxylation.** 2,3-Dimethylbut-2-ene gives 2,3-dimethylbutane-2,3-diol with Baeyer's reagent. Both OH groups are delivered to the same face of the double bond.

<Eq ce="(CH3)2C=C(CH3)2 ->[cold dil. alk. KMnO4] (CH3)2C(OH)-C(OH)(CH3)2" />

**Oxidative cleavage.** NCERT's example: but-2-ene with acidified KMnO₄ gives two molecules of ethanoic acid.

<Eq ce="CH3-CH=CH-CH3 ->[KMnO4/H+] 2CH3COOH" />

**Side-chain oxidation.** Propylbenzene loses two carbons and the benzylic carbon becomes COOH:

<Eq ce="C6H5CH2CH2CH3 ->[KMnO4, heat] C6H5COOH" />

**Popoff's rule for ketones.** Ketones resist mild oxidants, but hot KMnO₄ slowly breaks the C–C bond next to C=O. In an unsymmetrical ketone the carbonyl group stays with the **smaller** alkyl group. In butan-2-one the bond on the ethyl side breaks: CH₃CO– becomes acetic acid, and the CH₂ of the ethyl group is oxidised to a second acetic acid.

<Eq ce="CH3COCH2CH3 ->[KMnO4, heat] 2CH3COOH" />

<Trick>
**"कम C की तरफ double bond जाएगा"**: the C=O stays on the side with fewer carbons, which is Popoff's rule in one line.
</Trick>

## What it does not touch

- **Benzylic carbons with no hydrogen.** tert-Butylbenzene, C₆H₅C(CH₃)₃, is not oxidised.
- **The benzene ring itself** (phenol is an exception; it is oxidised to p-benzoquinone).
- **Ordinary alkane C–H bonds** that are neither tertiary nor benzylic.
- **Tertiary alcohols** under normal conditions, because there is no H on the carbinol carbon.

## Mechanism (short)

With cold, dilute alkaline KMnO₄, permanganate adds to the alkene in one step to form a **cyclic manganate ester**. Both C–O bonds form from the same side, which is why the addition is syn. Hydrolysis of this ester releases the cis-diol. With heat or more concentrated reagent, the diol is oxidised further and its C–C bond breaks, so the reaction runs on to ketones and acids.

<ExamTip title="Common mistake">
Do not stop side-chain oxidation at an aldehyde or keep part of the chain. With KMnO₄, ethylbenzene, propylbenzene and isopropylbenzene all give **benzoic acid**. Only a side chain with no benzylic H (tert-butyl) survives.
</ExamTip>

## Comparison with similar reagents

| Reagent | Strength | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Cold dil. alk. KMnO₄ | Mild | Syn diol; unsaturation test |
| Hot / acidified KMnO₄ | Very strong | C=C cleavage, side-chain oxidation, 1° alcohol to acid |
| [K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄](/reagents/potassium-dichromate/) | Strong | 1° alcohol to aldehyde or acid, 2° to ketone |
| [Jones reagent](/reagents/jones-reagent/) | Strong | 1° alcohol to acid, 2° to ketone; leaves C=C alone |
| [MnO₂](/reagents/manganese-dioxide/) | Mild, selective | Allylic and benzylic alcohols to carbonyls |
| [PCC](/reagents/pyridinium-chlorochromate/) | Mild | 1° alcohol stops at aldehyde |

For an **anti** diol, use an epoxide route instead: [Prilezhaev epoxidation](/reactions/prilezhaev-epoxidation/) followed by acid hydrolysis. For cleaving C=C to aldehydes without over-oxidation, use [ozonolysis](/reactions/ozonolysis/) with a Zn/H₂O work-up.

## Exam tips

- Decolourisation of Baeyer's reagent and of [bromine water](/tests/bromine-water-test/) both indicate unsaturation; aldehydes and formic acid also decolourise KMnO₄, so the test is not specific.
- Counting carbons in side-chain oxidation: the ring keeps exactly one carbon per side chain. A xylene gives a benzenedicarboxylic acid.
- A terminal =CH₂ group ends up as CO₂ with hot KMnO₄, while a =CR₂ end becomes a ketone.
- See the [oxidation reactions](/mechanisms/oxidation-reactions/) overview for how KMnO₄ compares with ozone, peracids and chromium reagents.
