## What is potassium dichromate?

**Potassium dichromate (K₂Cr₂O₇)** is a bright orange-red crystalline salt in which chromium is in the **+6** state. Dissolved in **dilute sulphuric acid** it forms **chromic acid (H₂CrO₄)**, a strong oxidising agent that converts **primary alcohols into aldehydes and then carboxylic acids**, **secondary alcohols into ketones**, and aldehydes into acids. During the oxidation the **orange** Cr₂O₇²⁻ is reduced to **green Cr³⁺**, a colour change used as a quick test for oxidisable groups.

## Composition and the dichromate–chromate equilibrium

In acid, the reduction half-reaction is a six-electron change:

<Eq ce="Cr2O7^2- + 14H+ + 6e- -> 2Cr^3+ + 7H2O" />

The orange dichromate ion and the yellow chromate ion are interconverted by changing the pH. Chromium stays at +6 in both:

<Eq ce="Cr2O7^2- + 2OH- <=> 2CrO4^2- + H2O" />

The forward direction (alkali added) turns orange to yellow; adding acid turns it back.

| Species | Name | Cr oxidation state | Colour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cr₂O₇²⁻ | Dichromate | +6 | Orange |
| CrO₄²⁻ | Chromate | +6 | Yellow |
| Cr³⁺ (aq) | Chromium(III) | +3 | Green |

**Chromic acid.** Adding sulphuric acid to a dichromate (or dissolving CrO₃ in water) gives the oxidising mixture called chromic acid. Formally, CrO₃ + H₂O → H₂CrO₄; pure molecular H₂CrO₄ cannot be isolated, but the name is used for the solution. CrO₃ in aqueous H₂SO₄ and acetone is [Jones reagent](/reagents/jones-reagent/).

<KeyPoint>
For organic oxidations dichromate is always used **in acid** (K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄ or Na₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄). In alkali it simply changes into yellow chromate and is not used as an oxidant.
</KeyPoint>

## What it oxidises

| Substrate | Conditions | Product |
|---|---|---|
| 1° alcohol R–CH₂OH | Distil the product as it forms | Aldehyde R–CHO |
| 1° alcohol R–CH₂OH | Excess oxidant, reflux | Carboxylic acid R–COOH |
| 2° alcohol R₂CH–OH | K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄ | Ketone R₂C=O |
| Aldehyde R–CHO | K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄ | R–COOH |
| Alkene | Acidified, heat | Ketones and/or acids (C=C cleaved) |
| Alkylbenzene | Chromic acid, vigorous | Benzoic acid |

**Ethanol to ethanal and ethanoic acid:**

<Eq ce="3CH3CH2OH + Cr2O7^2- + 8H+ -> 3CH3CHO + 2Cr^3+ + 7H2O" />

<Eq ce="CH3CH2OH ->[K2Cr2O7/H2SO4][reflux] CH3COOH" />

**Propan-2-ol to propanone:**

<Eq ce="(CH3)2CHOH ->[K2Cr2O7/H2SO4] (CH3)2C=O" />

## What it does not touch

- **Tertiary alcohols** such as 2-methylpropan-2-ol: no H on the carbinol carbon, so no oxidation (the solution stays orange).
- **Ketones** under ordinary conditions; only vigorous treatment cleaves them.
- **The benzene ring.**

## Mechanism (short)

1. **Chromate ester.** The alcohol reacts reversibly with chromic acid to form an alkyl chromate ester, R₂CH–O–CrO₃H.
2. **Rate-determining step.** Water acts as a base and removes the H on the carbinol carbon; the electrons form C=O and chromium is reduced from +6 to +4. Replacing that H by deuterium slows the reaction about sevenfold, which shows this C–H bond breaks in the slow step.
3. **Chromium chemistry.** Cr(IV) and Cr(V) species react further and end up as green Cr(III).

A primary alcohol gives an aldehyde first. In water the aldehyde forms a hydrate, R–CH(OH)₂, which is itself an alcohol and is oxidised again to R–COOH. This is why aldehydes are hard to isolate unless they are distilled out.

## Orange to green: the dichromate test

| Compound tested | Observation | Inference |
|---|---|---|
| 1° alcohol | Orange → green | Oxidised (aldehyde/acid) |
| 2° alcohol | Orange → green | Oxidised (ketone) |
| 3° alcohol | Stays orange | Not oxidised |
| Aldehyde | Orange → green | Oxidised to acid |
| Ketone | Stays orange | Not oxidised |

The same colour change was the basis of early breath-alcohol analysers. Together with the [Lucas test](/tests/lucas-test/), it separates 1°, 2° and 3° alcohols.

<ExamTip title="Common mistake">
The green colour is **Cr³⁺**, not chromate. Chromate (CrO₄²⁻) is **yellow** and still contains Cr(+6); it appears when alkali is added, not when an alcohol is oxidised.
</ExamTip>

## Exam tips

- "Orange → green" in a question means Cr(VI) → Cr(III): the compound is a 1°/2° alcohol or an aldehyde.
- To stop at the aldehyde with dichromate, mention **distilling the aldehyde out** as it forms; otherwise write the acid.
- Chromium(VI) compounds are toxic and carcinogenic, which is why milder, anhydrous reagents such as PCC are preferred today.
