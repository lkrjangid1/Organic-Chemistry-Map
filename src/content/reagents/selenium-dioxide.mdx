## What is selenium dioxide?

**Selenium dioxide (SeO₂)** is a colourless (white) solid oxidising agent, with selenium in the +4 state, that attacks **C–H bonds activated by a neighbouring π bond**. With **alkenes** it puts an **OH group on the allylic carbon** (allylic oxidation). With **aldehydes and ketones** it converts the **α-CH₂ (or α-CH₃) group into C=O**, giving a **1,2-dicarbonyl compound**; this is the **Riley oxidation**, first reported by H. L. Riley in 1932. Selenium is reduced to red elemental selenium.

## Preparation and composition

SeO₂ is made by oxidising selenium, either by burning it in air or with nitric acid or hydrogen peroxide; dehydrating selenous acid (H₂SeO₃) also gives it. It dissolves in water to form selenous acid and is soluble in alcohols. Selenium compounds are **highly toxic**, so the reagent is handled with care, and the red selenium formed is filtered off after the reaction. For exams, simply write SeO₂ over the arrow.

## What it oxidises

| Substrate | Site attacked | Product |
|---|---|---|
| Cyclohexene | Allylic CH₂ | Cyclohex-2-en-1-ol |
| R₂C=CR′–CHR″₂ | Allylic C–H | R₂C=CR′–C(OH)R″₂ (allylic alcohol) |
| Cyclohexanone | α-CH₂ | Cyclohexane-1,2-dione |
| Acetaldehyde (as paraldehyde) | α-CH₃ | Glyoxal, OHC–CHO |
| Ketone with two different α-CH₂ groups | Less hindered α-carbon | 1,2-Dione |

**Allylic oxidation of cyclohexene:**

<Eq ce="C6H10 ->[SeO2] C6H9OH" />

Here C₆H₁₀ is cyclohexene and C₆H₉OH is cyclohex-2-en-1-ol: the OH sits on the carbon next to the double bond, and the double bond does not move in this symmetric case.

**Riley oxidation of cyclohexanone:**

<Eq ce="C6H10O + SeO2 -> C6H8O2 + Se + H2O" />

The ring CH₂ next to C=O becomes a second C=O, so cyclohexanone gives cyclohexane-1,2-dione.

**Glyoxal from acetaldehyde:**

<Eq ce="CH3-CHO + SeO2 -> OHC-CHO + Se + H2O" />

<KeyPoint>
SeO₂ attacks the carbon **next to** a C=C or C=O. Alkenes give **allylic alcohols**; carbonyl compounds give **1,2-dicarbonyl compounds** (α-diketones or α-keto aldehydes). Red selenium is the by-product.
</KeyPoint>

## What it does not touch

- **The C=C bond itself.** It is not cleaved or hydroxylated; the double bond survives (it may shift position in unsymmetrical alkenes, see the mechanism).
- **Positions that are not activated.** Ordinary CH₂ groups far from a π bond are unaffected.
- **Bridgehead allylic positions** in bridged bicyclic alkenes, because a double bond cannot form at a bridgehead (Bredt's rule).

## Mechanism (short)

**Alkenes (allylic oxidation).**
1. **Ene reaction.** SeO₂ reacts with the alkene: the Se=O adds to one alkene carbon while an allylic hydrogen moves to oxygen. The double bond shifts by one position, giving an allylseleninic acid.
2. **[2,3]-Sigmatropic rearrangement.** The C–Se bond and an Se–O bond reorganise through a five-membered cyclic transition state, which moves the double bond back to its original position and puts oxygen on the allylic carbon.
3. **Hydrolysis.** The resulting selenium ester is hydrolysed to the allylic alcohol.

Because both steps are pericyclic, see [pericyclic reactions](/mechanisms/pericyclic-reactions/) for the ene and sigmatropic ideas.

**Carbonyl compounds (Riley).** The enol form of the ketone attacks electrophilic selenium. After rearrangement and loss of water, water attacks the α-carbon, and red amorphous selenium is released as the 1,2-dicarbonyl product forms.

## Regioselectivity

- In **trisubstituted alkenes**, oxidation takes place at the **more substituted end** of the double bond, with the order of reactivity **CH₂ greater than CH₃ greater than CH**.
- **Terminal alkenes** tend to give primary allylic alcohols, because the double bond moves during the ene step.
- In **cyclic alkenes**, a ring allylic position is preferred over a side-chain position.
- For **ketones** with two α-CH₂ groups, the less hindered side reacts.

## Comparison with similar reagents

| Reagent | What it does at the allylic position |
|---|---|
| SeO₂ | C–H → C–OH (allylic alcohol); α-CH₂ of ketone → C=O |
| [NBS, light or peroxide](/reactions/nbs-allylic-bromination/) | C–H → C–Br (allylic bromide) |
| [MnO₂](/reagents/manganese-dioxide/) | Existing allylic C–OH → C=O |
| [KMnO₄](/reagents/potassium-permanganate/) | Attacks the C=C itself (diol or cleavage) |

<ExamTip>
If a question shows an alkene turning into an alkene that has gained an OH on the neighbouring carbon, the reagent is SeO₂. If a ketone gains a second C=O on the adjacent carbon (cyclohexanone → cyclohexane-1,2-dione), the reagent is again SeO₂.
</ExamTip>

## Exam tips

- Count carbons: SeO₂ never adds or removes carbon atoms; only the oxidation level of one carbon changes.
- Look for the **activated** carbon: allylic (next to C=C) or α to C=O.
- Remember the by-product: red **selenium** metal, which separates out and is filtered off.
