## What is Jones reagent?

**Jones reagent** is a solution of **chromium trioxide (CrO₃, chromic anhydride)** in **dilute aqueous sulphuric acid**, which is added to a solution of the substrate in **acetone**. In this mixture CrO₃ behaves as **chromic acid (H₂CrO₄)**, a strong Cr(VI) oxidant. It converts **primary alcohols into carboxylic acids** and **secondary alcohols into ketones**, quickly and in high yield, while usually leaving C=C bonds alone. The method is called the **Jones oxidation**, after Sir Ewart Jones, whose group introduced it in the 1940s.

## Preparation and composition

| Component | Role |
|---|---|
| CrO₃ (chromic anhydride) | Source of Cr(VI); with water it gives chromic acid |
| Dilute H₂SO₄ | Provides acid, which speeds up chromate-ester formation |
| Acetone | Solvent for the alcohol; it is not oxidised under these conditions |

<Eq ce="CrO3 + H2O -> H2CrO4" />

The orange-red reagent is added dropwise to the alcohol in acetone, often with cooling, because the reaction is fast and exothermic. Sodium or potassium dichromate can replace CrO₃; the active oxidant is the same chromic acid. NCERT lists K₂Cr₂O₇ and CrO₃ in acidic media (Jones reagent) among the common reagents for turning primary alcohols into carboxylic acids.

## What it oxidises

| Substrate | Product |
|---|---|
| 1° alcohol R–CH₂OH | Carboxylic acid R–COOH |
| 2° alcohol R₂CH–OH | Ketone R₂C=O |
| 1° allylic or benzylic alcohol | Aldehyde (stops, little hydrate forms) |
| Aldehyde R–CHO (in water) | R–COOH |

<Eq ce="CH3CH2CH2OH ->[CrO3, H2SO4][acetone] CH3CH2COOH" />

<Eq ce="C6H11OH ->[Jones reagent] C6H10O" />

The second example is cyclohexanol going to cyclohexanone. The balanced equation for a secondary alcohol shows that two CrO₃ oxidise three alcohol molecules:

<Eq ce="3R2CHOH + 2CrO3 + 3H2SO4 -> 3R2C=O + Cr2(SO4)3 + 6H2O" />

<KeyPoint>
Jones reagent = CrO₃ + dil. H₂SO₄ + acetone. **1° alcohol → acid, 2° alcohol → ketone, 3° alcohol → no reaction.** Orange Cr(VI) turns green Cr(III).
</KeyPoint>

## What it does not touch

- **Tertiary alcohols.** They form a chromate ester (tert-butyl chromate can even be isolated), but with no H on the carbinol carbon the ester cannot break down to a carbonyl compound.
- **Ketones** and the acetone solvent.
- **C=C double bonds**, in most cases. An allylic secondary alcohol gives an enone with the double bond intact.

<Eq ce="CH2=CH-CH(OH)-CH3 ->[Jones reagent] CH2=CH-CO-CH3" />

## Mechanism (short)

1. **Chromate ester.** The alcohol attacks chromic acid (acid-catalysed) to form a mixed chromate ester, R₂CH–O–CrO₃H.
2. **C–H cleavage (rate-determining).** Water acts as a base and removes the H on the carbinol carbon; the C=O forms and Cr(VI) is reduced to Cr(IV). A large kinetic isotope effect shows this C–H bond breaks in the slow step.
3. **Over-oxidation of 1° alcohols.** The aldehyde formed first adds water to give a hydrate, R–CH(OH)₂. This hydrate is again an alcohol, forms a new chromate ester, and is oxidised to R–COOH.
4. **Chromium end product.** Cr(IV) and Cr(V) intermediates end up as green Cr(III). Excess propan-2-ol is added at the end to destroy leftover Cr(VI) and Cr(V).

Allylic and benzylic aldehydes hydrate only slightly, which is why they survive.

## Comparison with similar reagents

| Reagent | Medium | 1° alcohol gives | 2° alcohol gives |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jones (CrO₃/H₂SO₄/acetone) | Aqueous acid | Carboxylic acid | Ketone |
| [K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄](/reagents/potassium-dichromate/) | Aqueous acid | Aldehyde (distilled) or acid | Ketone |
| [PCC](/reagents/pyridinium-chlorochromate/) | Anhydrous CH₂Cl₂ | Aldehyde | Ketone |
| [PDC](/reagents/pyridinium-dichromate/) | Anhydrous | Aldehyde | Ketone |
| [KMnO₄](/reagents/potassium-permanganate/) | Acidic or alkaline | Carboxylic acid; C=C also attacked | Ketone |
| [MnO₂](/reagents/manganese-dioxide/) | Neutral suspension | Only allylic/benzylic → aldehyde | Only allylic/benzylic → ketone |

The key idea is **water**: in aqueous Jones conditions the aldehyde hydrates and is over-oxidised; in anhydrous PCC or Collins conditions no hydrate forms, so the aldehyde survives.

<ExamTip>
If a question asks for "1° alcohol to carboxylic acid without touching a C=C", Jones reagent is the answer. If it asks for "1° alcohol to aldehyde", choose PCC; with Jones the answer would be the acid (except for allylic and benzylic alcohols).
</ExamTip>

## Exam tips

- Do not confuse **chromic anhydride (CrO₃)** with **chromyl chloride (CrO₂Cl₂)**: the latter is the [Étard reagent](/reactions/etard-reaction/) for toluene → benzaldehyde.
- Side products: esters can form between the acid and unreacted alcohol, a known drawback of the method.
- Cr(VI) compounds are toxic and carcinogenic; this is why anhydrous, milder Cr reagents and chromium-free methods have largely replaced Jones oxidation.
- For the other route between alcohols and ketones, see [Oppenauer oxidation](/reactions/oppenauer-oxidation/); overview at [oxidation reactions](/mechanisms/oxidation-reactions/).
