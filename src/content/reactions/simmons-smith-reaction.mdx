## What is the Simmons–Smith reaction?

The **Simmons–Smith reaction** converts an **alkene into a cyclopropane** by adding a CH₂ unit across the C=C bond. The reagents are **diiodomethane (CH₂I₂) and a zinc–copper couple**, which react to form the organozinc **carbenoid iodomethylzinc iodide, ICH₂ZnI**. The addition is **syn and stereospecific**, so the geometry of the alkene is kept in the cyclopropane, and **zinc iodide** is the by-product. It is named after Howard E. Simmons Jr. and Ronald D. Smith.

## General reaction

<Eq ce="R2C=CR2 + CH2I2 ->[Zn(Cu)] cyclo-(CR2)2CH2 + ZnI2" />

ZnI₂ is the by-product. **Common mistake:** writing the reagent as plain "CH₂I₂, Zn" with heat. The zinc is activated with copper (the zinc–copper couple), and the reaction is usually run in an ether solvent.

## Mechanism

1. **Carbenoid formation.** Zinc inserts into a C–I bond of diiodomethane at the metal surface, giving ICH₂ZnI. (Ultrasound speeds this step.)
2. **Concerted CH₂ transfer.** The carbenoid approaches one face of the alkene and delivers the CH₂ group to **both carbons at the same time** in a single cheletropic step. Both new C–C bonds form on the same face, which is why the addition is syn.
3. **Release of ZnI₂.** The zinc leaves as zinc iodide, and the cyclopropane is formed.

<Eq ce="CH2I2 + Zn(Cu) -> ICH2ZnI" />

<Eq ce="ICH2ZnI + C=C -> cyclopropane + ZnI2" />

<KeyPoint>
The Simmons–Smith reagent is a **carbenoid**, not a free carbene. It behaves like singlet methylene (concerted, stereospecific addition) but does not give the C–H insertion side products that free :CH₂ does.
</KeyPoint>

## Examples

**1. Cyclohexene to norcarane:**

<Eq ce="C6H10 + CH2I2 ->[Zn(Cu)] C7H12 + ZnI2" />

**2. Stereospecificity with 2-butene:** cis-but-2-ene gives only cis-1,2-dimethylcyclopropane, and trans-but-2-ene gives only the trans isomer.

<Eq ce="cis-CH3CH=CHCH3 ->[CH2I2, Zn(Cu)] cis-C3H4(CH3)2" />

**3. Directed cyclopropanation:** in an allylic alcohol such as cyclohex-2-en-1-ol, the zinc coordinates to the OH group, so the CH₂ is delivered to the face of the ring **cis to the hydroxyl group**, even if that face is more crowded.

<ExamTip>
For JEE Advanced, treat Simmons–Smith exactly like singlet carbene addition: draw the cyclopropane with substituents in the same relative positions (cis stays cis, trans stays trans). If an OH group is near the double bond, put the new CH₂ on the same side as the OH.
</ExamTip>

## Comparison with carbene cyclopropanation

| Feature | Simmons–Smith | :CH₂ from CH₂N₂ + light | :CCl₂ from CHCl₃ + base |
|---|---|---|---|
| Species | ICH₂ZnI (carbenoid) | free methylene | dichlorocarbene |
| Product | cyclopropane | cyclopropane (plus insertion products) | 1,1-dichlorocyclopropane |
| Stereochemistry | syn, stereospecific | stereospecific for singlet | stereospecific |

## Limitations and exceptions

- **Steric effects:** without a directing group, the CH₂ adds to the less hindered face of the alkene.
- **Cost:** diiodomethane is expensive; cheaper variants use dibromomethane, or diazomethane with zinc iodide. The **Furukawa modification** replaces the Zn–Cu couple with diethylzinc for more reactive systems.
- **Side reactions:** the Lewis-acidic ZnI₂ can damage acid-sensitive products, and excess reagent over long times can methylate alcohols. Alkenes bearing amino groups can be N-alkylated instead.
- Many groups survive the conditions, including alcohols, ethers, ketones, esters and alkynes.
