## What is Friedel–Crafts alkylation?

**Friedel–Crafts alkylation** is the electrophilic aromatic substitution in which an **alkyl group replaces a ring hydrogen** of benzene or another arene. The arene is treated with an **alkyl halide** in the presence of a Lewis acid, usually **anhydrous AlCl₃**, and the product is an **alkylbenzene** plus HX. Benzene with chloromethane gives toluene; benzene with 2-chloropropane gives cumene (isopropylbenzene). It was discovered by Charles Friedel and James Crafts in 1877.

## General reaction

<Eq ce="Ar-H + R-X ->[anhyd. AlCl3] Ar-R + HX" />

The electrophile in every case is a **carbocation, R⁺** (or a strongly polarised R–X–AlCl₃ complex that behaves like one). The usual ways to make it:

| Starting material | Reagent | Electrophile formed |
|---|---|---|
| Alkyl halide, R–X | Lewis acid (AlCl₃, FeCl₃) | R⁺ |
| Alkene | H⁺ | carbocation (Markovnikov) |
| Alcohol, R–OH | H⁺ | R⁺ after loss of water |
| Aliphatic amine, R–NH₂ | HNO₂ | R⁺ after loss of N₂ |

The alkyl-halide and alkene routes are the textbook ones. The alcohol and amine routes are used in exam answers as other ways of generating the same carbocation.

## Mechanism

1. **Electrophile generation.** AlCl₃ takes chloride from the alkyl halide, giving the carbocation and AlCl₄⁻.
2. **Attack by the ring.** The π electrons of benzene attack R⁺, forming a resonance-stabilised arenium ion (σ-complex). Aromaticity is lost in this step, so it is the slow step.
3. **Deprotonation.** AlCl₄⁻ removes the proton from the sp³ ring carbon, restoring aromaticity, releasing HCl and regenerating AlCl₃.

<Eq ce="(CH3)2CHCl + AlCl3 -> (CH3)2CH+ + AlCl4-" />
<Eq ce="C6H6 + (CH3)2CH+ -> C6H5CH(CH3)2 + H+" />

<KeyPoint>
Because AlCl₃ is regenerated in the last step, it acts as a true catalyst in alkylation. This is different from acylation, where the ketone product ties up the AlCl₃.
</KeyPoint>

## Examples

**1. Toluene from benzene (NCERT example).**

<Eq ce="C6H6 + CH3Cl ->[anhyd. AlCl3] C6H5CH3 + HCl" />

**2. Cumene from benzene and 2-chloropropane.**

<Eq ce="C6H6 + CH3CHClCH3 ->[AlCl3] C6H5CH(CH3)2 + HCl" />

**3. Alkene as the alkylating agent.** Protonating 2,3-dimethylbut-2-ene gives the tertiary 2,3-dimethylbutan-2-yl cation, which then alkylates benzene. Industrially, ethylbenzene and cumene are made from benzene with ethene and propene over solid acid catalysts.

<Eq ce="(CH3)2C=C(CH3)2 ->[H+] (CH3)2C+-CH(CH3)2" />

## Limitations and exceptions

- **Carbocation rearrangement.** Primary alkyl halides rearrange by hydride or methyl shifts. 1-Chlorobutane with benzene at 0 °C gives roughly a 2:1 ratio of sec-butylbenzene to n-butylbenzene, and 1-chloropropane gives mainly isopropylbenzene.
- **Polyalkylation.** An alkyl group activates the ring, so the product reacts faster than benzene. A large excess of benzene is used to favour the mono-alkylated product.
- **Deactivated rings fail.** Rings carrying strongly electron-withdrawing groups such as –NO₂, –CN or C=O do not react, because the weak carbocation electrophile cannot attack such an electron-poor ring.
- **Amino groups block the reaction.** –NH₂, –NHR and –NR₂ groups are Lewis bases; they bind AlCl₃ and deactivate the ring. Aniline therefore does not undergo Friedel–Crafts reactions.

The same two reasons explain every "no reaction" case with R–X/AlCl₃ and, equally, with RCOCl/AlCl₃:

| Group on the ring | Example | Why Friedel–Crafts fails |
|---|---|---|
| –NO₂, –N⁺R₃, –CN, –SO₃H | nitrobenzene | Strongly electron-withdrawing; R⁺ and RCO⁺ are weak electrophiles and cannot attack the electron-poor ring |
| –CHO, –COR, –COOH, –COOR | acetophenone, benzaldehyde | C=O withdraws electrons by resonance, deactivating the ring |
| –NH₂, –NHR, –NR₂ | aniline | N lone pair binds AlCl₃; the resulting –N⁺ group strongly deactivates the ring |

Nitrobenzene is unreactive enough that it is sometimes used in the laboratory as a solvent for reactions with electrophilic reagents.
- **Aryl and vinyl halides cannot be used.** Their cations are too unstable to form, so chlorobenzene or vinyl chloride will not alkylate benzene.
- **Reversibility.** Alkylation can be reversed under the reaction conditions, which allows transalkylation.

<ExamTip>
When a question uses a primary alkyl halide (n-propyl, n-butyl, neopentyl), always check for a 1,2-hydride or 1,2-methyl shift first. To put an unrearranged straight chain on benzene, do Friedel–Crafts acylation and then reduce the C=O by Clemmensen or Wolff–Kishner reduction.
</ExamTip>
