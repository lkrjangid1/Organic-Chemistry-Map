## What is the Reformatsky reaction?

The **Reformatsky reaction** joins an **aldehyde or ketone** with an **α-halo ester** in the presence of **metallic zinc**. After acid work-up the product is a **β-hydroxy ester**. Zinc first inserts into the C–halogen bond of the ester to form an organozinc enolate (the Reformatsky reagent), which then adds to the carbonyl group like a mild Grignard reagent. The reaction is named after the Russian chemist Sergey Reformatsky.

Essential condition: **aldehyde or ketone + α-halo ester**, with Zn.

## General reaction

<Eq ce="R2C=O + BrCH2COOC2H5 ->[1. Zn][2. H3O+] R2C(OH)CH2COOC2H5" />

## Mechanism

1. **Oxidative addition of zinc.** Zinc metal gives up two electrons and inserts into the C–Br bond of the α-halo ester, forming BrZn–CH₂COOC₂H₅. This organozinc compound exists as a zinc enolate, with zinc bonded to both carbon and oxygen.
2. **Coordination.** The carbonyl oxygen of the aldehyde or ketone coordinates to zinc, giving a six-membered, chair-like arrangement.
3. **C–C bond formation.** The nucleophilic α-carbon of the ester attacks the carbonyl carbon. Zinc moves onto the former carbonyl oxygen, giving a zinc alkoxide.
4. **Acid work-up.** Dilute acid protonates the alkoxide and removes zinc as Zn²⁺ salts, giving the β-hydroxy ester.

<Eq ce="BrCH2COOC2H5 + Zn -> BrZnCH2COOC2H5" />
<Eq ce="BrZnCH2COOC2H5 + R2C=O -> R2C(OZnBr)CH2COOC2H5 ->[H3O+] R2C(OH)CH2COOC2H5" />

<KeyPoint>
The zinc enolate is much less basic and less reactive than a lithium enolate or a Grignard reagent. It does not attack the ester group of a second α-halo ester molecule, and proton-transfer side reactions are minimal. That selectivity is the whole point of using zinc.
</KeyPoint>

## Examples

**Benzaldehyde and ethyl bromoacetate:**

<Eq ce="C6H5CHO + BrCH2COOC2H5 ->[1. Zn][2. H3O+] C6H5CH(OH)CH2COOC2H5" />

Product: ethyl 3-hydroxy-3-phenylpropanoate.

**Acetone and ethyl bromoacetate:**

<Eq ce="CH3COCH3 + BrCH2COOC2H5 ->[1. Zn, benzene][2. H3O+] (CH3)2C(OH)CH2COOC2H5" />

Product: ethyl 3-hydroxy-3-methylbutanoate. A ketone works as well as an aldehyde.

<ExamTip>
To build the product quickly, join the carbon that carried the halogen to the carbonyl carbon, turn the C=O into C–OH, and keep the ester group unchanged. The OH always ends up on the carbon β to the ester carbonyl.
</ExamTip>

## Reformatsky vs Grignard

| Feature | Reformatsky (Zn) | Grignard (Mg) |
|---|---|---|
| Organometallic | Zinc enolate from α-halo ester | RMgX from alkyl/aryl halide |
| Reactivity | Mild, selective | Strong nucleophile and base |
| Tolerates ester group | Yes | No (adds to esters) |
| Product with R₂C=O | β-Hydroxy ester | Alcohol |

## Scope and variations

- Because zinc enolates are only weakly basic, they hardly ever remove a proton from the carbonyl partner, so even easily enolised aldehydes and ketones can be used.
- The zinc alkoxide formed after addition is less likely to undergo retro-aldol cleavage than the corresponding lithium or sodium alkoxide, which helps the yield of the β-hydroxy ester.
- Besides aldehydes and ketones, Reformatsky enolates add to acid chlorides, imines, nitriles (the Blaise reaction) and nitrones.
- Other metals or metal salts (for example indium, samarium(II) iodide or chromium(II) chloride) can replace zinc.
- Chiral ligands allow asymmetric versions.
