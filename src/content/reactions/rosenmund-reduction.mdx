## What is the Rosenmund reduction?

The **Rosenmund reduction** is the **catalytic hydrogenation of an acyl chloride (acid chloride) to an aldehyde**. Hydrogen gas is passed through the acyl chloride in the presence of **palladium supported on barium sulphate (Pd/BaSO₄)**, a catalyst whose activity has been deliberately lowered so that the aldehyde is not reduced any further. HCl is the by-product. Karl Wilhelm Rosenmund reported the reaction in 1918.

## General reaction

<Eq ce="R-COCl + H2 ->[Pd/BaSO4] R-CHO + HCl" />

NCERT gives it as the first method for preparing aldehydes from acyl chlorides.

## How it works

The reaction is a **hydrogenolysis**: the C–Cl bond of the acyl chloride is broken by hydrogen on the palladium surface and replaced by a C–H bond.

1. The acyl chloride and H₂ are both adsorbed on palladium.
2. The C–Cl bond is cleaved and hydrogen is delivered to the acyl carbon, giving R–CHO.
3. The chlorine leaves as HCl.

The danger is step 4, which a fully active catalyst would also carry out: hydrogenation of the aldehyde C=O to give R–CH₂OH. Lowering the catalyst's activity prevents this.

<KeyPoint title="Why the catalyst is poisoned">
Barium sulphate has a low surface area, which tempers the activity of the palladium. In Indian textbooks BaSO₄ itself is usually called the "poison". For especially reactive acyl chlorides an additional sulfur poison, such as thiourea, is added. Without this deactivation the aldehyde is reduced to a primary alcohol, which can then react with leftover acyl chloride to form an ester.
</KeyPoint>

<ExamTip title="Common mistake">
The metal in the Rosenmund catalyst is **palladium**, not nickel: write H₂, Pd/BaSO₄. Nickel is a hydrogenation catalyst too, but it is not the Rosenmund catalyst.
</ExamTip>

## Examples

**1. Benzoyl chloride to benzaldehyde.**

<Eq ce="C6H5COCl + H2 ->[Pd/BaSO4] C6H5CHO + HCl" />

**2. Ethanoyl chloride to ethanal.**

<Eq ce="CH3COCl + H2 ->[Pd/BaSO4] CH3CHO + HCl" />

**3. A two-step route from a carboxylic acid.** Convert the acid to its chloride with SOCl₂, then reduce:

<Eq ce="CH3CH2COOH ->[SOCl2] CH3CH2COCl ->[H2, Pd/BaSO4] CH3CH2CHO" />

## Limitations and exceptions

- **No formaldehyde.** The required starting material, formyl chloride (H–CO–Cl), is unstable at room temperature and decomposes to CO and HCl, so methanal cannot be made this way.

<Eq ce="HCOCl -> CO + HCl" />

- **Over-reduction.** With an insufficiently poisoned catalyst the product is the primary alcohol, plus some ester.
- **Moisture.** Strictly anhydrous solvents are recommended; water hydrolyses the acyl chloride back to the carboxylic acid and gives side products.

## Comparison with other routes to aldehydes

| Starting material | Reagent | Named method |
|---|---|---|
| Acyl chloride | H₂, Pd/BaSO₄ | Rosenmund reduction |
| Nitrile | SnCl₂, HCl, then H₃O⁺ | Stephen reduction |
| Nitrile or ester | DIBAL-H, then H₂O | DIBAL-H reduction |

LiAlH₄ is not used for this job: it reduces acyl chlorides all the way to primary alcohols.
