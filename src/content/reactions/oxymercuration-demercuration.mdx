## What is oxymercuration–demercuration?

**Oxymercuration–demercuration** is a two-step method for adding water across a C=C bond to make an **alcohol** with **Markovnikov** orientation. In step (i) the alkene reacts with **mercuric acetate, Hg(OAc)₂**, in aqueous THF; in step (ii) the organomercury intermediate is reduced with **sodium borohydride (NaBH₄)** in base. Because the reaction passes through a bridged mercurinium ion rather than a free carbocation, the carbon skeleton **does not rearrange**.

For propene, the product is **propan-2-ol**: OH on the more substituted carbon, H on the CH₂ end.

## Mechanism

**Step (i): oxymercuration**

1. **Electrophilic attack.** Hg(OAc)₂ loses an acetate ion, and the electrophilic ⁺HgOAc is attacked by the π electrons of the alkene. A three-membered **mercurinium ion** forms, with mercury bridging both carbons.
2. **Attack by water.** Water opens the bridge at the **more substituted carbon**, because that carbon carries more of the partial positive charge. It approaches from the side opposite to mercury, so this step is an **anti addition**.
3. **Deprotonation.** Acetate (or water) removes a proton from the oxonium ion, giving a neutral β-hydroxyalkylmercury acetate.

<Eq ce="CH3-CH=CH2 + Hg(OAc)2 + H2O -> CH3-CH(OH)-CH2-HgOAc + CH3COOH" />

**Step (ii): demercuration**

4. **Reduction.** NaBH₄ in aqueous base replaces the –HgOAc group with H, and metallic mercury separates out. This step goes through radical intermediates and is not stereospecific.

<Eq ce="CH3-CH(OH)-CH2-HgOAc ->[NaBH4, OH-] CH3-CH(OH)-CH3 + Hg" />

<KeyPoint>
Oxymercuration–demercuration = Markovnikov hydration **without rearrangement**. The first step is an anti addition, but the overall stereochemistry is scrambled by the radical demercuration step.
</KeyPoint>

## Examples

**1. Propene to propan-2-ol:**

<Eq ce="CH3-CH=CH2 ->[(i) Hg(OAc)2, H2O][(ii) NaBH4] CH3-CH(OH)-CH3" />

**2. 3,3-Dimethylbut-1-ene to 3,3-dimethylbutan-2-ol.** With dilute acid this alkene gives mainly the rearranged 2,3-dimethylbutan-2-ol through a methyl shift. Oxymercuration–demercuration gives the **unrearranged** alcohol:

<Eq ce="(CH3)3C-CH=CH2 ->[(i) Hg(OAc)2, H2O][(ii) NaBH4] (CH3)3C-CH(OH)-CH3" />

**3. Norbornene to exo-norborneol.** In this rigid bicyclic alkene, water attacks from the less hindered **exo** face, so the OH ends up exo.

<ExamTip>
If a question shows an alkene that would rearrange under H₃O⁺ (a 2° carbocation next to a quaternary or 3° carbon), and asks for the alcohol **without** rearrangement, the answer is oxymercuration–demercuration (Markovnikov) or hydroboration–oxidation (anti-Markovnikov).
</ExamTip>

## Alkoxymercuration: making ethers

If an alcohol is used as the solvent instead of water, the alcohol oxygen opens the mercurinium ion. After NaBH₄ reduction the product is an **ether** with the OR group on the more substituted carbon. For example, propene with Hg(OAc)₂ in methanol, then NaBH₄, gives 2-methoxypropane.

## Comparison of alkene hydration methods

| Method | Reagents | Orientation | Rearrangement? | Stereochemistry |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Acid-catalysed hydration | dil. H₂SO₄ / H₃O⁺ | Markovnikov | Yes (carbocation) | Not stereospecific |
| Oxymercuration–demercuration | Hg(OAc)₂, H₂O; NaBH₄ | Markovnikov | No | Anti in step (i); overall scrambled |
| Hydroboration–oxidation | BH₃·THF; H₂O₂, NaOH | Anti-Markovnikov | No | Syn |

## Limitations and exceptions

- Mercury compounds are highly toxic, so the method is used less in industry today; it remains a favourite in exam questions.
- Only the hydration step is anti. Do not write a fixed cis or trans relationship for the final H and OH unless the substrate is rigid (as with norbornene).
- The mercury salt may be written generally as (RCOO)₂Hg; mercuric acetate (R = CH₃) is the standard choice, and either form is accepted in exam answers as long as it is followed by NaBH₄.
