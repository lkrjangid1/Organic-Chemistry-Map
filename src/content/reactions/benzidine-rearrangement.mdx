## What is the benzidine rearrangement?

The **benzidine rearrangement** is the **acid-catalysed** conversion of **hydrazobenzene** (1,2-diphenylhydrazine, C₆H₅NH–NHC₆H₅) into **benzidine** (biphenyl-4,4′-diamine). With a mineral acid such as HCl or H₂SO₄, the **N–N bond breaks** and a new **C–C bond** joins the two **para** carbons, so two aniline units become linked ring-to-ring. It is the classic example of a **[5,5] sigmatropic shift** and has been studied since the 1860s.

## General reaction

<Eq ce="C6H5NH-NHC6H5 ->[H+] H2N-C6H4-C6H4-NH2 (4,4')" />

The starting material comes from **nitrobenzene**. Reduction in an alkaline (non-acidic) medium couples two molecules and stops at hydrazobenzene; acid then triggers the rearrangement.

<Eq ce="2 C6H5NO2 ->[reduction][alkaline medium] C6H5NH-NHC6H5 ->[H+] benzidine" />

In exam answers, the first step is often written as **electrolytic reduction of nitrobenzene in a basic medium**; metal reductions in alkali (iron or zinc dust) and selective catalytic hydrogenation give the same intermediate.

## Mechanism

1. **Diprotonation.** Both nitrogen atoms of hydrazobenzene are protonated by the acid, giving C₆H₅NH₂⁺–⁺NH₂C₆H₅. Protonation weakens the N–N bond and sets up the shift.
2. **[5,5] sigmatropic shift.** Counting from each nitrogen (1 and 1′) round to the para carbon (5 and 5′), the N–N σ bond is replaced by a C5–C5′ bond in one concerted step. Both rings stay attached to each other the whole time, which is why **crossover experiments** find no mixed products: the reaction is **intramolecular**.
3. **Rearomatisation.** Each ring loses the H⁺ from the new sp³ carbon, restoring aromaticity and giving the benzidine salt. Base releases free benzidine.

<KeyPoint>
The [5,5] label works like [3,3] in the Claisen and Cope rearrangements: number outward from the two atoms of the breaking σ bond (both called 1) to the two atoms that form the new bond.
</KeyPoint>

## Examples

**1. Hydrazobenzene to benzidine (major) and diphenyline (minor).** Diphenyline is 2,4′-diaminobiphenyl: one ring couples through its para carbon, the other through its ortho carbon.

<Eq ce="C6H5NHNHC6H5 ->[HCl] 4,4'-(H2N)2C6H4C6H4 (major) + 2,4'-isomer (minor)" />

**2. Semidines.** In smaller amounts, C–N coupling gives **o- and p-semidines** (aminodiphenylamines), in which one nitrogen stays bonded to the other ring. Recent experiments and calculations explain these by a different series of sigmatropic shifts.

**3. Benzidine to Congo red.** Tetrazotising benzidine (both NH₂ groups to N₂⁺) and coupling with two molecules of naphthionic acid (4-aminonaphthalene-1-sulphonic acid) gives the dye **Congo red**. See [diazo coupling](/reactions/diazo-coupling-reaction/).

<ExamTip>
Asked "nitrobenzene to benzidine"? Write two steps: reduction in **alkaline** medium to hydrazobenzene, then **acid** (H⁺) for the rearrangement. Reduction in strongly **acidic** medium instead gives aniline, or p-aminophenol through the [Bamberger rearrangement](/reactions/bamberger-rearrangement/).
</ExamTip>

## Limitations and exceptions

- The product is never pure benzidine: diphenyline and semidines always accompany it.
- The mechanism has been debated for over 150 years. The concerted [5,5] shift is the accepted textbook picture for benzidine itself, while diphenyline and the semidines are explained by other pathways.
- **Safety:** benzidine is a known human carcinogen linked to bladder cancer, so it and the benzidine-based dyes have been withdrawn from most industrial uses.
