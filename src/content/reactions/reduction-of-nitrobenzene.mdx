## What is the reduction of nitrobenzene?

The **reduction of nitrobenzene** converts the –NO₂ group of C₆H₅NO₂ into a less oxidised nitrogen group. Which product you get depends mainly on the **pH of the medium**: metal and acid (Sn/HCl, Fe/HCl) or catalytic hydrogenation give **aniline**; zinc with ammonium chloride (nearly neutral) gives **N-phenylhydroxylamine**; and reducing agents in alkali give two-ring products such as **azoxybenzene, azobenzene and hydrazobenzene**. Reduction in acid is the standard way to make aniline, the starting point for diazonium chemistry.

Nitrobenzene itself is a pale yellow, water-insoluble oil with an almond-like smell, sold in the past as **oil of mirbane** (also called artificial oil of bitter almonds).

## Products in different media

| Medium | Reagent | Product |
|---|---|---|
| Acidic (pH below 7) | Sn/HCl, Fe/HCl, Zn/CH₃COOH, SnCl₂/HCl, TiCl₃ | Aniline, C₆H₅NH₂ (as anilinium salt until NaOH is added) |
| Catalytic | H₂ with Ni, Pd/C or Pt | Aniline |
| Neutral (about pH 6–7) | Zn dust / aq. NH₄Cl | N-Phenylhydroxylamine, C₆H₅NHOH |
| Alkaline, mild | Na₃AsO₃ / NaOH | Azoxybenzene, C₆H₅–N(O)=N–C₆H₅ |
| Alkaline | Zn / NaOH | Azobenzene, C₆H₅–N=N–C₆H₅ |
| Alkaline, excess metal | Excess Zn dust / NaOH | Hydrazobenzene, C₆H₅–NH–NH–C₆H₅ |
| Metal hydride | LiAlH₄, then H₃O⁺ | Azobenzene (not aniline) |

<KeyPoint title="Acid → amine, neutral → hydroxylamine, base → dimers">
The same six-electron reduction runs to completion in strong acid. In a weakly acidic NH₄Cl solution it is slow enough to stop at the hydroxylamine. In alkali the partly reduced intermediates react with each other and give products with an N–N bond.
</KeyPoint>

In exam answers, a few more reagents are listed for each medium: glucose with NaOH (azoxybenzene), Sn with NaOH (azobenzene) and Al–Hg or Al with a salt solution such as NH₄Cl (phenylhydroxylamine). These follow the same pH rule.

## Mechanism: stepwise reduction

Converting –NO₂ to –NH₂ needs **six electrons and six protons**, and it happens in two-electron steps through two intermediates:

<Eq ce="C6H5NO2 ->[2e^-, 2H^+][-H2O] C6H5N=O ->[2e^-, 2H^+] C6H5NHOH ->[2e^-, 2H^+][-H2O] C6H5NH2" />

1. **Nitro to nitroso.** Two electrons and two protons remove one oxygen as water, giving nitrosobenzene.
2. **Nitroso to hydroxylamine.** Two more electrons give N-phenylhydroxylamine. In neutral NH₄Cl solution the reduction stops here.
3. **Hydroxylamine to amine.** In strong acid the last N–O bond is cleaved, giving the anilinium ion.
4. **Work-up.** NaOH removes H⁺ from C₆H₅NH₃⁺ to release aniline, which is separated by steam distillation.

In alkaline solution the intermediates meet before they are reduced further. Nitrosobenzene condenses with phenylhydroxylamine to give azoxybenzene, which can be reduced on to azobenzene and then hydrazobenzene:

<Eq ce="C6H5NO + C6H5NHOH -> C6H5N(O)=NC6H5 + H2O" />

## Examples

**Aniline from nitrobenzene (NCERT):**

<Eq ce="C6H5NO2 ->[Fe scrap, HCl][\Delta] C6H5NH3^+Cl^- ->[NaOH] C6H5NH2" />

**Azobenzene with LiAlH₄:**

<Eq ce="2C6H5NO2 ->[LiAlH4][then H3O+] C6H5-N=N-C6H5" />

**Phenylhydroxylamine with zinc and ammonium chloride:**

<Eq ce="C6H5NO2 + 2Zn + 4NH4Cl -> C6H5NHOH + 2ZnCl2 + 4NH3 + H2O" />

<ExamTip title="Common mistake">
Do not write aniline as the product of nitrobenzene with LiAlH₄. Metal hydrides give the azo compound with aromatic nitro groups. LiAlH₄ does reduce **aliphatic** nitro compounds (R–NO₂) to amines (R–NH₂).
</ExamTip>

## Electrolytic reduction and rearrangements

Electrolytic reduction can stop at the hydroxylamine stage. In exam answers the outcome is linked to the electrolyte: weakly acidic gives aniline; strongly acidic gives phenylhydroxylamine, which acid converts into **p-aminophenol** ([Bamberger rearrangement](/reactions/bamberger-rearrangement/)); alkaline gives hydrazobenzene, which acid converts into **benzidine** ([benzidine rearrangement](/reactions/benzidine-rearrangement/)).

<Eq ce="C6H5NHOH ->[H2SO4 (aq)] p-HOC6H4NH2" />

## Limitations and exceptions

- Sn/HCl and Fe/HCl reduce every nitro group present. To reduce only one nitro group of a dinitro compound, use the [Zinin reduction](/reactions/zinin-reduction/) with Na₂S or (NH₄)₂S.
- Catalytic hydrogenation also reduces C=C bonds and can remove aryl halogens. Metal/acid methods leave these alone.
- In acid the product is the ammonium salt, so a base must be added before the amine can be isolated.
