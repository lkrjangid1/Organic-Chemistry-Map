## What is the diazo coupling reaction?

In the **diazo (azo) coupling reaction**, an **arenediazonium ion, ArN₂⁺**, acts as a weak electrophile and attacks a strongly activated aromatic ring (a **phenol, naphthol or aryl amine**), normally at the **para position**. Unlike the Sandmeyer-type replacements, **both nitrogen atoms are kept**, and two rings end up joined by an **–N=N– (azo) bridge**. The products have extended conjugation, so they are brightly coloured and used as **azo dyes**.

## General reaction

<Eq ce="C6H5N2^+ Cl^- + C6H5OH ->[NaOH (aq), 273 K] p-HO-C6H4-N=N-C6H5 + HCl" />

<Eq ce="C6H5N2^+ Cl^- + C6H5NH2 ->[mild H+, 273 K] p-H2N-C6H4-N=N-C6H5 + HCl" />

Phenol gives **p-hydroxyazobenzene** (orange); aniline gives **p-aminoazobenzene** ("aniline yellow").

## Mechanism

1. **Electrophile.** The terminal nitrogen of Ar–N≡N⁺ is electron-poor. It is a weak electrophile, so only rings carrying –O⁻, –OH, –NH₂ or –NR₂ react.
2. **Attack.** The para carbon of the phenoxide (or amine) attacks the terminal N, giving a resonance-stabilised σ-complex.
3. **Loss of H⁺.** Deprotonation restores the aromatic ring and gives the azo compound, almost always as the more stable **trans (E)** isomer.

<KeyPoint title="Where coupling occurs">
Para to the activating group if that position is free; otherwise ortho, more slowly. In naphthalenes, coupling goes **ortho to OH at the α-carbon**, so β-naphthol couples at C-1.
</KeyPoint>

## pH control

| Coupling partner | Best medium | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Phenols, naphthols | Mildly alkaline (above about pH 7.5) | Phenoxide ion is a much stronger activator than phenol |
| Aryl amines, N,N-dialkylanilines | Mildly acidic (below about pH 6) | NH₂/NR₂ is the stronger activator here and the diazonium salt stays intact |

In exam answers the medium is kept **mild** both ways: very strong base destroys the diazonium ion, and very strong acid protonates the amine so its ring is no longer activated.

## Examples

**1. β-Naphthol test dye.** Benzenediazonium chloride and β-naphthol in NaOH give **1-phenylazo-2-naphthol**, an intense orange-red precipitate.

<Eq ce="C6H5N2^+ Cl^- + C10H7OH ->[NaOH] 1-(C6H5N=N)-2-C10H6OH + HCl" />

**2. Methyl orange.** Diazotised **sulphanilic acid** couples with **N,N-dimethylaniline** at its para position.

<Eq ce="p-HO3SC6H4N2^+ + C6H5N(CH3)2 -> p-HO3SC6H4-N=N-C6H4N(CH3)2 + H+" />

It is red below pH 3.1 and yellow above pH 4.4.

**3. Methyl red.** Diazotised **anthranilic acid** (o-aminobenzoic acid) couples with N,N-dimethylaniline, giving o-HOOC–C₆H₄–N=N–C₆H₄–NMe₂. It is red below pH 4.4 and yellow above 6.2.

**4. Bismarck brown Y.** **m-Phenylenediamine** is diazotised at both NH₂ groups, and the bis(diazonium) ion couples with two more molecules of m-phenylenediamine. The diamine is both the diazo component and the coupling partner.

<Eq ce="C6H4(N2^+)2 + 2 m-C6H4(NH2)2 -> [(H2N)2C6H3N=N]2C6H4 + 2H+" />

**5. Congo red.** Tetrazotised **benzidine** couples with two molecules of **naphthionic acid** (4-aminonaphthalene-1-sulphonic acid), each ortho to its NH₂ group. Congo red is blue below pH 3.0 and red above pH 5.2.

<ExamTip>
Coupling keeps N₂ in the product; replacement reactions (Sandmeyer, Gattermann, KI, hydrolysis) lose it. The diazonium salt is always prepared and used cold (273–278 K).
</ExamTip>

## Limitations and exceptions

- Benzene and deactivated rings do not undergo azo coupling.
- With primary or secondary amines in alkaline solution, the diazonium ion can attack **nitrogen** instead, giving a **triazene** (N-coupling); with aniline, C- and N-coupling compete.
- **Gomberg–Bachmann reaction:** benzene with a diazonium salt in the presence of base gives **biphenyl** through an aryl radical. This is a biaryl coupling, not an azo coupling, and yields are low.

<Eq ce="C6H5N2^+ Cl^- + C6H6 ->[NaOH] C6H5-C6H5 + N2 + HCl" />
