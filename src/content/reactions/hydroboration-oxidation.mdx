## What is hydroboration–oxidation?

**Hydroboration–oxidation** is a two-step conversion of an **alkene into an alcohol** in which water is added in the **anti-Markovnikov** sense. In step (i) **diborane (B₂H₆)** or its THF complex **BH₃·THF** adds across the C=C bond to give a trialkylborane. In step (ii) **alkaline hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂/NaOH)** replaces each C–B bond with a C–OH bond. The addition is **syn**, and there is **no rearrangement**. The reaction was introduced by H. C. Brown in the late 1950s (Nobel Prize, 1979).

Propene gives **propan-1-ol**, the alcohol that acid-catalysed hydration cannot make.

## Mechanism

**Step (i): hydroboration**

1. **Concerted four-centred addition.** The empty p orbital of boron accepts the π electrons of the alkene. At the same moment a hydride moves from boron to the other alkene carbon. B–C and C–H bonds form together on the **same face** (syn addition), and no carbocation is formed.
2. **Regiochemistry.** Boron attaches to the **less substituted (less hindered) carbon**; H goes to the more substituted carbon.
3. **Repeat.** Each BH₃ has three B–H bonds, so with an unhindered alkene it adds three times to give a trialkylborane, R₃B.

<Eq ce="3CH3-CH=CH2 + BH3 ->[THF] (CH3CH2CH2)3B" />

**Step (ii): oxidation**

4. Hydroperoxide ion (HOO⁻) attacks boron. An alkyl group then migrates from boron to oxygen with **retention of configuration**, pushing out OH⁻. After three such steps a trialkyl borate forms, which NaOH hydrolyses to the alcohol and borate.

<Eq ce="(CH3CH2CH2)3B + 3H2O2 ->[OH-] 3CH3CH2CH2OH + B(OH)3" />

<KeyPoint>
Three facts to write for hydroboration–oxidation: **syn addition**, **no rearrangement**, **anti-Markovnikov** orientation of H and OH.
</KeyPoint>

## Other reactions of the trialkylborane

The same trialkylborane, (CH₃CH₂CH₂)₃B, can be converted into different products. Checked against the sources:

| Reagent on R₃B | Product from propene | Status |
|---|---|---|
| H₂O₂ / NaOH | Propan-1-ol (H, OH added) | Confirmed |
| RCOOH (protonolysis) | Propane (H, H added) | Confirmed: organoboranes are cleaved by acids |
| NH₂Cl or NH₂OSO₃H | Propan-1-amine (H, NH₂ added) | Confirmed with NH₂Cl or NH₂OSO₃H; NH₃ alone is a common mistake |
| X₂ / NaOH | 1-Halopropane (H, X added) | Confirmed: base activation, then halogen |
| AgNO₃ / NaOH | n-Hexane (two propyl groups joined) | Not covered by the sources read |

In exam answers, alkaline AgNO₃ is given as a reagent that couples two alkyl groups of the borane to form an alkane with double the carbon count (propyl + propyl = hexane).

## Examples

**1. Propene to propan-1-ol**:

<Eq ce="CH3-CH=CH2 ->[(i) B2H6/THF][(ii) H2O2, NaOH] CH3-CH2-CH2-OH" />

**2. Styrene to 2-phenylethan-1-ol.** Boron goes to the terminal CH₂, so OH ends up away from the ring:

<Eq ce="C6H5-CH=CH2 ->[(i) BH3·THF][(ii) H2O2, NaOH] C6H5-CH2-CH2-OH" />

**3. 1-Methylcyclopentene to trans-2-methylcyclopentan-1-ol.** H and OH add to the same face (syn), so the new H and OH are cis to each other; the methyl group and OH are therefore trans.

<ExamTip>
Compare with acid-catalysed hydration: propene + H₃O⁺ gives propan-2-ol, while propene + B₂H₆ then H₂O₂/OH⁻ gives propan-1-ol. Oxymercuration–demercuration also gives propan-2-ol, but without rearrangement.
</ExamTip>

## Limitations and exceptions

- With very hindered alkenes, BH₃ may add only once or twice; bulky boranes such as 9-BBN or disiamylborane are used in practice to sharpen the regioselectivity.
- Diborane is toxic and catches fire in air, so it is handled as the BH₃·THF solution.
- Terminal alkynes undergo hydroboration–oxidation to give aldehydes (via the enol), again anti-Markovnikov.
