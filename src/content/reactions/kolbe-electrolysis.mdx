## What is Kolbe electrolysis?

**Kolbe electrolysis** (Kolbe's electrolytic method) is the electrolysis of an **aqueous solution of the sodium or potassium salt of a carboxylic acid**, which gives an **alkane with an even number of carbon atoms at the anode**, along with carbon dioxide. Two carboxylate ions each lose an electron and CO₂, and the two alkyl radicals combine: 2RCOO⁻ → R–R + 2CO₂ + 2e⁻. It is a decarboxylative dimerisation, named after Hermann Kolbe, and is an NCERT method for preparing alkanes.

## General reaction

<Eq ce="2 RCOONa + 2 H2O ->[electrolysis] R-R + 2 CO2 + H2 + 2 NaOH" />

With the potassium salt it is written as: R–COO⁻K⁺ →(electrolysis) R–R + CO₂.

## Mechanism

The reaction is a **free radical** process.

**In solution:** the salt ionises.

<Eq ce="2 CH3COONa -> 2 CH3COO- + 2 Na+" />

**At the anode (oxidation):**

1. Each carboxylate ion loses one electron, forming a carboxyl (acyloxy) radical.
2. The carboxyl radical loses CO₂ almost at once, giving an alkyl radical.
3. Two alkyl radicals combine to form the alkane.

<Eq ce="2 CH3COO- ->[-2e-] 2 CH3COO^. -> 2 CH3^. + 2 CO2" />
<Eq ce="CH3^. + CH3^. -> CH3-CH3" />

**At the cathode (reduction):** water is reduced to hydrogen gas, leaving hydroxide ions that pair with Na⁺ or K⁺.

<Eq ce="2 H2O + 2 e- -> H2 + 2 OH-" />

<ExamTip title="Common mistake">
Writing the cathode step as K⁺ + e⁻ → K, followed by K + H₂O → KOH + H₂. NCERT writes the cathode reaction as reduction of water itself to H₂ and OH⁻; the overall result (H₂ gas and KOH/NaOH in solution) is the same. Write the NCERT form in board answers.
</ExamTip>

## Examples

1. **Sodium acetate to ethane** (NCERT):

<Eq ce="2 CH3COONa + 2 H2O ->[electrolysis] CH3-CH3 + 2 CO2 + H2 + 2 NaOH" />

2. **Potassium propanoate to butane:**

<Eq ce="2 CH3CH2COOK + 2 H2O ->[electrolysis] CH3CH2CH2CH3 + 2 CO2 + H2 + 2 KOH" />

3. **Mixed salts give mixtures:** electrolysing sodium acetate with sodium propanoate gives ethane, propane and butane, because all three radical combinations occur.

<Eq ce="CH3COO- + C2H5COO- ->[electrolysis] CH3-CH3 + CH3-C2H5 + C2H5-C2H5" />

<ExamTip>
Count carbons: the alkane has 2 × (carbons in the acid − 1). Sodium butanoate (C₄) gives hexane (C₆). Methane can never be made this way, which is a favourite NCERT question.
</ExamTip>

## Stereochemistry

Because the alkyl radical is planar (or rapidly inverting), a stereocentre on the carbon next to COO⁻ is not preserved. In exam answers this is described as both **retention and inversion** occurring, so an optically active acid gives a racemised product.

## Limitations and exceptions

- **No methane:** the smallest product is ethane.
- **Odd-carbon alkanes** need two different salts and give a mixture (as in the Wurtz reaction).
- **Side products:** if the alkyl radical is oxidised further, it forms a carbocation (Hofer–Moest reaction), leading to alcohols, alkenes or rearranged products. This happens readily when the α-carbon carries a carbocation-stabilising group.
- In practice yields are often below 50%. Coupling is favoured by a platinum anode, high current density and weakly acidic, protic solvents.
