## What is the pinacol coupling reaction?

The **pinacol coupling reaction** (pinacol formation) is the **reductive coupling of two aldehyde or ketone molecules** at their carbonyl carbons to give a **vicinal (1,2-) diol**. A one-electron donor such as **magnesium amalgam (Mg/Hg)** reduces each C=O group to a radical, the two radicals join through a new C–C bond, and **water** then protonates the two oxygens. With acetone the product is **pinacol** (2,3-dimethylbutane-2,3-diol), which gives the reaction its name.

## General reaction

<Eq ce="2 R2C=O ->[1. Mg/Hg][2. H2O] R2C(OH)-C(OH)R2" />

In general, R–CO–R →(Mg–Hg, H₂O) R–C(OH)(R)–C(OH)(R)–R. Aldehydes react the same way and give 1,2-diols with H on each carbinol carbon. The molecule doubles in size: two C₃ acetone units give the C₆ diol, so the reaction is a C–C bond-forming step as well as a reduction. Water (or another proton donor) is essential in the second step; without it the product stays as the magnesium diolate.

**Unsymmetrical ketones.** A ketone R–CO–R′ with two different groups couples head-to-head at the carbonyl carbons to give R(R′)C(OH)–C(OH)(R′)R. Butanone, for example, gives 3,4-dimethylhexane-3,4-diol:

<Eq ce="2 CH3COC2H5 ->[1. Mg/Hg][2. H2O] C2H5(CH3)C(OH)-C(OH)(CH3)C2H5" />

Both carbinol carbons are now stereocentres, so a mixture of diastereomers (meso and racemic forms) is obtained; controlling this ratio is why modern reagents such as SmI₂ are used.

## Mechanism

1. **Electron transfer.** Magnesium gives up two electrons (Mg → Mg²⁺ + 2e⁻). One electron goes to each of two carbonyl groups, converting them into **ketyl radical anions** (an O⁻ and an unpaired electron on carbon).
2. **Radical coupling.** The two ketyl radicals combine at carbon, forming the central C–C bond and a 1,2-diolate. With magnesium, both oxygens bind the Mg²⁺ ion in a five-membered cyclic complex.
3. **Hydrolysis.** Water breaks up the magnesium complex, protonating both oxygens to give the diol and forming Mg(OH)₂.

<Eq ce="2 (CH3)2C=O + Mg -> 2 (CH3)2C^.-O^- + Mg^2+" />
<Eq ce="2 (CH3)2C^.-O^- -> (CH3)2C(O^-)-C(O^-)(CH3)2" />
<Eq ce="(CH3)2C(O^-)-C(O^-)(CH3)2 + 2 H2O -> (CH3)2C(OH)-C(OH)(CH3)2 + 2 OH-" />

<KeyPoint>
Pinacol coupling is a reduction (each carbonyl carbon gains an electron) and a free radical C–C bond formation in one step. The product always has two OH groups on adjacent carbons.
</KeyPoint>

## Examples

1. **Acetone to pinacol** (the classic example):

<Eq ce="2 CH3COCH3 ->[1. Mg/Hg][2. H2O] (CH3)2C(OH)-C(OH)(CH3)2" />

2. **Benzophenone to benzopinacol**, a diaryl ketone that couples well (also possible photochemically):

<Eq ce="2 (C6H5)2C=O ->[1. Mg/Hg][2. H2O] (C6H5)2C(OH)-C(OH)(C6H5)2" />

3. **Benzaldehyde to 1,2-diphenylethane-1,2-diol** (hydrobenzoin), an aldehyde example:

<Eq ce="2 C6H5CHO ->[1. Mg/Hg][2. H2O] C6H5CH(OH)-CH(OH)C6H5" />

## Pinacol coupling followed by rearrangement

Pinacols and other highly substituted 1,2-diols readily undergo the **pinacol–pinacolone rearrangement** with acid. This gives the common two-step exam sequence:

<Eq ce="2 CH3COCH3 ->[1. Mg/Hg][2. H2O] (CH3)2C(OH)-C(OH)(CH3)2 ->[H+][\Delta] (CH3)3C-CO-CH3" />

<ExamTip>
If a question shows a ketone →(Mg/Hg, H₂O) → A →(H⁺, Δ) → B, then A is the pinacol and B is the rearranged ketone (pinacolone for acetone). Do not confuse Mg/Hg (coupling) with Zn/Hg + HCl (Clemmensen reduction to CH₂).
</ExamTip>

## Limitations and exceptions

- The reaction is usually a **homocoupling** (two identical carbonyl compounds). Mixing two different ketones gives a mixture of diols.
- Aryl ketones and aldehydes generally give **higher yields** than aliphatic ones.
- With classical alkali or alkaline-earth metals, yields and selectivity are modest. Modern versions use catalytic early transition-metal salts or SmI₂ for better yields and stereoselectivity.
- Stoichiometric low-valent titanium removes both oxygens and gives an **alkene** instead (McMurry reaction).
