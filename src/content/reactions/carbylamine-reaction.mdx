## What is the carbylamine reaction?

The **carbylamine reaction**, also called the **isocyanide test**, is the reaction of a **primary amine** (aliphatic or aromatic) with **chloroform and ethanolic potassium hydroxide** on heating. The product is an **isocyanide (carbylamine), R–N≡C**, which has a strong, very unpleasant smell. Because **secondary and tertiary amines do not react**, the smell is used as a test for primary amines.

In preparative chemistry the same reaction is known as the **Hofmann isocyanide synthesis**, where aqueous NaOH and a phase-transfer catalyst are often used.

## General reaction

<Eq ce="R-NH2 + CHCl3 + 3KOH ->[\Delta] R-NC + 3KCl + 3H2O" />

The base may be written as NaOH (giving R–NC + NaCl + H₂O) or KOH; both work, and NCERT uses alcoholic KOH. With three equivalents of base the balanced equation has three KCl (or NaCl) and three water.

## Mechanism

1. **Making the carbene (α-elimination).** Hydroxide removes the acidic H of chloroform, giving CCl₃⁻, which loses Cl⁻ to form **dichlorocarbene, :CCl₂**.
2. **Nucleophilic attack.** The lone pair on the amine nitrogen attacks the electron-poor carbene carbon, giving R–NH₂⁺–CCl₂⁻.
3. **Proton transfer.** A proton moves from nitrogen to the carbanion carbon, giving R–NH–CHCl₂.
4. **First dehydrochlorination.** The base removes HCl, giving R–N=CHCl.
5. **Second dehydrochlorination.** The base removes a second HCl, leaving the isocyanide, R–N⁺≡C⁻.

<Eq ce="CHCl3 + OH- -> CCl3^- + H2O" />

<Eq ce="CCl3^- -> :CCl2 + Cl-" />

<Eq ce="R-NH2 + :CCl2 -> R-NH-CHCl2 ->[- 2HCl] R-NC" />

<KeyPoint>
The nitrogen loses both of its hydrogens during the reaction (as two HCl, removed by the base). That is why only primary amines, with an –NH₂ group, can form an isocyanide.
</KeyPoint>

## Examples

**1. Aniline gives phenyl isocyanide:**

<Eq ce="C6H5NH2 + CHCl3 + 3KOH ->[\Delta] C6H5NC + 3KCl + 3H2O" />

**2. Ethylamine gives ethyl isocyanide:**

<Eq ce="C2H5NH2 + CHCl3 + 3KOH ->[\Delta] C2H5NC + 3KCl + 3H2O" />

**3. tert-Butylamine (Hofmann isocyanide synthesis, phase-transfer conditions):**

<Eq ce="(CH3)3CNH2 + CHCl3 + 3NaOH -> (CH3)3CNC + 3NaCl + 3H2O" />

<ExamTip>
Use the carbylamine test to tell a primary amine from secondary and tertiary amines, and remember it works for both aliphatic and aromatic primary amines. To separate 1°, 2° and 3° amines more fully, combine it with the Hinsberg test or the nitrous acid test.
</ExamTip>

## Isocyanide vs cyanide

The product is an **isocyanide** (R–N≡C, carbon bonded through nitrogen), not a nitrile (R–C≡N). Alkyl cyanides are made from alkyl halides and KCN; isocyanides from the carbylamine reaction or from alkyl halides and AgCN.

## Limitations and exceptions

- Secondary and tertiary amines give no isocyanide, so no smell.
- Isocyanides are toxic and foul-smelling, so the test is done on a very small scale in a fume cupboard.
- Dichlorocarbene also reacts with alkenes (to dichlorocyclopropanes) and with phenoxides ([Reimer–Tiemann reaction](/reactions/reimer-tiemann-reaction/)), so other functional groups in the molecule can compete.
