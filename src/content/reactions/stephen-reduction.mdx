## What is the Stephen reduction?

The **Stephen reduction** converts a **nitrile (R–C≡N) into an aldehyde (R–CHO)**. The nitrile is reduced by **stannous chloride (SnCl₂) and hydrochloric acid** to an **imine** salt, R–CH=NH·HCl, and this is then **hydrolysed with water** to the aldehyde, releasing ammonia. It was reported by the British chemist Henry Stephen in 1925, and NCERT describes it as a standard method for preparing aldehydes from nitriles.

## General reaction

<Eq ce="R-C#N ->[SnCl2, HCl] R-CH=NH ->[H3O+] R-CHO + NH3" />

In acid the ammonia is not set free as a gas; it stays in solution as ammonium chloride.

## Mechanism

1. **Activation by HCl.** Hydrogen chloride adds to the nitrile to give a salt, which is much more easily reduced than the neutral nitrile.
2. **Reduction by SnCl₂.** Tin(II) transfers electrons (it is oxidised to tin(IV)) and, with protons from HCl, the C≡N carbon receives one hydrogen. The product is the iminium salt R–CH=NH₂⁺ Cl⁻.
3. **Precipitation.** The iminium salt separates as a complex with tin chloride (an aldimine stannichloride). Because it is out of solution, it is not reduced further to the amine.
4. **Hydrolysis.** Water adds to the C=N, giving a hemiaminal that loses ammonia to form the aldehyde.

<Eq ce="R-CH=NH2+ + H2O -> R-CHO + NH4+" />

<KeyPoint>
Stephen reduction adds only **two** hydrogens to the nitrile (to the imine level). Reagents such as LiAlH₄, H₂/Ni or Na/ethanol add four hydrogens and give the primary amine R–CH₂–NH₂ instead.
</KeyPoint>

## Examples

**1. Benzonitrile to benzaldehyde.**

<Eq ce="C6H5CN ->[SnCl2, HCl] C6H5CH=NH ->[H3O+] C6H5CHO + NH3" />

**2. Ethanenitrile to ethanal.**

<Eq ce="CH3CN ->[SnCl2, HCl] CH3CH=NH ->[H3O+] CH3CHO + NH3" />

**3. Step-up sequence.** An alkyl halide can be converted to an aldehyde with one more carbon: KCN gives the nitrile, and Stephen reduction gives the aldehyde.

<Eq ce="CH3CH2Br ->[alc. KCN] CH3CH2CN ->[1. SnCl2, HCl][2. H3O+] CH3CH2CHO" />

## What different reagents do to a nitrile

| Reagent | Product | Name |
|---|---|---|
| SnCl₂ + HCl, then H₃O⁺ | R–CHO | Stephen reduction |
| DIBAL-H, then H₂O | R–CHO | DIBAL-H reduction |
| Na + C₂H₅OH | R–CH₂–NH₂ | Mendius reduction |
| LiAlH₄, or H₂/Ni | R–CH₂–NH₂ | complete reduction |
| H₃O⁺, heat (no reductant) | R–COOH | hydrolysis |

<ExamTip>
Read the reagent carefully: "SnCl₂/HCl then H₂O" on a nitrile means aldehyde; "Sn/HCl" (tin metal) on a nitro compound means amine. The two are easy to confuse.
</ExamTip>

## Limitations

- **Aromatic nitriles work better** than aliphatic nitriles, which often give lower yields.
- **Substituent effects.** Electron-donating groups on an aryl nitrile favour formation of the tin complex; strongly electron-withdrawing groups favour side products, and some substituted aryl nitriles give poor yields.
- **Two separate steps.** The imine salt is formed first; water is added only for the final hydrolysis, which can be done directly in the reaction solution.
