## What is the Étard reaction?

The **Étard reaction** is the **oxidation of a methyl group on an aromatic ring to an aldehyde (–CHO)** using **chromyl chloride, CrO₂Cl₂**. Toluene in an inert solvent such as **CS₂** reacts with chromyl chloride to give a solid chromium complex, the **Étard complex**, which on **hydrolysis gives benzaldehyde**. Because the methyl carbon is trapped in the complex, the oxidation stops at the aldehyde stage instead of going on to benzoic acid. It is named after the French chemist Alexandre Léon Étard.

## General reaction

<Eq ce="Ar-CH3 + 2 CrO2Cl2 ->[CS2] Ar-CH(OCrOHCl2)2 ->[H3O+] Ar-CHO" />

## Mechanism

1. **Complex formation.** Chromyl chloride reacts with the benzylic C–H bonds of the methyl group. The modern description is an **ene-type reaction** between CrO₂Cl₂ and the benzylic hydrogen, repeated so that two chromium units end up on the same carbon. The solid that precipitates is the Étard complex, written for exams as **C₆H₅CH(OCrOHCl₂)₂**.
2. **Decomposition.** The complex is filtered off and decomposed. It undergoes a rearrangement that frees the C=O group and leaves reduced chromium behind, giving benzaldehyde.

<Eq ce="C6H5CH3 + 2 CrO2Cl2 ->[CS2] C6H5CH(OCrOHCl2)2" />
<Eq ce="C6H5CH(OCrOHCl2)2 ->[H3O+] C6H5CHO" />

<KeyPoint>
**Exam answer versus lab practice.** For JEE and NEET, write "hydrolysis with H₃O⁺". In the laboratory the complex is decomposed under **reducing conditions**, usually with saturated aqueous **sodium sulphite**, so that the freshly released benzaldehyde is not oxidised further by leftover Cr(VI). The common solvent in practice is CCl₄; CS₂, CHCl₃ and CH₂Cl₂ are also used.
</KeyPoint>

## Examples

**1. Benzaldehyde from toluene (NCERT example).**

<Eq ce="C6H5CH3 ->[1) CrO2Cl2, CS2][2) H3O+] C6H5CHO" />

**2. A longer side chain does not give a clean aldehyde.** n-Propylbenzene gives a mixture: benzyl methyl ketone (major), propiophenone and chlorinated products. For this reason the Étard reaction is used almost only for converting an aromatic CH₃ group into CHO.

## Ways to oxidise toluene

| Reagent | Product from toluene | Stops at aldehyde? |
|---|---|---|
| CrO₂Cl₂ in CS₂, then H₃O⁺ (Étard) | benzaldehyde | yes |
| CrO₃ in acetic anhydride, then aq. acid | benzylidene diacetate → benzaldehyde | yes |
| Cl₂/hν (side chain), then H₂O | benzal chloride → benzaldehyde | yes |
| hot alkaline KMnO₄, then H⁺ | benzoic acid | no |
| acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ | benzoic acid | no |

<ExamTip>
"Toluene to benzaldehyde in one named step" means **Étard reaction** (CrO₂Cl₂). "Benzene to benzaldehyde in one named step" means **Gattermann–Koch** (CO, HCl, AlCl₃/CuCl). Strong oxidants such as KMnO₄ always give benzoic acid from toluene.
</ExamTip>

## Limitations and exceptions

- **Mainly for aromatic methyl groups.** Longer alkyl chains rearrange and give ketone mixtures and chlorinated by-products.
- **Slow and hazardous.** The reaction may take days to weeks, and chromyl chloride is a toxic, aggressive Cr(VI) oxidant that also attacks alkenes and alkanes.
- **Unreacted reagent must be removed.** The Étard complex is usually purified before decomposition, otherwise leftover CrO₂Cl₂ can over-oxidise the product.
