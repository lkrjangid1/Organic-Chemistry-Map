## What is the Robinson annulation?

The **Robinson annulation** is a ring-forming reaction that adds a **new six-membered ring** carrying an α,β-unsaturated ketone (a cyclohex-2-enone) onto a ketone. It combines two reactions in sequence: a **Michael addition** of a ketone enolate to an α,β-unsaturated ketone, usually **methyl vinyl ketone (MVK)**, followed by an **intramolecular aldol condensation**. Robert Robinson reported it in 1935, and it is a key route to fused rings in steroids and terpenes. *Annulus* is Latin for "ring".

## Mechanism

**Stage 1: Michael addition (1,4-addition)**

1. OH⁻ removes an α-H from cyclohexanone to give its enolate.
2. The enolate carbon attacks the **β-CH₂** of CH₂=CH–CO–CH₃ (conjugate addition), giving a new enolate on the MVK part.
3. Proton transfer (tautomerisation) gives the **1,5-diketone** 2-(3-oxobutyl)cyclohexanone.

<Eq ce="cyclohexanone + CH2=CHCOCH3 ->[OH-] 2-(3-oxobutyl)cyclohexanone" />

**Stage 2: Intramolecular aldol condensation**

4. OH⁻ removes an H from the terminal CH₃ of the side-chain ketone, giving ⁻CH₂–CO–.
5. This carbanion attacks the ring C=O, closing a six-membered ring and giving a bicyclic β-hydroxy ketone (keto alcohol).
6. Dehydration (E1cB, on warming) gives the conjugated enone: **Δ¹,⁹-octal-2-one**, with the C=C at the ring fusion.

<Eq ce="2-(3-oxobutyl)cyclohexanone ->[OH-] bicyclic keto alcohol ->[\Delta][-H2O] octalone" />

<KeyPoint>
In the new ring, the four carbons of methyl vinyl ketone's CH₂=CH–CO–CH₃ unit make a four-carbon bridge, and two carbons of the original ketone (its C=O carbon and its α-carbon) complete the six-membered ring. The new C=C always ends up conjugated with the new ring C=O.
</KeyPoint>

## Examples

| Donor (enolate source) | Acceptor | Annulation product |
|---|---|---|
| Cyclohexanone | Methyl vinyl ketone | Δ¹,⁹-Octal-2-one (bicyclic enone) |
| 2-Methylcyclohexanone | Methyl vinyl ketone | Methyl-substituted octalone (angular CH₃ at the ring fusion) |
| 2-Methylcyclohexane-1,3-dione | Methyl vinyl ketone | Wieland–Miescher ketone (steroid building block) |
| 2-Methylcyclopentane-1,3-dione | Aryl-substituted enone | Ring C/D unit used in a synthesis of estrone |

With a β-diketone or β-keto ester donor, deprotonation happens at the CH flanked by two carbonyl groups. This fixes where the Michael addition takes place.

<ExamTip>
To work backwards from a cyclohexenone product, first undo the aldol: break the C=C (α,β-bond) and restore the C=O on the β-carbon. This gives a 1,5-diketone. Then undo the Michael addition at the bond between the α-carbon of the original ketone and the CH₂ that came from MVK.
</ExamTip>

## Conditions and practical points

- **Base or acid:** the reaction is usually run with base (NaOH, KOH, NaOEt), but acid (H₂SO₄) gives similar results. The Michael step can also run under neutral conditions through an enamine (compare the [Stork enamine reaction](/reactions/stork-enamine-reaction/)).
- **Two separate steps:** the Michael adduct is often isolated first and then cyclised in a separate step. This stops the starting enolate from reacting with the enone product.
- **MVK polymerises easily.** A precursor such as a β-chloroketone or a Mannich base can be used, which releases the enone slowly.
- **Asymmetric versions:** proline and other chiral amines catalyse an enantioselective annulation, for example to make the Wieland–Miescher ketone in enantiopure form.

## Limitations

- Unsymmetrical ketones can form two different enolates, so the Michael step can occur on either side. The thermodynamic enolate usually reacts under equilibrating conditions.
- The enone product is itself a Michael acceptor, so side reactions with excess enolate can occur.
