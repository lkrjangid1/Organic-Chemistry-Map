## What is the Gattermann reaction?

The **Gattermann reaction** converts an **arenediazonium salt** into an **aryl chloride or aryl bromide** by warming the diazonium salt solution with the corresponding **halogen acid (HCl or HBr) in the presence of copper powder**. Benzenediazonium chloride gives chlorobenzene with Cu/HCl and bromobenzene with Cu/HBr, with loss of nitrogen gas.

Ludwig Gattermann developed it as an improved Sandmeyer reaction that uses **metallic copper** in place of a copper(I) salt.

<KeyPoint>
Do not confuse it with the Gattermann–Koch reaction or the Gattermann formylation. Those make aldehydes from arenes; this one makes aryl halides from diazonium salts.
</KeyPoint>

## General reaction

<Eq ce="Ar-N2^+ Cl^- ->[Cu, HX] Ar-X + N2 ^ \quad (X = Cl, Br)" />

In exam answers this branch is written as **Cu + HX, X → Cl, Br**, as in NCERT.

## Mechanism

1. **Copper(I) forms in situ.** Copper metal reduces some diazonium ion, producing Cu⁺ (and some biaryl as a side product).
2. **Electron transfer and loss of N₂.** Cu⁺ passes one electron to ArN₂⁺; the radical loses nitrogen to give an aryl radical.
3. **Halogen transfer.** The aryl radical takes a halogen from the copper(II) halide, forming Ar–X and regenerating Cu⁺.

<Eq ce="2Cu + 2ArN2^+ -> 2Cu^+ + Ar-Ar + 2N2" />

<Eq ce="ArN2^+ + HX ->[Cu^+] ArX + N2 + H^+" />

So, once started, the Gattermann reaction runs on the same Cu(I) chemistry as the [Sandmeyer reaction](/reactions/sandmeyer-reaction/). Do not draw the single aryl-cation mechanism here: copper-mediated halogenation follows this radical pathway instead.

## Examples

**1. Chlorobenzene:**

<Eq ce="C6H5N2^+ Cl^- ->[Cu/HCl] C6H5Cl + N2 ^" />

**2. Bromobenzene:**

<Eq ce="C6H5N2^+ Cl^- ->[Cu/HBr] C6H5Br + N2 ^" />

**3. From p-toluidine to p-bromotoluene (two steps):**

<Eq ce="CH3C6H4NH2 ->[NaNO2, HCl][273-278 K] CH3C6H4N2^+ Cl^- ->[Cu/HBr] CH3C6H4Br + N2" />

<ExamTip>
When a question shows "Cu, HCl" or "Cu/HBr" on a diazonium salt, name it **Gattermann**; when it shows "CuCl/HCl", "Cu₂Cl₂/HCl" or "CuCN", name it **Sandmeyer**. NCERT asks which gives the better yield: Sandmeyer.
</ExamTip>

## Gattermann vs Sandmeyer

| Feature | Gattermann | Sandmeyer |
|---|---|---|
| Copper source | Cu powder | CuCl, CuBr, CuCN |
| Groups introduced | Cl, Br | Cl, Br, CN |
| Yield | Lower | Higher |
| Key intermediate | Aryl radical (via Cu⁺ formed in situ) | Aryl radical |

## Where it fits in a synthesis

The Gattermann reaction is normally the last step of a three-step route from an arene: nitration, reduction of –NO₂ to –NH₂, then diazotization and replacement. Because the amino group decides the orientation before it is removed, the halogen ends up at a position that direct halogenation might not give. In questions, both the Gattermann and Sandmeyer options lead to the same aryl halide; only the reagent label and the yield differ.

## Limitations and exceptions

- Only chlorine and bromine are introduced; cyanide, iodide and fluoride need other methods.
- Yields are generally lower than with Sandmeyer conditions, partly because biaryl forms when copper metal reduces the diazonium ion.
- The diazonium salt must be made and used in the cold; warming in water alone gives phenol.
