## What is the Claisen rearrangement?

The **Claisen rearrangement** is a **[3,3]-sigmatropic rearrangement** of an allyl ether that needs only **heat**. In the **aromatic** version, **allyl phenyl ether** rearranges to **2-allylphenol (o-allylphenol)**: the allyl group moves from oxygen to the ortho carbon of the ring. In the **aliphatic** version, an **allyl vinyl ether** gives a **γ,δ-unsaturated carbonyl compound**. Reported by Ludwig Claisen in 1912, it was the first known [3,3]-sigmatropic rearrangement.

## General reaction

<Eq ce="C6H5-O-CH2-CH=CH2 ->[\Delta] 2-(CH2=CH-CH2)C6H4-OH" />

<Eq ce="CH2=CH-O-CH2-CH=CH2 ->[\Delta] CH2=CH-CH2-CH2-CHO" />

A new C–C bond forms and the C–O bond breaks. The aliphatic reaction is driven by forming the strong C=O bond; the aromatic reaction is driven by getting back the aromatic ring.

## Mechanism

Number the six atoms of the system: the allyl CH₂=CH–CH₂ is carbons 1, 2, 3 (C-1 bonded to oxygen), and O–C(ring)=C(ortho) is the other set of 1, 2, 3.

1. **Cyclic transition state.** On heating, the six atoms arrange in a ring, preferably a chair-like transition state.
2. **Concerted [3,3] shift.** In one step, the O–CH₂ bond (between the two "1" atoms) breaks, a new bond forms between the two "3" atoms (the far CH₂ of the allyl group and the ortho carbon), and the π bonds move over.
3. **Dienone intermediate.** The product of the shift is 6-allylcyclohexa-2,4-dien-1-one, which is not aromatic.
4. **Tautomerisation.** The hydrogen on the sp³ ortho carbon moves to oxygen (keto → enol). The ring becomes aromatic again, giving **o-allylphenol**.

<KeyPoint>
Because the new bond forms at the **far end** of the allyl group, the allyl unit is attached to the ring **upside down** (transposed). If the CH₂ at the end of the double bond is labelled (for example with ¹⁴C), the label ends up bonded to the ring at the ortho position.
</KeyPoint>

**When both ortho positions are blocked**, the dienone cannot tautomerise. The allyl group then undergoes a second [3,3] shift (a Cope rearrangement) from the ortho carbon to the para carbon. The second inversion restores the original orientation, and tautomerisation gives the **p-allylphenol**.

<Trick>
**Ortho pe Ulta or Para pe Sedha**: at ortho the allyl group sits inverted (one [3,3] shift); at para it sits the right way round (two shifts, two inversions).
</Trick>

## Examples

**1. Allyl phenyl ether → 2-allylphenol:**

<Eq ce="C6H5OCH2CH=CH2 ->[\Delta] 2-(CH2=CHCH2)C6H4OH" />

**2. Ortho positions blocked (para product):** allyl 2,6-dimethylphenyl ether gives 4-allyl-2,6-dimethylphenol.

<Eq ce="2,6-(CH3)2C6H3OCH2CH=CH2 ->[\Delta] 4-(CH2=CHCH2)-2,6-(CH3)2C6H2OH" />

**3. Aliphatic Claisen:** allyl vinyl ether gives pent-4-enal, a γ,δ-unsaturated aldehyde.

<Eq ce="CH2=CHOCH2CH=CH2 ->[\Delta] CH2=CHCH2CH2CHO" />

<ExamTip title="Common mistake">
o-Allylphenol does not go on to rearrange into p-allylphenol. The para product appears only when both ortho positions already carry substituents. With a free ortho position, the ortho product is formed and the reaction stops there.
</ExamTip>

<ExamTip>
To draw the product fast: number O as 1 and count three atoms each way. Break bond 1–1, make bond 3–3, shift the double bonds, then tautomerise if a phenol can form. The allyl ether is usually made first by [Williamson ether synthesis](/reactions/williamson-ether-synthesis/) from a phenoxide and allyl bromide.
</ExamTip>

| Type | [3,3]-sigmatropic, pericyclic |
| Molecularity | Intramolecular (no crossover products) |

| Kinetics | First order |
| Stereochemistry | Suprafacial, stereospecific |

## Limitations and variations

- Uncatalysed reactions need temperatures above 100 °C. Polar and hydrogen-bonding solvents speed up the reaction.
- If an –CHO or –COOH group occupies the ortho or para position the allyl group moves to, that group can be pushed out as CO or CO₂.
- Named variants (Ireland–, Johnson– and Eschenmoser–Claisen) start from allylic esters or allylic alcohols and give γ,δ-unsaturated acids, esters and amides under milder conditions.
- In nature, the enzyme chorismate mutase carries out a Claisen rearrangement of chorismate to prephenate on the way to phenylalanine and tyrosine.
