## What is the Meerwein–Ponndorf–Verley reduction?

The **Meerwein–Ponndorf–Verley (MPV) reduction** converts an **aldehyde into a primary alcohol** or a **ketone into a secondary alcohol** using **aluminium isopropoxide** in **isopropanol (propan-2-ol)**. A hydride is transferred from the isopropoxide group to the carbonyl carbon, and the isopropanol is oxidised to acetone. Only the C=O group is reduced: **C=C and C≡C bonds survive**.

The reaction was found independently by Albert Verley and by Hans Meerwein with Rudolf Schmidt in 1925; Wolfgang Ponndorf introduced aluminium isopropoxide in isopropanol as the reagent system in 1926.

## General reaction

<Eq ce="R-CO-R' + (CH3)2CHOH <=>[Al(O-iPr)3] R-CH(OH)-R' + CH3COCH3" />

Here Al(O-iPr)₃ stands for Al[OCH(CH₃)₂]₃. The reaction is an equilibrium; acetone (b.p. 56 °C) is distilled out of the flask as it forms, which pulls the reaction towards the alcohol.

## Mechanism

1. **Coordination.** The carbonyl oxygen of the substrate binds to the Lewis-acidic aluminium of Al(O-iPr)₃.
2. **Hydride transfer through a six-membered ring.** Aluminium, the isopropoxide oxygen, its α-carbon and hydrogen, and the substrate's C=O form a cyclic six-membered transition state. The hydride moves from the isopropoxide α-carbon to the carbonyl carbon in one concerted step.
3. **Release of acetone.** The isopropoxide ligand, having lost its hydride, leaves as acetone; the substrate is now an aluminium alkoxide.
4. **Alkoxide exchange.** Isopropanol swaps with the new alkoxide on aluminium, freeing the product alcohol and regenerating aluminium isopropoxide.

<KeyPoint>
No free hydride ion and no metal hydride are involved: the hydride comes from the **C–H of isopropanol**. That is why the reaction is so selective for C=O.
</KeyPoint>

## Examples

1. **Ketone to secondary alcohol.**
<Eq ce="C6H10O ->[Al(O-iPr)3][(CH3)2CHOH] C6H11OH" />
Cyclohexanone gives cyclohexanol.

2. **Unsaturated aldehyde: C=C is kept.**
<Eq ce="CH3CH=CHCHO ->[Al(O-iPr)3][(CH3)2CHOH] CH3CH=CHCH2OH" />
But-2-enal gives but-2-en-1-ol; the double bond is not touched.

3. **Aldehyde in the presence of a ketone.** Aldehydes are reduced faster than ketones, so careful control can reduce the aldehyde group first.

## Chemoselectivity: what MPV does and does not reduce

| Group | MPV result |
|---|---|
| Aldehyde | 1° alcohol |
| Ketone | 2° alcohol |
| C=C, C≡C | No reaction |
| Other groups (esters, nitro, halides) | Not reduced under these mild conditions |

<ExamTip>
If a question asks for "reduction of only the carbonyl group of an unsaturated aldehyde or ketone", MPV (Al(O-iPr)₃ / isopropanol) and [NaBH₄](/reagents/sodium-borohydride/) are the safe answers. [LiAlH₄](/reagents/lithium-aluminium-hydride/) in excess may also attack a C=C conjugated with C=O.
</ExamTip>

## Relation to the Oppenauer oxidation

The [Oppenauer oxidation](/reactions/oppenauer-oxidation/) is the **reverse** of the MPV reduction. With aluminium isopropoxide and a large excess of **acetone**, a secondary alcohol gives up its hydride to acetone and becomes a ketone. Which product forms depends only on which side of the equilibrium is pushed:

| | MPV reduction | Oppenauer oxidation |
|---|---|---|
| Substrate | Aldehyde or ketone | Secondary alcohol |
| Hydride donor | Isopropanol | Substrate alcohol |
| Hydride acceptor | Substrate C=O | Acetone (excess) |
| Driving the equilibrium | Distil off acetone | Excess acetone |
| Product | Alcohol | Ketone |

## Limitations and side reactions

- **Aldol condensation** can compete for aldehydes or ketones with α-hydrogens, because aluminium alkoxides are basic (see [aldol condensation](/reactions/aldol-condensation/)).
- **Aldehydes with no α-hydrogen** can undergo the [Tishchenko reaction](/reactions/tishchenko-reaction/), giving an ester instead of the alcohol.
- Being an equilibrium, the reaction needs acetone removal or a large excess of isopropanol for good yields.
