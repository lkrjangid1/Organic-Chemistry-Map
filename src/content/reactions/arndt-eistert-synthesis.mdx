## What is the Arndt–Eistert synthesis?

The **Arndt–Eistert synthesis** is a **one-carbon homologation of carboxylic acids**: it converts an acid **R–COOH into R–CH₂–COOH**. The acid is turned into its **acid chloride**, the acid chloride reacts with **diazomethane** to give an **α-diazoketone**, and the diazoketone undergoes the silver-catalysed **Wolff rearrangement** to a **ketene**, which water converts into the higher acid. It was introduced by Fritz Arndt and Bernd Eistert in 1935.

<Eq ce="R-COOH ->[SOCl2] R-COCl ->[CH2N2] R-CO-CHN2 ->[Ag2O, H2O][\Delta] R-CH2-COOH + N2" />

## Mechanism

1. **Acid chloride.** SOCl₂ (or PCl₅) converts R–COOH into R–COCl.
2. **Acylation of diazomethane.** The nucleophilic carbon of CH₂N₂ (best written ⁻CH₂–N⁺≡N) attacks the acyl carbon and Cl⁻ leaves, giving R–CO–CH₂–N₂⁺.
3. **Diazoketone.** Loss of H⁺ gives the neutral α-diazoketone R–CO–CH=N₂. The proton ends up as HCl, which a **second equivalent of diazomethane** removes as CH₃Cl + N₂. Most diazoketones are stable enough to isolate.
4. **Wolff rearrangement.** With Ag₂O (or silver benzoate), heat or UV light, N₂ leaves and the R group **migrates from the carbonyl carbon to the neighbouring carbon** in a 1,2-shift. The product is a **ketene**, R–CH=C=O.
5. **Trapping the ketene.** Water adds across the C=C of the ketene to give R–CH₂–COOH.

<Eq ce="R-CO-CHN2 ->[Ag2O] R-CH=C=O + N2" />
<Eq ce="R-CH=C=O + H2O -> R-CH2-COOH" />

<KeyPoint>
The **Wolff rearrangement** (diazoketone → ketene) is the key step. It may be concerted or go through an α-ketocarbene; either way the migrating group keeps its configuration and the new carbon from CH₂N₂ ends up next to the COOH.
</KeyPoint>

## Examples

**1. Acetic acid to propanoic acid**

<Eq ce="CH3COOH ->[SOCl2] CH3COCl ->[CH2N2] CH3COCHN2 ->[Ag2O, H2O] CH3CH2COOH" />

**2. Butanoic acid to pentanoic acid**

<Eq ce="CH3CH2CH2COCl + 2 CH2N2 -> CH3CH2CH2COCHN2 + CH3Cl + N2" />
<Eq ce="CH3CH2CH2COCHN2 ->[Ag2O, H2O][\Delta] CH3CH2CH2CH2COOH + N2" />

**3. Trapping with other nucleophiles**

<Eq ce="C6H5CH=C=O + C2H5OH -> C6H5CH2COOC2H5" />

Benzoic acid gives phenylacetic acid with water, ethyl phenylacetate with ethanol, and phenylacetamide with ammonia.

<ExamTip title="Common mistake">
Do not "step up" acetic acid by making BrCH₂COOH (HVZ) and then adding CH₃MgBr. A Grignard reagent reacts first with the acidic COOH proton (giving methane), so no C–C bond forms. For adding one carbon to a carboxylic acid, the Arndt–Eistert sequence is the answer.
</ExamTip>

## Ascent and descent of carboxylic acids

| Change | Reaction | Product from RCOOH |
|---|---|---|
| Add one carbon | Arndt–Eistert | RCH₂COOH |
| Remove one carbon, give halide | [Hunsdiecker](/reactions/hunsdiecker-reaction/) | RX |
| Remove one carbon, give amine | [Hofmann](/reactions/hofmann-bromamide-degradation/), [Curtius](/reactions/curtius-rearrangement/), [Schmidt](/reactions/schmidt-reaction/) | RNH₂ |
| Remove CO₂, give alkane | [Decarboxylation](/reactions/decarboxylation/) | RH |

## Limitations

- **Diazomethane is toxic and explosive**; safer reagents such as trimethylsilyldiazomethane are used in modern work.
- If too little diazomethane is used, HCl converts the diazoketone into R–CO–CH₂Cl.
- Higher diazoalkanes (R′CHN₂) are less reliable and give side products.
- The thermal Wolff rearrangement needs high temperatures (around 180 °C); silver catalysis or light is preferred.
