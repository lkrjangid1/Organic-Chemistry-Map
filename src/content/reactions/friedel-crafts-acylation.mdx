## What is Friedel–Crafts acylation?

**Friedel–Crafts acylation** is the electrophilic aromatic substitution in which an **acyl group (R–CO–) replaces a ring hydrogen**. An arene is treated with an **acyl chloride** or an **acid anhydride** in the presence of **anhydrous AlCl₃**, and the product is an **aryl ketone**. The classic example is benzene with acetyl chloride giving **acetophenone**. The electrophile is the resonance-stabilised **acylium ion**.

## General reaction

<Eq ce="Ar-H + R-CO-Cl ->[anhyd. AlCl3] Ar-CO-R + HCl" />
<Eq ce="Ar-H + (RCO)2O ->[anhyd. AlCl3] Ar-CO-R + RCOOH" />

## Mechanism

1. **Acylium ion formation.** AlCl₃ accepts chloride from the acyl chloride. The resulting cation is stabilised by the lone pair on oxygen, so it is best written as a resonance hybrid.

<Eq ce="CH3-CO-Cl + AlCl3 -> CH3-C#O+ + AlCl4-" />

2. **Attack by the ring.** The π electrons of benzene attack the carbon of the acylium ion, giving a resonance-stabilised arenium ion (σ-complex). This step breaks aromaticity and is the slow step.
3. **Deprotonation.** AlCl₄⁻ removes the ring proton, aromaticity is restored and HCl is released.
4. **Complexation and work-up.** The ketone oxygen binds AlCl₃. Adding water at the end breaks this complex and releases the free ketone.

<KeyPoint>
The acylium ion is drawn as R–C≡O⁺ ↔ R–C⁺=O. Because the positive charge is shared with oxygen, the acylium ion is far more stable than an ordinary primary carbocation and **does not rearrange** (the pivaloyl case below, which loses CO, is the exception to remember).
</KeyPoint>

## Examples

**1. Acetophenone from benzene (NCERT example).**

<Eq ce="C6H6 + CH3COCl ->[anhyd. AlCl3] C6H5COCH3 + HCl" />

**2. Acylation of an activated ring.** The –OCH₃ group of anisole is an ortho/para director, and the bulky acyl group goes mainly para, giving 4-methoxyacetophenone.

<Eq ce="C6H5OCH3 + CH3COCl ->[AlCl3] p-CH3OC6H4COCH3 + HCl" />

**3. Heterocycles and ring closure.** Thiophene reacts with acetyl chloride under milder SnCl₄ catalysis to give 2-acetylthiophene; furan is acylated by acetic anhydride to give 2-acetylfuran. In the intramolecular version, 4-phenylbutanoic acid cyclises with polyphosphoric acid or methanesulfonic acid to α-tetralone (3,4-dihydronaphthalen-1(2H)-one).

<Eq ce="C6H5(CH2)3COOH ->[PPA][\Delta] C10H10O + H2O" />

## Exception: pivaloyl chloride gives alkylation

The one well-known case where an "acylation" gives an alkylbenzene uses **pivaloyl chloride**, (CH₃)₃C–COCl (2,2-dimethylpropanoyl chloride). Its acylium ion, (CH₃)₃C–C≡O⁺, is in equilibrium with the **tert-butyl cation and carbon monoxide**, because the tertiary carbocation is unusually stable. The tert-butyl cation then alkylates the ring, so in exam answers the product of benzene with pivaloyl chloride and AlCl₃ is **tert-butylbenzene**, not the ketone.

<Eq ce="(CH3)3C-C#O+ <=> (CH3)3C+ + CO" />
<Eq ce="C6H6 + (CH3)3C-COCl ->[AlCl3] C6H5-C(CH3)3 + CO + HCl" />

<ExamTip>
Before writing a ketone for a Friedel–Crafts acylation, look at the carbon next to C=O. If it is tertiary (as in pivaloyl chloride), loss of CO gives a stable tertiary carbocation, and the answer is the alkylation product.
</ExamTip>

## Acylation versus alkylation

| Feature | Alkylation | Acylation |
|---|---|---|
| Electrophile | carbocation R⁺ | acylium ion RCO⁺ |
| Rearrangement | common with primary halides | none |
| Further substitution | polyalkylation (product activated) | stops at one (product deactivated) |
| AlCl₃ needed | catalytic | at least one equivalent |

<ExamTip>
For a straight-chain alkylbenzene, the standard two-step answer is Friedel–Crafts acylation followed by reduction of the C=O to CH₂ (Clemmensen: Zn-Hg/HCl, or Wolff–Kishner: NH₂NH₂/KOH). This avoids both rearrangement and polysubstitution.
</ExamTip>

## Limitations and exceptions

- **Deactivated rings fail.** Nitrobenzene and other rings carrying strong meta-directing deactivators do not undergo acylation.
- **Amino groups block the reaction.** Aniline and other basic amines bind AlCl₃ through the nitrogen lone pair, which deactivates the ring.
- **Stoichiometric Lewis acid.** Both the acyl chloride and the ketone product complex the AlCl₃, so it is consumed. With electron-rich rings, milder catalysts such as zinc(II) salts can be used in smaller amounts.
- **Formyl group.** To put a –CHO group on benzene, the related Gattermann–Koch reaction (CO and HCl with anhydrous AlCl₃) is used, not an ordinary acyl chloride.
