## What is the Gattermann aldehyde synthesis?

The **Gattermann aldehyde synthesis** (Gattermann formylation) introduces a **–CHO group into an aromatic ring** using **hydrogen cyanide and hydrogen chloride** with a **Lewis acid catalyst such as anhydrous AlCl₃**. The first product is an **aldimine** (Ar–CH=NH, as its hydrochloride), which is **hydrolysed by water or dilute acid** to the aldehyde and ammonia. Benzene gives **benzaldehyde**. It is an electrophilic aromatic substitution, named after the German chemist Ludwig Gattermann, and is similar in spirit to Friedel–Crafts acylation.

## General reaction

<Eq ce="Ar-H + HCN + HCl ->[AlCl3] Ar-CH=NH2+ Cl- ->[H2O] Ar-CHO + NH4Cl" />

## Mechanism

1. **Making the electrophile.** HCl protonates the nitrogen of HCN, and the Lewis acid assists. In exam answers the electrophile is written as the iminium-type cation **H–C⁺=NH** (from H–C≡N⁺–H).

<Eq ce="H-C#N + HCl -> H-C#N+-H Cl- <-> H-C+=NH" />

2. **Attack by the ring.** Benzene's π electrons attack the electrophilic carbon. The arenium ion formed loses H⁺, and aromaticity returns.
3. **Aldimine formation.** The product of substitution is the aldimine C₆H₅–CH=NH, held as its hydrochloride salt C₆H₅CH=NH₂⁺Cl⁻ in the acidic mixture.
4. **Hydrolysis.** On adding water (H₃O⁺), water attacks the C=N carbon, nitrogen is protonated and leaves as ammonia, and the C=O bond forms. This is the same hydrolysis that converts any imine back to its carbonyl compound.

<Eq ce="C6H5CH=NH + H2O ->[H3O+] C6H5CHO + NH3" />

<KeyPoint>
The Gattermann synthesis is the hydrogen cyanide version of the **Houben–Hoesch reaction**. Hoesch uses a nitrile R–C≡N and gives an aryl **ketone**; Gattermann uses H–C≡N and gives an aryl **aldehyde**. In both, an imine is formed first and hydrolysed during work-up.
</KeyPoint>

## Examples

**1. Benzaldehyde from benzene.**

<Eq ce="C6H6 ->[1) HCN, HCl, AlCl3][2) H3O+] C6H5CHO" />

**2. Zinc cyanide modification.** HCN is a very toxic gas, so the reaction is often run with solid Zn(CN)₂ and HCl. These react in the flask to give HCN and ZnCl₂, and the ZnCl₂ serves as the Lewis acid. Mesitylene (1,3,5-trimethylbenzene) is formylated this way to mesitaldehyde (2,4,6-trimethylbenzaldehyde).

<Eq ce="C6H3(CH3)3 ->[1) Zn(CN)2, HCl][2) H2O] (CH3)3C6H2CHO" />

**3. Phenols and phenol ethers.** Electron-rich rings such as phenols and their ethers respond well, which is why the reaction is also called the Gattermann *salicylaldehyde* (hydroxy aldehyde) synthesis. This is the main practical advantage over the Gattermann–Koch method.

## Three "Gattermann" reactions compared

| Name | Substrate | Reagents | Product |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gattermann aldehyde synthesis | arene, phenol, phenol ether | HCN + HCl, AlCl₃; then H₂O | Ar–CHO |
| Gattermann–Koch reaction | benzene, alkylbenzene | CO + HCl, AlCl₃/CuCl | Ar–CHO |
| Gattermann reaction | arenediazonium salt | Cu powder + HCl or HBr | Ar–Cl or Ar–Br |

<ExamTip>
If the question shows **HCN/HCl** on the arrow and asks for the product, answer an aldehyde (via the aldimine, then hydrolysis). If it shows **Cu/HCl on a diazonium salt**, answer an aryl chloride. Only the formylation reactions are electrophilic aromatic substitutions.
</ExamTip>

<ExamTip title="Common mistake">
The by-product of the hydrolysis step is ammonia, but in the acidic mixture it is present as the ammonium ion (NH₄⁺/NH₄Cl), not as free NH₃ gas. Also, a Lewis acid catalyst is needed for benzene itself; HCN and HCl alone do not formylate benzene.
</ExamTip>

## Limitations and exceptions

- **HCN is extremely toxic**; the Zn(CN)₂ method (or other cyanide sources) is preferred in practice.
- **Deactivated rings** (nitrobenzene, benzonitrile) do not react, as in other Friedel–Crafts-type substitutions.
- **Anhydrous first step.** Water must be kept out until the aldimine salt has formed; it is added only for the hydrolysis.
