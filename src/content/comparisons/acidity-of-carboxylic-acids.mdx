## What decides the acidity of carboxylic acids?

A carboxylic acid ionises to a carboxylate ion whose negative charge is shared equally by two oxygens. **Anything that stabilises this carboxylate ion makes the acid stronger.** Electron-withdrawing groups (–I or –R) spread the charge and raise acidity; electron-donating groups (+I, +R) concentrate it and lower acidity. The effect is strongest when the group is close to the COOH.

<KeyPoint title="Orders to remember">
- **HCOOH > C₆H₅COOH > CH₃COOH > CH₃CH₂COOH**
- **CF₃COOH > CCl₃COOH > CHCl₂COOH > CH₂ClCOOH > CH₃COOH**
- **FCH₂COOH > ClCH₂COOH ≥ BrCH₂COOH > ICH₂COOH**
- **2-chloro- > 3-chloro- > 4-chlorobutanoic acid > butanoic acid**
- **Oxalic > malonic > succinic > glutaric > adipic acid**
- Most ortho-substituted benzoic acids are stronger than their meta and para isomers.
</KeyPoint>

## Formic, benzoic and aliphatic acids

| Acid | pKa | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| HCOOH | 3.75 | no electron-releasing group |
| C₆H₅COOH | 4.19 | COOH on an sp² carbon, which is more electronegative than sp³ |
| CH₂=CHCOOH | 4.25 | also attached to an sp² carbon |
| C₆H₅CH₂COOH | 4.31 | ring one carbon away, weaker effect |
| CH₃COOH | 4.76 | +I effect of CH₃ |
| CH₃CH₂COOH | 4.87 | slightly larger +I effect |

Beyond propanoic acid, extra CH₂ groups hardly change the value (butanoic acid 4.82), so do not build long "longer chain, weaker acid" orders.

## Halogen and other electron-withdrawing groups

NCERT gives the strength of common substituents in raising acidity as **Ph &lt; I &lt; Br &lt; Cl &lt; F &lt; CN &lt; NO₂ &lt; CF₃**.

| Acid | pKa |
|---|---|
| CF₃COOH | about 0.2 |
| CCl₃COOH | 0.66 |
| CHCl₂COOH | 1.35 |
| NC–CH₂COOH | 2.5 |
| FCH₂COOH | about 2.6 |
| ClCH₂COOH | 2.86 |
| BrCH₂COOH | 2.86 |
| ICH₂COOH | 3.12 |

The inductive effect fades with distance through σ bonds. 2-Chlorobutanoic acid has pKa 2.86, the 3-chloro isomer 4.05 and the 4-chloro isomer 4.52, almost back to butanoic acid. Note that chloroacetic and bromoacetic acids have practically the same pKa; the textbook order Cl > Br follows electronegativity.

## Substituted benzoic acids

| Substituent | ortho | meta | para |
|---|---|---|---|
| H | 4.2 | 4.2 | 4.2 |
| CH₃ | 3.9 | 4.3 | 4.4 |
| Cl | 2.9 | 3.8 | 4.0 |
| OCH₃ | 4.1 | 4.1 | 4.5 |
| NO₂ | 2.2 | 3.5 | 3.4 |
| OH | 2.98 | 4.08 | 4.58 |

- **Para** isomers follow electronic effects: –NO₂, –CN, –Cl raise acidity; –CH₃, –OCH₃, –OH lower it because they donate electrons into the ring.
- **Ortho effect:** an ortho group twists the COOH out of the ring plane, so the ring can no longer donate electrons to it by resonance. Even o-toluic acid (3.9) is stronger than benzoic acid.
- **Salicylic acid** (o-hydroxybenzoic acid, 2.98) is especially strong. In exam answers this is explained by intramolecular hydrogen bonding between the OH and the carboxylate ion, which stabilises the anion.

Resulting orders: toluic acids p &lt; m &lt; o; nitrobenzoic acids m &lt; p &lt; o (m and p are close); hydroxybenzoic acids p &lt; m &lt; o; methoxybenzoic acids p &lt; m ≈ o.

## Dicarboxylic acids

| Acid | Formula | pKa₁ | pKa₂ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oxalic | HOOC–COOH | 1.27 | 4.27 |
| Malonic | HOOC–CH₂–COOH | 2.85 | 5.05 |
| Succinic | HOOC–(CH₂)₂–COOH | 4.21 | 5.41 |
| Glutaric | HOOC–(CH₂)₃–COOH | 4.34 | 5.41 |
| Adipic | HOOC–(CH₂)₄–COOH | 4.41 | 5.41 |

The second COOH acts as an electron-withdrawing group on the first, and its influence falls as the chain between them grows. pKa₂ is always larger than pKa₁ because removing H⁺ from a monoanion creates a dianion with two repelling charges.

<ExamTip title="Common mistake">
Succinic acid is **not** stronger than malonic acid. The first-dissociation order is oxalic > malonic > succinic > glutaric > adipic, matching the distance between the two carboxyl groups.
</ExamTip>

<ExamTip title="Thioacids are stronger">
Replacing the OH of a carboxylic acid by SH increases acidity: thioacetic acid, CH₃COSH, has pKa about 3.4, roughly 15 times stronger than acetic acid. Do not treat RCOSH and RCOOH as equal acids.
</ExamTip>

## Solved examples

**1. Arrange in increasing acidity: CH₃COOH, ClCH₂COOH, Cl₂CHCOOH, HCOOH.**
More Cl, more –I. Formic acid (3.75) lies between acetic and chloroacetic acid.
**Answer:** CH₃COOH &lt; HCOOH &lt; ClCH₂COOH &lt; Cl₂CHCOOH.

**2. Arrange benzoic, p-nitrobenzoic, p-toluic and o-nitrobenzoic acids in increasing acidity.**
**Answer:** p-toluic &lt; benzoic &lt; p-nitrobenzoic &lt; o-nitrobenzoic acid.

**3. Arrange phenylacetic, acetic, benzoic and cyanoacetic acids in decreasing acidity.**
**Answer:** cyanoacetic (2.5) > benzoic (4.19) > phenylacetic (4.31) > acetic (4.76).
