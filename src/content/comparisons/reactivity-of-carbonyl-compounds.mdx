## What decides the reactivity of aldehydes and ketones?

In nucleophilic addition, a nucleophile attacks the **partially positive carbonyl carbon**. Anything that makes this carbon **more positive** or **easier to reach** speeds up the reaction; anything that donates electrons to it or crowds it slows the reaction down. So the order of reactivity of aldehydes and ketones is decided by two factors together: **steric hindrance** and the **electronic effect** of the groups on the C=O carbon.

<KeyPoint title="Final order (nucleophilic addition)">
**HCHO > CH₃CHO > higher aliphatic aldehydes (RCHO) > aromatic aldehydes (ArCHO)**, and **aldehydes > ketones**. Among ketones: **CH₃COCH₃ > CH₃COC₂H₅ > bulkier dialkyl ketones > C₆H₅COCH₃ > C₆H₅COC₆H₅**. Electron-withdrawing groups raise reactivity (p-NO₂C₆H₄CHO > C₆H₅CHO > p-CH₃C₆H₄CHO).
</KeyPoint>

## Step 1: aldehydes versus ketones

| Factor | Aldehyde R–CHO | Ketone R–CO–R |
|---|---|---|
| Groups crowding the C=O carbon | one R and a small H | two R groups |
| Electron release to C=O carbon | one alkyl group (+I, hyperconjugation) | two alkyl groups |
| Result | more electrophilic, less hindered | less electrophilic, more hindered |

The attacking nucleophile comes in roughly perpendicular to the plane of the carbonyl group, and the carbon changes from sp² (120°) to sp³ (109.5°) in the product. Two large groups get squeezed closer together in a ketone adduct, so the ketone's product is less stable as well as harder to reach.

## Step 2: formaldehyde at the top

Formaldehyde has two hydrogens and no alkyl group, so it is the least hindered and most electrophilic carbonyl compound. Each alkyl group added (ethanal, then propanal, then ketones) lowers reactivity a little more. The hydration equilibrium shows the trend clearly:

| Compound | Hydrate (gem-diol) at equilibrium in water |
|---|---|
| Formaldehyde, HCHO | about 99.9% |
| Acetone, CH₃COCH₃ | about 0.1% |

## Step 3: aromatic compounds react more slowly

In benzaldehyde the benzene ring is conjugated with the C=O and pushes electron density onto the carbonyl carbon by resonance, so the carbon is less positive. Benzaldehyde is therefore **less reactive than propanal**, and acetophenone and benzophenone (one and two rings) are the least reactive members of the list.

## Step 4: substituent effects

- **Electron-withdrawing groups** (–NO₂, –CN, –CCl₃, –CF₃) increase the positive charge on the carbonyl carbon and increase reactivity. Chloral, Cl₃C–CHO, hydrates so readily that it is handled as the stable solid chloral hydrate.
- **Electron-donating groups** (–CH₃, –OCH₃, –NH₂ on the ring) decrease reactivity.

## Exceptions and where the order shows up

- **NaHSO₃ addition.** The equilibrium lies to the product side for most aldehydes and to the starting side for most ketones, mainly for steric reasons. This is why bisulphite adducts are used to separate and purify aldehydes.
- **HCN addition.** Aldehydes and unhindered ketones form cyanohydrins; very hindered ketones such as di-tert-butyl ketone barely react.
- **Equilibrium versus rate.** The same factors govern both the speed of attack and how far the equilibrium lies towards the adduct, which is why the order holds for hydration, bisulphite and cyanohydrin formation alike.

## Which site does a Grignard reagent attack first?

When a molecule has several groups, use this sequence in exam answers:

1. **Acid–base reaction first.** A Grignard reagent is a strong base. Any acidic hydrogen (–OH, –COOH, –NH, ≡C–H, water) is deprotonated before anything else, and RMgX is destroyed as R–H. This includes carboxylic acids: RCOOH + R'MgX gives RCOO⁻MgX⁺ and R'–H.
2. **Then carbonyl attack.** Aldehydes and ketones undergo **nucleophilic addition** (alkoxide, then alcohol). Acid chlorides, anhydrides, esters and amides undergo **nucleophilic acyl substitution**, because they carry a leaving group.
3. **Alkyl halides** react far more slowly (SN2-type coupling), and **aryl halides** are practically inert.

<ExamTip title="Common mistake">
Do not write a fixed rate order "addition to C=O > acyl substitution" for every carbonyl compound. Acid chlorides are the most reactive carbonyl derivatives towards nucleophiles, followed by anhydrides, esters and then amides. Also, a free –COOH does not undergo acyl substitution with RMgX; it simply protonates the reagent.
</ExamTip>

## Solved examples

**1. Arrange ethanal, propanal, propanone, butanone in increasing reactivity.**
Ketones below aldehydes; within each class, the larger group lowers reactivity.
**Answer:** butanone < propanone < propanal < ethanal.

**2. Arrange benzaldehyde, p-tolualdehyde, p-nitrobenzaldehyde, acetophenone.**
Acetophenone is a ketone (lowest). Among the aldehydes, –CH₃ donates and –NO₂ withdraws electrons.
**Answer:** acetophenone < p-tolualdehyde < benzaldehyde < p-nitrobenzaldehyde.

**3. Arrange acetaldehyde, acetone, di-tert-butyl ketone, methyl tert-butyl ketone towards HCN.**
Steric hindrance dominates: each tert-butyl group blocks the approach of CN⁻.
**Answer:** di-tert-butyl ketone < methyl tert-butyl ketone < acetone < acetaldehyde.
