## What decides the basic strength of an amine?

An amine is a base because the **lone pair on nitrogen** can accept a proton. The more available that lone pair, and the more stable the protonated ammonium ion, the stronger the base. A stronger base has a larger Kb, a **smaller pKb**, and a conjugate acid (R–NH₃⁺) with a **larger pKa**. Basic character rises with electron density on nitrogen and falls when the lone pair is delocalised.

<KeyPoint title="Orders to remember">
- **Gas phase:** 3° > 2° > 1° > NH₃
- **Water, methyl series:** (CH₃)₂NH > CH₃NH₂ > (CH₃)₃N > NH₃ (2° > 1° > 3°)
- **Water, ethyl series:** (C₂H₅)₂NH > (C₂H₅)₃N > C₂H₅NH₂ > NH₃ (2° > 3° > 1°)
- **By type:** amidine/guanidine > alkylamine > NH₃ > pyridine > aniline > amide, pyrrole
</KeyPoint>

## Three competing factors for alkylamines

| Factor | Favours | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Inductive (+I) effect | 3° > 2° > 1° | more alkyl groups push more electron density onto N |
| Solvation of R–NH₃⁺ by water | 1° > 2° > 3° | more N–H bonds, more hydrogen bonds, more stable cation |
| Steric hindrance | small groups | bulky groups block both the proton and water molecules |

In the **gas phase** there is no solvent, so only the inductive effect counts. In **water** the three factors compete. With small methyl groups the tertiary amine loses most, giving 2° > 1° > 3°; with ethyl groups the inductive gain of the tertiary amine is large enough to beat the primary one, giving 2° > 3° > 1°.

## Arylamines and amides

In aniline the lone pair is in conjugation with the ring (five resonance structures), while the anilinium ion has only the two Kekulé forms, so protonation costs resonance energy. **Aniline (pKb 9.38) is a much weaker base than ammonia and alkylamines.** Interestingly, in the gas phase aniline is more basic than ammonia; solvation reverses the order in water.

In an **amide**, R–CO–NH₂, the lone pair is delocalised onto the carbonyl oxygen, so amides are practically non-basic and are not protonated by dilute aqueous acid. Hence **R–CO–NH₂ &lt; C₆H₅NH₂ &lt; R–NH₂**. In an **amidine**, R–C(=NH)–NH₂, protonation at the sp² nitrogen gives a cation whose charge is shared by both nitrogens, so amidines (and guanidine, conjugate-acid pKa 13.6) are among the strongest neutral organic bases.

N-Alkyl groups raise the basicity of aniline slightly: aniline &lt; N-methylaniline &lt; N,N-dimethylaniline (pKb 9.38, 9.30, 8.92).

## Ortho effect and steric inhibition of resonance

Almost any **ortho** substituent makes aniline a weaker base, because the protonated –NH₃⁺ group (now sp³ and non-planar) clashes with the neighbouring group. So:

- p-toluidine > m-toluidine > aniline > o-toluidine
- aniline > m-nitroaniline (2.46) > p-nitroaniline (1.00) > o-nitroaniline (−0.3)
- aniline > p-chloroaniline > m-chloroaniline > o-chloroaniline

The opposite happens with **N,N-dialkylanilines carrying two ortho groups**: the bulky NR₂ group is twisted out of the ring plane, its lone pair can no longer conjugate with the ring, and in exam answers such compounds are more basic than N,N-dimethylaniline. This is steric inhibition of resonance.

## Heterocycles and other substituent effects

- **Pyridine > aniline > pyrrole.** Pyridine's lone pair (sp²) is outside the aromatic ring current. Pyrrole's lone pair is part of the aromatic sextet; its conjugate acid has a pKa well below zero, so it is barely basic.
- **Hybridisation of the attached carbon:** propylamine (conjugate-acid pKa 10.71) > allylamine (9.49), because an sp² carbon withdraws electrons more than an sp³ carbon.
- **−I groups:** ethanolamine, HOCH₂CH₂NH₂ (9.50), is weaker than ethylamine (about 10.8).
- **Aryl group one carbon away:** cyclohexylamine (10.64) > benzylamine (9.34) > aniline (4.63) > diphenylamine (0.79).

<ExamTip title="Conjugate bases follow the acid order in reverse">
The weaker the acid, the stronger its conjugate base: Cl⁻ &lt; RCOO⁻ &lt; OH⁻ &lt; RO⁻ &lt; NH₂⁻, and HC≡C⁻ &lt; CH₂=CH⁻ &lt; CH₃CH₂⁻. NCERT notes that sodium ethoxide is a stronger base than sodium hydroxide.
</ExamTip>

## Solved examples

**1. (NCERT) Arrange in decreasing basic strength: C₆H₅NH₂, C₂H₅NH₂, (C₂H₅)₂NH, NH₃.**
**Answer:** (C₂H₅)₂NH > C₂H₅NH₂ > NH₃ > C₆H₅NH₂.

**2. Arrange in increasing basicity: acetanilide, aniline, benzylamine, cyclohexylamine.**
**Answer:** acetanilide &lt; aniline &lt; benzylamine &lt; cyclohexylamine.

**3. Arrange aniline, o-toluidine, p-toluidine and p-nitroaniline in decreasing basicity.**
**Answer:** p-toluidine > aniline > o-toluidine > p-nitroaniline.
