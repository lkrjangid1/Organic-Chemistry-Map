## What decides the stability of reaction intermediates?

Carbocations, carbanions and free radicals are short-lived species with an electron-poor or electron-rich carbon. Anything that **spreads out the charge or the odd electron** stabilises them: resonance, hyperconjugation, inductive effects, hybridisation and aromaticity. Electron donation helps cations and radicals but hurts carbanions, so the alkyl orders run in opposite directions.

<KeyPoint title="Final orders">
**Carbocations:** aromatic (tropylium) and cyclopropyl- or resonance-stabilised > 3° > 2° > 1° > CH₃⁺; vinyl and phenyl cations are very unstable.
**Free radicals:** allyl ≈ benzyl > 3° > 2° > 1° > CH₃• > vinyl ≈ phenyl.
**Carbanions:** stabilised (aromatic C₅H₅⁻, resonance, sp carbon, –I groups) > CH₃⁻ > 1° > 2° > 3°.
</KeyPoint>

## Carbocations

Alkyl groups stabilise a positive carbon by the inductive effect and hyperconjugation, so **(CH₃)₃C⁺ > (CH₃)₂CH⁺ > CH₃CH₂⁺ > CH₃⁺**. Gas-phase hydride ion affinity (HIA, the energy released when R⁺ captures H⁻) measures this directly: the **lower** the HIA, the **more stable** the cation.

| Cation | HIA (kcal/mol) | Main reason |
|---|---|---|
| Tropylium, C₇H₇⁺ | 201 | aromatic, 6π |
| Triphenylmethyl | 215 | resonance over three rings |
| Cyclopropenyl, C₃H₃⁺ | 221 | aromatic, 2π |
| tert-Butyl | 231 | 3°, nine C–H hyperconjugation |
| Benzyl | 234 | resonance |
| Isopropyl | 246 | 2° |
| Cyclopropylmethyl | 249 | cyclopropyl donation ("dancing resonance") |
| Allyl | 256 | resonance |
| Ethyl | 273 | 1° |
| Vinyl, CH₂=CH⁺ | 287 | charge on sp carbon |
| Phenyl | 298 | charge in an in-plane sp² orbital |
| Methyl | 312 | no stabilisation |

**Cyclopropylmethyl series.** The bent C–C bonds of a cyclopropane ring donate into the empty p orbital when the charge sits just outside the ring. More rings give more stabilisation, so exam answers rank **tricyclopropylmethyl > dicyclopropylmethyl > cyclopropylmethyl**.

**Bridgehead cations** of small bicyclic systems (such as the 1-norbornyl cation) cannot flatten, so Bredt's rule makes them extremely unstable, typically placed last in exam orders.

<ExamTip title="Common mistake">
"Dancing resonance beats aromaticity" is not supported by data: the aromatic cyclopropenyl cation (HIA 221) is more stable than the cyclopropylmethyl cation (249). Also, the vinyl cation is more stable than CH₃⁺ in the gas phase, though less stable than a primary alkyl cation.
</ExamTip>

**Exam convention versus data.** Most JEE answer keys use **benzyl > allyl > 3° > 2° > 1°**. Gas-phase HIA puts the unsubstituted benzyl cation close to, and slightly behind, tert-butyl, and the allyl cation near isopropyl. Follow the key's convention in objective questions, but remember that extra resonance wins clearly: the doubly benzylic diphenylmethyl cation (HIA 222) is well ahead of tert-butyl.

## Free radicals

Radical stability is read from the C–H bond dissociation energy (BDE) of the parent: a weaker bond means a more stable radical.

| Radical formed | C–H BDE (kcal/mol) |
|---|---|
| Allyl | 89 |
| Benzyl | 90 |
| tert-Butyl (3°) | 96.5 |
| Isopropyl (2°) | 99 |
| Ethyl (1°) | 101 |
| Methyl | 105 |
| Vinyl | 111 |
| Phenyl | 113 |

So **allyl ≈ benzyl > 3° > 2° > 1° > CH₃• > vinyl ≈ phenyl**, which is also the order of ease of H abstraction in [free-radical halogenation](/reactions/free-radical-halogenation/) and why [NBS](/reactions/nbs-allylic-bromination/) brominates the allylic position. An allylic radical is about 40 kJ/mol more stable than an alkyl radical of the same substitution.

## Carbanions

Simple carbanions are **pyramidal (sp³)**. Alkyl groups destabilise them, so **CH₃⁻ > 1° > 2° > 3°**. Stabilising factors are the reverse of those for cations:

| Factor | Example | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Greater s character | HC≡C⁻ more stable than CH₂=CH⁻, more stable than CH₃CH₂⁻ | lone pair held closer to nucleus |
| Resonance | benzyl, allyl, enolate anions | charge spread out |
| Aromaticity | cyclopentadienyl anion (cyclopentadiene pKa 18 in DMSO) | 6π aromatic |
| –I groups | CCl₃⁻, CF₃⁻ | charge pulled away |

Because a stable carbanion means an acidic C–H, pKa values of the parent hydrocarbons rank carbanions directly.

## Hybridisation of key intermediates

| Species | Shape | Hybridisation |
|---|---|---|
| CH₃⁺ | trigonal planar | sp² (empty p) |
| •CH₃ | planar | sp² (odd electron in pure p) |
| CH₃⁻ | pyramidal | sp³ |
| CF₃⁻ | pyramidal | sp³ |
| CCl₃⁻ | treated as planar in exam keys | sp², by back-bonding into Cl 3d |

The sp² label for CCl₃⁻ is an exam convention; the measurable fact is that CHCl₃ (pKa 24.4) is far more acidic than CHF₃ (pKa 30.5).

## Solved examples

**1. Arrange CH₃•, (CH₃)₃C•, CH₂=CH• and CH₂=CHCH₂• in decreasing stability.**
Allyl has resonance; vinyl has its odd electron on an sp² carbon with no delocalisation.
**Answer:** CH₂=CHCH₂• > (CH₃)₃C• > CH₃• > CH₂=CH•.

**2. Arrange C₆H₅C≡C⁻, (C₆H₅)₃C⁻, C₆H₅CH₂⁻ and CH₃⁻ in decreasing stability.**
Use parent pKa values (DMSO): phenylacetylene 28.8, triphenylmethane 30.6, toluene about 43, methane about 56.
**Answer:** C₆H₅C≡C⁻ > (C₆H₅)₃C⁻ > C₆H₅CH₂⁻ > CH₃⁻.

**3. Arrange CH₃⁺, C₂H₅⁺, (CH₃)₂CH⁺, (CH₃)₃C⁺ and C₇H₇⁺ (tropylium) in decreasing stability.**
Aromatic tropylium first, then alkyl cations by number of alkyl groups.
**Answer:** C₇H₇⁺ > (CH₃)₃C⁺ > (CH₃)₂CH⁺ > C₂H₅⁺ > CH₃⁺.
