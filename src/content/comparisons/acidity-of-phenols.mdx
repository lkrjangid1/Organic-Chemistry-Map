## What makes phenol acidic?

Phenol loses H⁺ from its OH group to give the **phenoxide ion**, whose negative charge is delocalised over the oxygen and the ortho and para carbons of the ring. This resonance stabilisation makes phenol (pKa 10.0) far more acidic than alcohols, though much weaker than carboxylic acids. **Electron-withdrawing groups increase the acidity of phenol, and electron-releasing groups decrease it**; the effect is largest when the group sits ortho or para to the OH.

<KeyPoint title="Orders to remember">
- **Picric acid > 2,4-dinitrophenol > p-nitrophenol > o-nitrophenol > m-nitrophenol > phenol**
- **Phenol > m-cresol > o-cresol ≈ p-cresol** (all cresols are weaker than phenol)
- **o-Chlorophenol > m-chlorophenol > p-chlorophenol > phenol**
- **Carboxylic acids > phenol > water > ethanol**
</KeyPoint>

## Nitrophenols: position matters

1. **p-Nitrophenol.** The phenoxide charge can be pushed right onto the nitro oxygens through the ring (–R effect) as well as pulled by –I. Strongest of the three.
2. **o-Nitrophenol.** Same resonance and an even larger –I effect, because the nitro group is next door. But the O–H hydrogen is locked in an **intramolecular hydrogen bond** with a nitro oxygen, which makes it slightly harder to remove. In exam answers this is why p > o.
3. **m-Nitrophenol.** No resonance path from the meta position to the nitro group, so only –I works. Still more acidic than phenol.

So the acidity order is **p-nitrophenol > o-nitrophenol > m-nitrophenol > phenol**. Every extra nitro group at ortho or para adds to the effect, which is why 2,4-dinitrophenol and picric acid are as strong as, or stronger than, carboxylic acids.

<ExamTip title="Same hydrogen bond, two exam questions">
Intramolecular hydrogen bonding in o-nitrophenol explains two facts: it is slightly **less acidic** than p-nitrophenol, and it is **steam volatile** (no association between molecules), which is how NCERT separates o- and p-nitrophenol by steam distillation.
</ExamTip>

## Intramolecular hydrogen bonding in ortho-substituted phenols

An OH group can form a six-membered hydrogen-bonded ring with an ortho group that carries a lone-pair acceptor: –NO₂, –CHO or –COR, –COOH, and to a smaller extent –F. The bond keeps the proton in place in the neutral phenol. In contrast, when the hydrogen bond forms in the **anion** (as in the salicylate ion, where the phenolic OH bonds to the neighbouring –COO⁻), it stabilises the conjugate base and the acid becomes stronger. Ask which species, the acid or its anion, gains the hydrogen bond.

## Cresols and other electron-releasing groups

The methyl group donates electrons by +I and hyperconjugation and concentrates the charge on the phenoxide oxygen, so all three cresols are weaker acids than phenol. By NCERT's values the ortho and para isomers (10.2) are slightly weaker than m-cresol (10.1), which is only marginally weaker than phenol. Strong +R donors such as –NH₂ at the para position weaken the acid more (p-aminophenol 10.46).

A methoxy group ortho to OH is roughly neutral: guaiacol (o-methoxyphenol) has pKa 9.98, close to phenol. This is why **o-nitrophenol (7.2) is much more acidic than o-methoxyphenol**, an NCERT exercise.

## Halophenols

Halogens withdraw electrons by –I but donate by +R. For acidity the –I effect wins, and it falls off with distance, so the ortho isomer is strongest and the para isomer weakest: **o-chlorophenol (8.56) > m-chlorophenol (9.12) > p-chlorophenol (9.41) > phenol (10.0)**. At the para position the +R donation partly cancels the –I pull.

## Solved examples

**1. (NCERT) Arrange in increasing acidity: propan-1-ol, 2,4,6-trinitrophenol, 3-nitrophenol, 3,5-dinitrophenol, phenol, 4-methylphenol.**
**Answer:** propan-1-ol &lt; 4-methylphenol &lt; phenol &lt; 3-nitrophenol &lt; 3,5-dinitrophenol &lt; 2,4,6-trinitrophenol.

**2. Arrange o-nitrophenol, m-nitrophenol, p-nitrophenol and phenol in decreasing acidity.**
**Answer:** p-nitrophenol > o-nitrophenol > m-nitrophenol > phenol.

**3. Which dissolves in aqueous NaHCO₃: phenol, p-cresol, picric acid or benzoic acid?**
Only acids stronger than carbonic acid react. **Answer:** picric acid and benzoic acid.
