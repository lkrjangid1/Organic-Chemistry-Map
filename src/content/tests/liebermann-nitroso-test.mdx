## What is the Liebermann nitroso test?

The **Liebermann nitroso test** is a colour test for **phenols** and for the **nitroso group**. A phenol with a free para position is warmed with **sodium nitrite in concentrated sulphuric acid**: the solution turns **deep green or blue**, becomes **red when poured into water**, and turns **blue again with NaOH**. **Secondary amines** are detected the same way after conversion into their yellow **N-nitrosamines**. The coloured product is the dye **phenolindophenol**.

## Reagent and how to prepare it

| Reagent | Purpose |
|---|---|
| NaNO₂ (or KNO₂) dissolved in conc. H₂SO₄ | Source of the nitrosonium ion NO⁺ |
| Water | Dilution step; brings out the red colour |
| Aqueous NaOH | Alkaline step; gives the blue phenoxide form |

Mixing potassium nitrite with concentrated sulphuric acid is also the Liebermann reagent used in forensic spot tests. Handle concentrated H₂SO₄ with great care.

## Procedure

**For a phenol**

1. Place a few crystals of the phenol and a crystal of NaNO₂ in a dry test tube.
2. Add about 1 mL of concentrated H₂SO₄ and warm gently. Note the colour (green to blue).
3. Pour the mixture carefully into cold water. Note the colour (red).
4. Add NaOH solution until alkaline. Note the colour (blue or green).

**For a secondary amine**

1. Treat the amine with NaNO₂ and dilute HCl in the cold. A yellow oily N-nitrosamine separates.
2. Warm a drop of this oil with phenol and concentrated H₂SO₄, then dilute and make alkaline as above. The same green, red and blue colours confirm the nitroso group.

## Observations and inference

| Sample | In conc. H₂SO₄ | After dilution with water | After adding NaOH | Inference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phenol (free para position) | Deep green to blue | Red | Blue | Phenol present |
| N-Nitrosamine of a 2° amine (with phenol) | Deep green to blue | Red | Blue | 2° amine (nitroso group) present |
| p-Substituted phenol, e.g. p-cresol | No characteristic colour | No red | No blue | Negative |
| 1° or 3° aliphatic amine (after HNO₂) | No nitrosamine formed | No colour | No colour | Negative |

## Chemistry behind it

1. **Nitrosation.** NO⁺ from nitrite and acid attacks the activated phenol ring at the para position to give **p-nitrosophenol**:

<Eq ce="C6H5OH + HNO2 -> p-ON-C6H4-OH + H2O" />

2. **Tautomerism.** p-Nitrosophenol is in equilibrium with its tautomer, **benzoquinone monoxime**, O=C₆H₄=N–OH.

3. **Condensation with a second phenol.** In concentrated acid the oxime is protonated, and the activated nitrogen is attacked by the para carbon of another phenol molecule. Loss of water gives the indophenol skeleton, present in strong acid as a protonated cation that is **green to blue**:

<Eq ce="O=C6H4=N-OH + C6H5OH ->[conc. H2SO4] O=C6H4=N-C6H4-OH + H2O" />

4. **Dilution.** In water the cation loses its extra protons and the neutral **phenolindophenol** appears **red**.

5. **Alkali.** NaOH removes the phenolic proton. The resulting **sodium salt of indophenol** has extended conjugation over both rings and is **blue**:

<Eq ce="O=C6H4=N-C6H4-OH + NaOH -> O=C6H4=N-C6H4-O^- Na+ + H2O" />

For a secondary amine, the N-nitrosamine supplies the NO⁺ group. Nitrosation of amines is reversible in strongly acidic solution, so the nitrosamine hands the nitroso group to phenol and steps 1–5 follow.

<Trick>
**GRB test (हरा लाल नीला)**: Green, Red, Blue, in the order conc. H₂SO₄ → water → NaOH.
</Trick>

<KeyPoint>
The test needs three things: a nitroso source (nitrite or an N-nitroso compound), concentrated acid, and a phenol with a free para position. The colours come from one dye, phenolindophenol, in three protonation states.
</KeyPoint>

<ExamTip>
If a question shows an amine giving a yellow oil with HNO₂ and then a positive Liebermann test, the amine is **secondary**. Primary aliphatic amines give N₂ instead, and tertiary aliphatic amines give no nitroso compound.
</ExamTip>

## Limitations

- **Para-substituted phenols** usually do not react, because the para position is needed for nitrosation. Hydroquinone and 4-iodophenol are exceptions.
- Some phenols with a free para position, such as 2- and 3-nitrophenol and the hydroxybenzoic acids, still give a negative result.
- The test is less sensitive than other phenol tests, and the exact shades vary with the phenol, so it is a qualitative test only.
- Concentrated sulphuric acid and carcinogenic nitrosamines make it hazardous; the [ferric chloride test](/tests/ferric-chloride-test/) is the routine phenol test.
