## What is the Victor Meyer test?

The **Victor Meyer test** distinguishes **primary, secondary and tertiary alcohols**. The alcohol is first turned into an alkyl iodide (red phosphorus and iodine), then into a **nitroalkane** (silver nitrite), which is treated with **nitrous acid** and finally made alkaline. The expected result in exam answers is: **primary alcohol, blood-red colour; secondary alcohol, blue colour; tertiary alcohol, colourless**. The test rests on Viktor Meyer's 1870s work on nitroalkanes and nitrolic acids.

## Reagent and how to prepare it

The test is a four-step sequence, not a single reagent:

| Step | Reagent | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Red phosphorus + I₂ | Makes PI₃ in situ, which converts R–OH into R–I |
| 2 | Silver nitrite, AgNO₂ | Replaces I by NO₂ through nitrogen, giving the nitroalkane R–NO₂ |
| 3 | Nitrous acid, HNO₂ (NaNO₂ + dilute mineral acid, cold) | Nitrosates the carbon next to NO₂ if it carries hydrogen |
| 4 | NaOH solution | Converts a nitrolic acid into its red sodium salt |

**Why AgNO₂?** Nitrite is an ambident nucleophile. AgNO₂ bonds through nitrogen and gives the nitroalkane, while KNO₂ bonds through oxygen and gives the alkyl nitrite R–O–N=O.

## Procedure

1. Warm the alcohol with red phosphorus and iodine to form the alkyl iodide, and separate it.
2. Treat the iodide with silver nitrite to form the nitroalkane.
3. Add sodium nitrite and dilute acid (nitrous acid forms in situ) and shake.
4. Make the solution alkaline with NaOH and note the colour.

## Observations and inference

| Alcohol | Product after HNO₂ | Colour (exam convention) | Inference |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1° (R–CH₂–OH) | Nitrolic acid, R–C(NO₂)=N–OH; its sodium salt in NaOH | **Blood red** | Primary |
| 2° (R₂CH–OH) | R₂C(NO₂)(N=O), called pseudonitrole | **Blue**; NaOH causes no further change | Secondary |
| 3° (R₃C–OH) | No reaction with HNO₂ | **Colourless** | Tertiary |

## Chemistry behind it

**Primary alcohol (ethanol):**

<Eq ce="CH3CH2OH ->[red P, I2] CH3CH2I ->[AgNO2] CH3CH2NO2" />

The nitroethane has two α-hydrogens. Nitrous acid nitrosates the α-carbon (the reaction goes through the nitronate ion), and the nitroso compound tautomerises to the oxime form, the nitrolic acid:

<Eq ce="CH3CH2NO2 + HNO2 -> CH3C(=NOH)NO2 + H2O" />

The N–OH hydrogen of the nitrolic acid is acidic. In NaOH it forms the sodium salt, which gives the red colour:

<Eq ce="CH3C(=NOH)NO2 + NaOH -> CH3C(=NO^-Na+)NO2 + H2O" />

**Secondary alcohol (propan-2-ol):** the nitroalkane (CH₃)₂CH–NO₂ has only one α-hydrogen. Nitrosation gives (CH₃)₂C(NO₂)(N=O). No hydrogen is left on that carbon, so it cannot tautomerise to an oxime and does not dissolve in NaOH as a salt. The blue colour is attributed to its C–N=O (nitroso) group.

<Eq ce="(CH3)2CH-NO2 + HNO2 -> (CH3)2C(NO2)NO + H2O" />

**Tertiary alcohol (2-methylpropan-2-ol):** the nitroalkane (CH₃)₃C–NO₂ has **no α-hydrogen**, so it cannot form a nitronate and does not react with nitrous acid. No colour develops.

<KeyPoint>
The test counts the **α-hydrogens of the nitroalkane**: two (1°) gives a nitrolic acid that forms a red salt in alkali, one (2°) gives a blue nitroso compound that cannot form a salt, and none (3°) gives no reaction.
</KeyPoint>

<Trick>
**RBC** for 1°, 2°, 3°: **R**ed, **B**lue, **C**olourless, like red blood cells.
</Trick>

<ExamTip title="Common mistake">
Write **red phosphorus**, not "P₄": P₄ is the formula of white phosphorus. Red phosphorus only serves to generate PI₃ (or HI) in situ with iodine. Also write **AgNO₂**, not AgNO₃ or KNO₂.
</ExamTip>

## Limitations

- It is a long, multi-step procedure. For a quick laboratory classification, the [Lucas test](/tests/lucas-test/) is far simpler.
- Each intermediate must be formed cleanly; side products such as alkyl nitrites (formed alongside nitroalkanes) do not give the colours.
- It identifies the class of the alcohol only, not its full structure.
