## What is the nitrous acid test for amines?

The **nitrous acid test** classifies an amine by how it reacts with **nitrous acid (HNO₂)**, made in the test tube from **NaNO₂ and dilute HCl** in an **ice bath (273–278 K)**. **Primary aliphatic amines** release **nitrogen gas** and form alcohols, **primary aromatic amines** form a **diazonium salt**, **secondary amines** give a **yellow oily N-nitrosamine**, and **tertiary aliphatic amines** only form a **soluble salt**. Tertiary aromatic amines are nitrosated on the ring.

## Reagent and how to prepare it

Nitrous acid is unstable and is always generated just before use:

<Eq ce="NaNO2 + HCl -> HNO2 + NaCl" />

In acid it gives the electrophile that actually reacts, the **nitrosonium ion, NO⁺**:

<Eq ce="HNO2 + H+ <=> H2O + NO+" />

## Procedure

1. Dissolve a few drops of the amine in dilute HCl and cool to 0–5 °C in ice.
2. Add an ice-cold NaNO₂ solution dropwise, swirling and keeping the tube in ice.
3. Watch for gas, an oily layer or a coloured solid.
4. For a suspected aromatic primary amine, pour some of the cold solution into alkaline 2-naphthol. An orange-red dye confirms a diazonium salt.

## Observations and inference

| Amine | Example | Observation | Product |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1° aliphatic | ethylamine | **Effervescence of N₂** from a clear solution | Alcohol (with some alkene, alkyl halide) |
| 1° aromatic | aniline | No gas in the cold; orange-red dye with alkaline 2-naphthol | Arenediazonium salt, ArN₂⁺Cl⁻ |
| 2° (aliphatic or aromatic) | diethylamine, N-methylaniline | **Yellow oil or solid** separates | N-Nitrosamine, R₂N–N=O |
| 3° aliphatic | triethylamine | No visible change; clear solution | Ammonium salt, R₃NH⁺ |
| 3° aromatic | N,N-dimethylaniline | **Green solid** (if para position free) | p-Nitroso-N,N-dimethylaniline |

## Chemistry behind it

**Primary aliphatic amine.** NO⁺ attacks nitrogen, and after proton shifts and loss of water an alkyldiazonium ion forms. It is very unstable, so it loses N₂ at once; the carbocation is captured by water:

<Eq ce="RNH2 + HNO2 -> [RN2+] -> ROH + N2 ^ + H2O" />

Nitrogen is given off quantitatively, which is used to estimate amino groups in amino acids and proteins.

**Primary aromatic amine.** The aryldiazonium ion is stabilised by the ring and survives in the cold (diazotisation):

<Eq ce="C6H5NH2 + NaNO2 + 2HCl ->[273-278 K] C6H5N2+Cl- + NaCl + 2H2O" />

On warming it decomposes to phenol and N₂, so temperature control matters.

**Secondary amine.** The N-nitroso compound forms and the reaction stops, because nitrogen has no hydrogen left to lose:

<Eq ce="(C2H5)2NH + HNO2 -> (C2H5)2N-N=O + H2O" />

**Tertiary aliphatic amine.** There is no N–H, so the only change is protonation:

<Eq ce="(C2H5)3N + HNO2 -> (C2H5)3NH+ NO2-" />

**Tertiary aromatic amine.** The strongly activating –NMe₂ group lets the weak electrophile NO⁺ attack the ring at the para position:

<Eq ce="C6H5N(CH3)2 + HNO2 -> p-ON-C6H4-N(CH3)2 + H2O" />

In exam answers this p-nitroso compound is sometimes shown being oxidised further by HNO₃ to p-nitro-N,N-dimethylaniline.

<KeyPoint>
Count the N–H hydrogens. Two (1°): full diazotisation, then N₂ loss (aliphatic) or a stable diazonium salt (aromatic, cold). One (2°): stops at R₂N–N=O. None (3°): only salt formation, or ring nitrosation for activated aryl amines.
</KeyPoint>

<ExamTip title="Common mistake">
The yellow product from a secondary amine is an **N-nitroso**amine, R₂N–N=O, not an N-nitro compound (R₂N–NO₂). The nitrosamine is also what gives a positive [Liebermann nitroso test](/tests/liebermann-nitroso-test/).
</ExamTip>

## Limitations

- **Nitrosamines are carcinogenic**, so this test is now avoided in school laboratories; the [Hinsberg test](/tests/hinsberg-test/) is safer.
- With 1° aliphatic amines the carbocation can rearrange and give mixtures, so the test identifies the class, not a single product.
- Nitrous acid must be made fresh and kept cold; if the solution warms, the aromatic diazonium salt also decomposes with N₂ evolution and can be mistaken for an aliphatic amine.
- A tertiary aromatic amine with its para position blocked shows no green nitroso compound.
