## What is purification of organic compounds?

**Purification of organic compounds** means separating a compound from the impurities that come with it after extraction from nature or after a reaction. The choice of method depends on the physical properties that differ between the compound and its impurities: volatility, solubility, boiling point or adsorption. The standard methods are **sublimation, crystallisation, distillation, differential extraction and chromatography**. Purity is finally confirmed by a **sharp melting or boiling point**.

<KeyPoint>
Sublimable solid + non-sublimable impurity → sublimation. Solid with impurities of different solubility → crystallisation. Liquids → distillation. Compound dissolved in water → differential extraction. Small amounts or similar compounds → chromatography.
</KeyPoint>

## Choosing a method

| Mixture | Method | Property used |
|---|---|---|
| Camphor, naphthalene or iodine mixed with non-volatile solids | Sublimation | Solid turns directly to vapour |
| Impure solid (e.g. crude product of a reaction) | Crystallisation | Different solubility in a hot vs cold solvent |
| Liquids, or a volatile liquid with non-volatile impurities | Distillation (simple, fractional, reduced pressure, steam) | Different boiling points or steam volatility |
| Organic compound in an aqueous solution | Differential extraction | Higher solubility in an immiscible organic solvent |
| Mixture of similar compounds, coloured pigments, amino acids | Chromatography | Different adsorption or partition between two phases |

## 1. Sublimation

Some solids pass from solid to vapour without melting when heated. The impure solid is heated gently; its vapour condenses as pure crystals on a cold surface (an inverted funnel or a cold finger), and the **non-sublimable impurities stay behind**. Doing this under reduced pressure lets compounds sublime at a lower temperature. Common examples: camphor, naphthalene and iodine.

## 2. Crystallisation

The most common way to purify an organic solid. It depends on the **difference in solubility** of the compound and its impurities in a chosen solvent.

1. Choose a solvent in which the compound is **sparingly soluble at room temperature but fairly soluble when hot**.
2. Dissolve the impure compound in the minimum amount of hot solvent to get a nearly saturated solution.
3. If the solution is coloured, boil it briefly with **activated charcoal**, which adsorbs coloured impurities, and filter while hot.
4. Cool the filtrate slowly: the pure compound crystallises out.
5. Collect the crystals by filtration. The **mother liquor** keeps the soluble impurities and a little of the compound.

If no single solvent works, a **mixture of two solvents** is used: one that dissolves the compound well and one that dissolves it poorly (for example aqueous ethanol). Impurities of similar solubility need **repeated crystallisation**.

## 3. Distillation

Distillation separates liquids with different boiling points, or a volatile liquid from non-volatile impurities. Its four variants (simple, fractional, under reduced pressure and steam distillation) are covered in detail in [Distillation methods](/concepts/distillation-methods/). Example: chloroform (b.p. 334 K) is separated from aniline (b.p. 457 K) by simple distillation.

## 4. Differential extraction

Used when an organic compound is dissolved in water. The aqueous solution is shaken in a **separatory funnel** with an organic solvent in which the compound is **more soluble than in water**. The two liquids must be **immiscible**, so they form two layers; the organic layer is run off and the solvent is removed by distillation or evaporation. When the compound is only slightly soluble in the organic solvent, **continuous extraction** is used, in which the same solvent is recycled through the solution many times.

## 5. Chromatography

Chromatography (Greek *chroma*, colour; first used for plant pigments) separates the components of a mixture between a **stationary phase** (a solid or a liquid held on a solid) and a **mobile phase** (a liquid or gas) that moves over it. Components that interact more with the stationary phase move more slowly.

| Type | Principle | Stationary phase | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adsorption chromatography | Different degrees of **adsorption** on a solid | Silica gel or alumina | Column chromatography, thin-layer chromatography (TLC) |
| Partition chromatography | Continuous **partition** between two liquids | Water held in paper fibres | Paper chromatography |

**Column chromatography:** the mixture is placed on top of a glass column packed with adsorbent and an eluant is run down slowly. The most strongly adsorbed component stays near the top; the others move down to different distances and are collected separately.

**Thin-layer chromatography:** a thin layer (about 0.2 mm) of silica gel or alumina is spread on a glass plate. A spot of the mixture is placed about 2 cm from the bottom and the plate is stood in a closed jar with the eluant. The **retardation factor** describes how far each spot moves:

Rf = distance moved by the substance from the base line ÷ distance moved by the solvent from the base line

Coloured spots are seen directly. Colourless ones are located under **UV light** (if they fluoresce), with **iodine vapour** (brown spots), or with a spray reagent such as **ninhydrin** for amino acids.

**Paper chromatography:** water trapped in the chromatography paper is the stationary phase, so this is partition chromatography. The developed strip is called a chromatogram.

<ExamTip>
A higher Rf means weaker adsorption (or greater solubility in the mobile phase). In column chromatography the **most strongly adsorbed** compound is eluted **last**. Rf has no unit and is always less than 1.
</ExamTip>

## Solved examples

1. **Separate iodine from common salt.** Iodine sublimes, NaCl does not: use sublimation.
2. **Purify a crude solid that is slightly brown.** Crystallise it from a suitable hot solvent with a little activated charcoal, then filter hot and cool.
3. **A spot moves 3.0 cm while the solvent front moves 7.5 cm.** Rf = 3.0 ÷ 7.5 = 0.40.
