## What are Nylon-6,6 and Nylon-6?

**Nylons** are synthetic **polyamides**: long chains held together by amide (–CO–NH–) links. **Nylon-6,6** is made by condensation polymerisation of **hexamethylenediamine** with **adipic acid**, losing water at each amide bond. **Nylon-6** is made from a single monomer, **caprolactam**, by heating it with a little water so that the ring opens and the units join. Both are strong, partly crystalline **fibres** used in textiles, ropes and tyre cords.

<KeyPoint>
Naming rule: the numbers count carbon atoms in the monomers. Nylon-6,6 = 6 C diamine + 6 C diacid. Nylon-6,10 = 6 C diamine + 10 C diacid. Nylon-6 = one monomer with 6 C (caprolactam / 6-aminohexanoic acid).
</KeyPoint>

## Nylon-6,6

Wallace Carothers at DuPont made the first nylons around 1930 by heating a diamine with a diacid. Industrially, equal amounts of the two monomers are first combined in water; this mixture is then heated (around 280 °C, under pressure) while water is removed, which drives amide formation:

<Eq ce="nHOOC(CH2)4COOH + nH2N(CH2)6NH2 ->[high T, high P] (-OC(CH2)4CO-NH(CH2)6NH-)_{n} + 2nH2O" />

Each amide bond forms by attack of an –NH₂ group on a –COOH group with loss of H₂O (a nucleophilic acyl substitution). Every step makes a new bifunctional molecule that can react again, so the chain grows **stepwise**. Strictly, (2n − 1) H₂O is lost for a chain with two free ends; exam answers usually write 2n H₂O.

Repeating unit: C₁₂H₂₂N₂O₂ (molar mass 226 g/mol). Uses: textile and carpet fibres, sheets, brush bristles, airbags, tyre cord, and moulded engineering parts such as gears and bearings.

<ExamTip>
Nylon-6,6 is a **copolymer** made by condensation of two different monomers. Exam questions often ask "which polymer releases water on formation": Nylon-6,6 does, PVC and Teflon do not.
</ExamTip>

## Nylon-6

Caprolactam (azepan-2-one) is a seven-membered ring containing one N and six C atoms. It is made from cyclohexanone oxime by the [Beckmann rearrangement](/reactions/beckmann-rearrangement/). Heating caprolactam with a small amount of water at high temperature (about 533 K under nitrogen for 4–5 hours) opens the ring:

<Eq ce="n(CH2)5CONH ->[H2O, 533 K] (-NH(CH2)5CO-)_{n}" />

How it works: water hydrolyses a few caprolactam rings to 6-aminohexanoic acid, H₂N(CH₂)₅COOH. The free –NH₂ end then attacks the C=O of another caprolactam ring, opening it and lengthening the chain, and the process repeats.

<ExamTip title="Common mistake">
NCERT and JEE/NEET keys classify Nylon-6 as a **condensation polymer (polyamide)**, and that is the expected answer. Strictly speaking it is formed by **ring-opening polymerisation** of a single monomer, so it is a special case between addition and condensation polymers. Do not write two monomers for Nylon-6.
</ExamTip>

Nylon-6 was developed by Paul Schlack (1938) and sold as Perlon. It melts lower than Nylon-6,6 (about 218 °C vs 264 °C). Uses: tyre cords, fabrics and ropes.

## Nylon-6,10

<Eq ce="nH2N(CH2)6NH2 + nHOOC(CH2)8COOH -> (-NH(CH2)6NH-CO(CH2)8CO-)_{n} + 2nH2O" />

The longer hydrocarbon segment from **sebacic acid** (decanedioic acid) makes Nylon-6,10 more hydrophobic, so it is used for bristles. In the classroom "nylon rope trick", hexamethylenediamine in water is layered under sebacoyl chloride in cyclohexane; a film of Nylon-6,10 forms at the interface and can be pulled out as a continuous rope (interfacial polymerisation, HCl is the small molecule lost).

## Why nylons are fibres

Amide groups on neighbouring chains form **N–H···O=C hydrogen bonds**. These strong intermolecular forces pack the chains closely and give partial crystallinity, so nylons have high tensile strength and can be drawn into oriented fibres. That is why NCERT uses Nylon-6,6 as the standard example of a **fibre** in classification by molecular forces.

## Exam tips

- Count carbons in the repeating unit to identify the nylon: (CH₂)₆ + (CH₂)₄ with two C=O gives 6 + 6.
- Nylon-6,6 and Nylon-6,10 need two monomers; Nylon-6 needs one.
- Polyamide = amide linkage; polyester (Terylene, Glyptal) = ester linkage.
- Nylon-2–nylon-6 (from glycine and 6-aminohexanoic acid) is a biodegradable polyamide.
