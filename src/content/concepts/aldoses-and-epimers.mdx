## What are D-aldoses and epimers?

**Aldoses** are monosaccharides with an aldehyde group (–CHO) at C1. A **D-aldose** is one whose **stereocentre farthest from the CHO group has its OH on the right** in the Fischer projection, the same as in **D-(+)-glyceraldehyde**. Starting from D-glyceraldehyde and adding one carbon at a time gives a "family tree" of 2 D-aldotetroses, 4 D-aldopentoses and 8 D-aldohexoses. **Epimers** are pairs of these sugars that differ in configuration at **only one** stereocentre.

## How the family tree is built

When an aldose is lengthened by one carbon at the CHO end (the Kiliani–Fischer synthesis), a **new stereocentre appears at C2**. It can form with the OH on the right or on the left, so **every parent sugar gives a pair of C2 epimers**, while all its old stereocentres keep their configuration. For example, D-arabinose gives D-glucose and D-mannose.

- The bottom stereocentre (C3 in tetroses, C4 in pentoses, C5 in hexoses) stays **OH-right** for the whole D-series.
- Number of stereoisomers = 2ⁿ, where n is the number of stereocentres. Half of them are D and half are L.

| Class | Stereocentres | Total stereoisomers | D-sugars |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aldotriose | 1 (C2) | 2 | 1 (D-glyceraldehyde) |
| Aldotetrose | 2 (C2, C3) | 4 | 2 |
| Aldopentose | 3 (C2–C4) | 8 | 4 |
| Aldohexose | 4 (C2–C5) | 16 | 8 |

## The D-aldose family tree

R = OH on the right, L = OH on the left in the Fischer projection (CHO at the top, CH₂OH at the bottom).

**Tetroses (from D-glyceraldehyde)**

| Sugar | C2 | C3 |
|---|---|---|
| D-Erythrose | R | R |
| D-Threose | L | R |

**Pentoses** (from erythrose: ribose and arabinose; from threose: xylose and lyxose)

| Sugar | C2 | C3 | C4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-Ribose | R | R | R |
| D-Arabinose | L | R | R |
| D-Xylose | R | L | R |
| D-Lyxose | L | L | R |

**Hexoses** (two from each pentose, in order)

| Parent pentose | Sugar | C2 | C3 | C4 | C5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ribose | D-Allose | R | R | R | R |
| Ribose | D-Altrose | L | R | R | R |
| Arabinose | D-Glucose | R | L | R | R |
| Arabinose | D-Mannose | L | L | R | R |
| Xylose | D-Gulose | R | R | L | R |
| Xylose | D-Idose | L | R | L | R |
| Lyxose | D-Galactose | R | L | L | R |
| Lyxose | D-Talose | L | L | L | R |

<Trick>
**RAXL** gives the pentoses in order: Ribose, Arabinose, Xylose, Lyxose. For the hexoses, the classic English sentence "All altruists gladly make gum in gallon tanks" gives allose, altrose, glucose, mannose, gulose, idose, galactose, talose.
</Trick>

A quick way to rebuild the tables: read the C2 column as alternating R, L, R, L…; the C3 column of the hexoses as R, R, L, L, R, R, L, L; and the C4 column as four R then four L. C5 is always R for D-sugars.

## Epimers

Two sugars are **epimers** if they have the same constitution and differ at exactly **one** stereocentre. Each pair of siblings in the tree (same parent) is a **C2 epimer** pair:

| C2 epimer pairs |
|---|
| Erythrose / threose |
| Ribose / arabinose; xylose / lyxose |
| Allose / altrose; glucose / mannose; gulose / idose; galactose / talose |

Epimers need not be siblings. Comparing columns also gives:

- **D-Glucose and D-galactose**: differ only at **C4**, so they are **C4 epimers**.
- **D-Glucose and D-allose**: differ only at **C3**, so they are **C3 epimers**.
- **D-Mannose and D-galactose** differ at C2 and C4, so they are diastereomers but **not epimers**.

<KeyPoint>
Glucose, mannose and fructose give the **same osazone** with excess phenylhydrazine. Osazone formation uses up C1 and C2, so sugars that differ only at C1–C2 (the C2 epimers glucose and mannose, and the ketose fructose) end up as one product.
</KeyPoint>

## Anomers versus epimers

In water, an aldohexose such as glucose closes into a six-membered ring when the C5–OH adds to the CHO group. C1 becomes a new stereocentre, the **anomeric carbon**, and two ring forms result: **α** and **β**. These are **anomers**.

| Feature | Epimers | Anomers |
|---|---|---|
| Where they differ | Any one stereocentre (C2, C3, C4…) | Only the anomeric carbon (C1 of a cyclic aldose, C2 of a cyclic ketose) |
| Structure needed | Open-chain or cyclic | Cyclic (hemiacetal) form only |
| Interconvert in water? | No (needs a chemical reaction) | Yes, through the open chain (mutarotation) |
| Example | D-glucose / D-mannose (C2) | α-D-glucose / β-D-glucose |

All anomers are epimers, but not all epimers are anomers.

<ExamTip title="Common mistake">
Glucose and fructose are not epimers: one is an aldose and the other a ketose, so they are functional isomers. Also, "D" and "L" describe configuration only; D-fructose is laevorotatory and D-glucose is dextrorotatory.
</ExamTip>

## Solved examples

**1. Which sugar is the C4 epimer of D-glucose?**
D-Galactose (glucose R, L, R, R; galactose R, L, L, R).

**2. How many stereoisomers does an aldopentose have, and how many are D?**
Three stereocentres give 2³ = 8 stereoisomers, of which 4 are D.

**3. Are α-D-glucose and β-D-mannose anomers?**
No. They differ at C1 and C2, so they are neither anomers nor epimers.
