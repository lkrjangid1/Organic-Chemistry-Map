## What is steric inhibition of resonance?

**Steric inhibition of resonance (SIR)** is the reduction or loss of resonance between a substituent and a benzene ring when **bulky neighbouring groups** force the substituent to rotate out of the plane of the ring. Resonance needs the p orbitals of the substituent and the ring to be parallel; once the group is twisted, the overlap is poor and its **+M or −M effect is largely switched off**. Its inductive effect, which acts through σ bonds, remains.

The cause is van der Waals (steric) repulsion between two groups that are too close: typically two ortho groups on either side of the substituent, or one ortho group next to a branched substituent.

## Why planarity matters

The [resonance effect](/concepts/resonance-effect/) is an interaction between π bonds, or between a π bond and a lone pair on the next atom. In aniline, the nitrogen lone pair has high p character, and the –NH₂ group is flatter than in an alkylamine because the lone pair overlaps with the ring π system. This overlap is best when the lone-pair orbital is parallel to the ring p orbitals. Any twist about the C(ring)–N bond reduces it; at 90° it is essentially zero.

## Example 1: basicity of dimethylanilines

| Compound | Label | What the ortho methyls do | Effect on basicity |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2,6-Dimethylaniline | a | crowd the –NH₃⁺ formed on protonation (–NH₂ itself is small) | lower (ortho effect) |
| Aniline | b | none | reference |
| N,N-Dimethylaniline | c | none; lone pair delocalised into ring | slightly higher than aniline (+I of N-methyls) |
| N,N,2,6-Tetramethylaniline | d | twist –NMe₂ out of plane; lone pair no longer delocalised | much higher (SIR) |

<KeyPoint title="Basicity order">
**d > c > b > a**: N,N,2,6-tetramethylaniline > N,N-dimethylaniline > aniline > 2,6-dimethylaniline.
</KeyPoint>

The key contrast is between a and d. The small –NH₂ group fits between two ortho methyls without much twisting, so SIR is minor and the [ortho effect](/concepts/ortho-effect/) (steric inhibition of protonation) makes a weaker than aniline. The –NMe₂ group is bulky: its methyl groups clash with the ring methyls, the group turns out of plane, and the localised lone pair behaves more like that of an aliphatic tertiary amine.

## Example 2: acidity of nitrophenols

A para-nitro group strengthens phenol (p-nitrophenol pKa 7.15 versus phenol 9.89) mainly by −M delocalisation of the phenoxide charge onto the nitro oxygens.

| Compound | Position of CH₃ groups | Nitro group | Acidity |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2,6-Dimethyl-4-nitrophenol | beside the OH | stays coplanar, −M works | similar to p-nitrophenol |
| 3,5-Dimethyl-4-nitrophenol | beside the NO₂ | twisted out of plane, −M lost | clearly weaker |

If the nitro group is replaced by a linear cyano group (4-cyano-3,5-dimethylphenol), the methyl groups cannot twist it, so the acidity changes very little.

## Example 3: ortho-substituted benzoic acids

An ortho group twists the –COOH group out of the ring plane. Here the lost conjugation (ring to C=O) normally stabilises the undissociated acid, so removing it makes the acid **stronger**. This is one of the main reasons for the ortho effect in benzoic acids: o-toluic acid (pKa 3.9) is stronger than benzoic acid (4.2).

## When SIR does not operate

| Situation | Reason |
|---|---|
| Linear group (–C≡N) | the group has no width to clash with ortho neighbours |
| Small atoms or groups (–F, –OH) | too small to be forced out of plane |
| Neighbours attract each other (hydrogen bond) | attraction holds the group in plane rather than pushing it out |
| Group already non-conjugating (alkyl, –NH₃⁺) | no resonance to lose |

<ExamTip>
To decide whether SIR applies, check three things: (1) the substituent must be a resonance-active group (–NR₂, –NO₂, –COOH, –COR); (2) it must be **non-linear and bulky enough**; (3) there must be bulky groups on **both** ortho positions, or a very large one on one side. Then remove its ±M effect and re-judge using only its inductive effect.
</ExamTip>

## SIR vs ortho effect

| Feature | SIR effect | Ortho effect (anilines) |
|---|---|---|
| What happens | substituent twisted, resonance lost | protonated –NH₃⁺ crowded by ortho group |
| Needs | bulky resonance-active group plus bulky neighbours | any ortho group |
| Effect on N,N-dialkylanilines | basicity increases | minor |
| Effect on primary anilines | minor | basicity decreases |

<ExamTip title="Common mistake">
SIR does not always increase basicity or acidity. It removes a resonance effect, so the result depends on what that resonance was doing: for –NMe₂ (+M lost) basicity rises; for a para –NO₂ in phenol (−M lost) acidity falls.
</ExamTip>

The size of the effect in water also depends on solvation of the ions, so measured differences can be smaller than the simple picture suggests.
