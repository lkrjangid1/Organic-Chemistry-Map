## What are Terylene and Glyptal?

**Terylene** (also called **Dacron**; chemically poly(ethylene terephthalate), **PET**) and **Glyptal** (an **alkyd resin**) are **polyesters**: condensation polymers in which the repeating units are joined by **ester (–CO–O–) links**. Both are made by heating a diol, **ethylene glycol**, with a benzenedicarboxylic acid, losing water at every ester bond. Terylene uses **terephthalic acid** (the 1,4 or para acid) and is a fibre; Glyptal, as taught for exams, uses **phthalic acid** (the 1,2 or ortho acid) and is a resin for paints and lacquers.

<KeyPoint>
Para acid (terephthalic) + ethylene glycol = Terylene/Dacron (fibre). Ortho acid (phthalic) + ethylene glycol = Glyptal (paint resin). Both are polyesters and step-growth (condensation) copolymers.
</KeyPoint>

## Comparison table

| Feature | Terylene (Dacron, PET) | Glyptal (alkyd resin) |
|---|---|---|
| Diol | Ethylene glycol, HOCH₂CH₂OH | Ethylene glycol (exam answer); glycerol in commercial resins |
| Acid | Terephthalic acid, p-C₆H₄(COOH)₂ | Phthalic acid, o-C₆H₄(COOH)₂ (or phthalic anhydride) |
| Linkage | Ester | Ester |
| Conditions | 420–460 K, zinc acetate–Sb₂O₃ catalyst (exam shorthand: H⁺, heat) | Heat with acid catalyst (exam shorthand: H⁺, heat) |
| Small molecule lost | H₂O (CH₃OH if dimethyl terephthalate is used) | H₂O |
| Nature | Thermoplastic fibre and film | Resin; network-forming when glycerol is used |
| Uses | Crease-resistant fabrics (blended with cotton and wool), sails, soft-drink bottles, films and tapes (Mylar), reinforcement in safety helmets | Paints, lacquers and varnishes |

## Terylene (Dacron)

<Eq ce="nHOCH2CH2OH + nHOOC-C6H4-COOH ->[zinc acetate, Sb2O3][420-460 K] (-OCH2CH2O-OC-C6H4-CO-)_{n} + 2nH2O" />

Each step is an esterification: the –OH of the glycol attacks the –COOH of the acid and water is removed. Since every product is again a molecule with reactive ends, the chain grows step by step. The repeating unit, –O–CH₂–CH₂–O–CO–C₆H₄–CO–, has the formula **C₁₀H₈O₄** (ten carbon atoms: two from the glycol, eight from terephthalic acid).

In industry, **dimethyl terephthalate** is often used in place of the acid; it reacts with ethylene glycol by **transesterification**, releasing methanol instead of water, and the intermediate bis(2-hydroxyethyl) terephthalate is then polycondensed.

Properties and uses:

- **Fibre:** strong and crease resistant; blended with cotton and wool for clothing; sailcloth is commonly Dacron.
- **Bottles:** PET is the familiar clear soft-drink bottle plastic, a thermoplastic that softens on heating.
- **Film:** stretched PET film (Mylar) has tensile strength close to steel and was used for recording tape.
- Glass-reinforced PET is used in safety helmets.

The names come from brands: **Terylene** was ICI's name, **Dacron** is DuPont's name for the fibre, and **Mylar** is DuPont's name for the film. All are the same polymer.

## Glyptal (alkyd resin)

<Eq ce="nHOCH2CH2OH + n o-C6H4(COOH)2 ->[H+][\Delta] (-OCH2CH2O-OC-C6H4-CO-)_{n} + 2nH2O" />

With ethylene glycol (two –OH groups) and phthalic acid (two –COOH groups) the product is a **linear** polyester; this is the version NCERT gives and exams expect. Glyptal is used to make **paints and lacquers**.

<ExamTip>
For exams, write the monomers of Glyptal as **ethylene glycol + phthalic acid**. In practice the original Glyptal resins (and most alkyds) were made from **glycerol** (three –OH groups) and phthalic acid or **phthalic anhydride**. The third –OH lets chains branch and cross-link, so the resin hardens into a tough film; drying oils are added to alkyd paints for the same reason.
</ExamTip>

## Terylene vs Glyptal: why the position matters

Terephthalic acid carries its two –COOH groups on opposite corners of the ring (1,4), so each acid unit continues the chain in a straight line. Straight, regular chains can line up side by side, which is what a fibre needs: Terylene is semicrystalline and is drawn into oriented fibres, just like nylon. Phthalic acid has its two –COOH groups on neighbouring carbons (1,2), so each unit turns the chain sharply; Glyptal is used as a coating resin rather than as a fibre.

<ExamTip title="Common mistake">
Do not mix up the acids: **tere**phthalic (para) goes with **Tery**lene. Phthalic (ortho) goes with Glyptal. Isophthalic acid is the meta isomer and is not used for either in exam answers.
</ExamTip>

## Exam tips

- Polyester = diacid + diol; polyamide (nylon) = diacid + diamine.
- Terylene, Dacron and PET are one polymer; count 10 carbons in its repeating unit.
- Terylene is classified as a **fibre** in classification by molecular forces; Glyptal is a resin used in paints.
- Hydrolysis with acid or base breaks the ester links and returns the diol and diacid (the reverse of formation).
