## What is a carbocation?

A **carbocation** is an intermediate in which a carbon carries a positive charge and has only **six valence electrons**. The cationic carbon is **sp² hybridised and trigonal planar**, with an **empty p orbital** perpendicular to the plane. Because its octet is incomplete, a carbocation is a **Lewis acid and electrophile**, and most carbocations live only for a moment before a nucleophile or base captures them.

| Property | Carbocation R₃C⁺ |
|---|---|
| Electrons on cationic C | 6 |
| Hybridisation, shape | sp², trigonal planar (empty p orbital) |
| Behaves as | electrophile, Lewis acid |
| Lifetime | very short (observable only in superacids) |

In a **vinyl cation** (CH₂=CH⁺) the charged carbon is linear, and in a **phenyl cation** the charge sits in an in-plane sp² orbital that cannot overlap with the ring π system, so both are very unstable.

## How carbocations are formed

| Starting material | Reagent | Step |
|---|---|---|
| Alkyl halide | Lewis acid (AlCl₃, SbCl₅) or a polar solvent | R–X loses X⁻ |
| Alkene | H⁺ (HX, H₂SO₄) | π bond takes H⁺, Markovnikov carbon becomes C⁺ |
| Alcohol | H⁺ | R–OH₂⁺ loses water |
| 1° aliphatic amine | HNO₂ (NaNO₂ + HCl) | R–N₂⁺ loses N₂ |

<Eq ce="(CH3)3C-Cl + AlCl3 -> (CH3)3C+ + AlCl4-" />
<Eq ce="CH3-CH=CH2 + H+ -> CH3-CH+-CH3" />
<Eq ce="R-OH + H+ -> R-OH2+ -> R+ + H2O" />
<Eq ce="R-NH2 + HNO2 ->[-H2O] R-N=N-OH ->[H+][-H2O] R-N2+ -> R+ + N2 ^" />

An aliphatic diazonium ion is so unstable that it loses N₂ at once, which is why primary amines with nitrous acid give mixtures of alcohols, alkenes and rearranged products (see [Demjanov rearrangement](/reactions/demjanov-rearrangement/)).

## Stability in brief

**3° > 2° > 1° > CH₃⁺**, because alkyl groups release electrons by the inductive effect and by [hyperconjugation](/concepts/hyperconjugation/). Resonance (allyl, benzyl), donation from cyclopropyl rings and aromaticity (tropylium) give extra stabilisation, while vinyl and phenyl cations are the least stable. The full order with reasons is on [stability of reaction intermediates](/comparisons/stability-of-reaction-intermediates/).

### Dancing resonance (cyclopropylmethyl cation)

The C–C bonds of a cyclopropane ring are bent and rich in p character. When a CH₂⁺ is attached directly to the ring (charge **outside** the three-membered ring), these bonds overlap with the empty p orbital and spread the positive charge into the ring. Indian textbooks call this **dancing resonance**. Each extra cyclopropyl group adds more stabilisation, so tricyclopropylmethyl > dicyclopropylmethyl > cyclopropylmethyl cation.

The resonance structures connect three ions: cyclopropylmethyl, cyclobutyl and but-3-enyl (homoallyl). In reality these interconvert through one common nonclassical ion, and solvolysis of cyclopropylmethyl, cyclobutyl or homoallyl derivatives is unusually fast for that reason. In exam answers a cyclobutyl cation is usually drawn contracting to the better-stabilised cyclopropylmethyl cation.

## Rearrangement: 1,2-shifts

A carbocation rearranges when a group on the **neighbouring carbon** can move, **with its bonding electron pair**, to the cationic carbon and leave a **more stable** cation behind.

<KeyPoint title="Rule for shifts">
Shift only if the new cation is at least as stable: 1° to 2° or 3°, 2° to 3°, or to a resonance-stabilised cation. The group moves with its electron pair, so the charge moves the opposite way, onto the carbon it left.
</KeyPoint>

**Hydride shift.** Adding HCl to 3-methylbut-1-ene gives a 2° cation, which becomes 3° by a 1,2-H shift, so 2-chloro-2-methylbutane forms alongside the expected product.

<Eq ce="(CH3)2CH-CH+-CH3 ->[\text{1,2-H shift}] (CH3)2C+-CH2CH3" />

**Methyl (alkyl) shift.** With 3,3-dimethylbut-1-ene and HCl, a methyl group moves from the quaternary carbon, and rearranged 2-chloro-2,3-dimethylbutane forms. Similarly, neopentyl bromide in ethanol (SN1) gives tert-pentyl ethyl ether, never the neopentyl ether.

<Eq ce="(CH3)3C-CH+-CH3 ->[\text{1,2-CH3 shift}] (CH3)2C+-CH(CH3)2" />

## Migratory aptitude

| Group | Tendency to migrate |
|---|---|
| H⁻ and aryl | highest (relative order depends on the reaction) |
| Alkyl | 3° > 2° > 1° > methyl |
| Para-substituted aryl | p-CH₃O-C₆H₄ > p-CH₃-C₆H₄ > C₆H₅ > p-NO₂-C₆H₄ |
| Ortho-substituted aryl | migrates poorly (steric hindrance) |

Electron-donating groups help an aryl ring carry the charge during the shift (p-anisyl migrates far faster than phenyl); electron-withdrawing groups slow it.

<ExamTip title="Migratory aptitude is not universal">
Different reactions give different orders: one standard summary for the pinacol rearrangement puts phenyl ahead of hydride, another puts hydride first. In exam answers, first form the most stable carbocation, then let the best electron-donating group (H, an activated aryl or a 3° alkyl) shift. Worked case: on a carbon carrying p-nitrophenyl, phenyl and o-methoxyphenyl next to CH₂⁺, the phenyl group is chosen to migrate, because p-nitrophenyl is deactivated and the ortho-substituted ring is hindered.
</ExamTip>

## Ring expansion and ring contraction

A ring C–C bond can also shift. If the cationic carbon sits next to a **strained ring**, migration of a ring bond enlarges the ring by one carbon and relieves strain, even when the new cation is less substituted.

- **Cyclobutyl → cyclopentyl:** a 3° cation next to a cyclobutane ring expands to a 2° cyclopentyl cation, which can then become 3° by a further shift.
- **Cyclopentyl → cyclohexyl:** 1-cyclopentylethyl cation expands to the 2-methylcyclohexyl cation, and a 1,2-H shift then gives the 3° 1-methylcyclohexyl cation.

<Eq ce="\text{1-cyclopentylethyl}^+ ->[\text{ring expansion}] \text{2-methylcyclohexyl}^+ ->[\text{1,2-H shift}] \text{1-methylcyclohexyl}^+" />

- **With an OH on the ring carbon** the expanded cation is captured by the oxygen lone pair as C=OH⁺, and loss of H⁺ gives a ketone: the (1-hydroxycyclopropyl)methyl cation becomes **cyclobutanone**. This is the [Tiffeneau–Demjanov](/reactions/demjanov-rearrangement/) ring expansion, which works for rings from cyclopropane to cyclooctane.
- **Bicyclic terpenes:** HCl adds to α-pinene (four-membered ring in a bicyclo[3.1.1] skeleton) and a Wagner–Meerwein shift gives bornyl chloride (bicyclo[2.2.1]), relieving the four-membered-ring strain.
