## What is leaving group ability?

A **leaving group** is the atom or group that departs with the bonding pair of electrons when a bond to carbon breaks, as X⁻ does in R–X + Nu⁻ → R–Nu + X⁻. **Leaving group ability** measures how easily it departs. Since it must carry away extra electron density, the best leaving groups are **weak bases** that form stable anions or stable neutral molecules.

<KeyPoint title="Rule">
Leaving group ability ∝ 1 / basicity ∝ stability of the departing species ∝ strength of its conjugate acid.
</KeyPoint>

## The order of leaving groups

Approximate order for SN1 and SN2 reactions at sp³ carbon:

**–N₂⁺ (as N₂) > R–OR₂⁺ (as ether) > triflate (TfO⁻) > I⁻ ≈ tosylate, mesylate > Br⁻ > H₂O (from –OH₂⁺) > Cl⁻ > F⁻ > RCOO⁻ > ArO⁻ > OH⁻, RO⁻ > NH₂⁻ > H⁻, R⁻**

| Leaving group | Leaves as | Conjugate acid | Quality |
|---|---|---|---|
| –N₂⁺ (diazonium) | N₂ gas | (none, very stable molecule) | best; N₂ escapes as a gas |
| –OSO₂CF₃ (triflate, –OTf) | CF₃SO₃⁻ | triflic acid, a superacid | "super" leaving group |
| –OTs, –OMs, –ONs (sulfonates) | ArSO₃⁻, CH₃SO₃⁻ | strong sulfonic acids | excellent |
| –I, –Br | I⁻, Br⁻ | HI, HBr (strong acids) | very good, good |
| –OH₂⁺ (protonated alcohol) | H₂O | H₃O⁺ | good |
| –Cl | Cl⁻ | HCl | fair |
| –F | F⁻ | HF (weak acid) | poor |
| –OCOCH₃ (acetate) | CH₃COO⁻ | acetic acid (weak) | poor |
| –OH, –OR, –NH₂ | OH⁻, RO⁻, NH₂⁻ | water, alcohols, ammonia (very weak) | do not leave in SN1/SN2 |

Among sulfonates, exam answers use **triflate > nosylate (p-nitrobenzenesulfonate) > tosylate > mesylate**: the more electron-withdrawing the group on sulphur (CF₃, then NO₂C₆H₄), the more the negative charge is spread and the weaker the base.

<ExamTip title="Common mistake">
Do not put acetate (CH₃COO⁻) above Br⁻ or Cl⁻. Although its charge is delocalised over two oxygens, acetate is the conjugate base of a weak acid and is far more basic than any halide except F⁻ in polar aprotic solvents. Carboxylates, like phenoxides, are poor leaving groups in substitution at sp³ carbon.
</ExamTip>

## Why the order looks like this

1. **Basicity (electronegativity across a period).** Across a row, the more electronegative atom holds its electrons more tightly and is less basic: NH₂⁻ > OH⁻ > F⁻ in basicity, so F⁻ is the best leaving group of the three. But none of them is a useful leaving group in practice.
2. **Size (down a group).** Down the halogen group, the anion becomes larger and more polarisable and the C–X bond weaker, so basicity falls and leaving ability rises: **I⁻ > Br⁻ > Cl⁻ > F⁻**. NCERT states that for a given alkyl group the reactivity is RI > RBr > RCl ≫ RF in both SN1 and SN2.
3. **Resonance.** In a sulfonate the negative charge is spread over three oxygens, and electron-withdrawing groups (CF₃, NO₂) spread it further. That is why tosylates and triflates leave so easily.
4. **Neutral leaving groups from cations.** A positively charged substrate can lose a stable neutral molecule: R–N₂⁺ loses N₂, R–OH₂⁺ loses H₂O, R–OR₂⁺ loses an ether.

## Turning a poor leaving group into a good one

The –OH of an alcohol cannot leave as OH⁻. Two standard fixes:

<Eq ce="R-OH + H+ <=> R-OH2+ -> R+ + H2O" />

- **Protonation** with HX, H₂SO₄ or a Lewis acid (ZnCl₂ in the Lucas reagent) gives –OH₂⁺, which leaves as water.
- **Conversion to a sulfonate ester** with TsCl or MsCl in pyridine gives R–OTs or R–OMs, which react with nucleophiles about as readily as alkyl iodides.

## Context matters

- **Nucleophile dependence.** With ethoxide, relative rates (Br = 1) are Cl 0.0024, I 1.9 and OTs 3.6, so tosylate beats iodide; with a thiolate nucleophile, iodide and even bromide leave faster than tosylate.
- **SNAr reactions.** In [nucleophilic aromatic substitution](/reactions/nucleophilic-aromatic-substitution/) the slow step is addition of the nucleophile, so the order reverses: Ar–F reacts fastest because fluorine's strong –I effect stabilises the anionic intermediate.
- **Friedel–Crafts alkylation.** The halide leaves as a complex with AlCl₃, and the order is RF > RCl > RBr > RI; see [Friedel–Crafts alkylation](/reactions/friedel-crafts-alkylation/).
- **Acyl substitution.** Even poor leaving groups (RO⁻, R₂N⁻) are expelled from a tetrahedral intermediate, because re-forming the strong C=O bond drives the step.

## Solved examples

**1. Arrange in decreasing leaving ability: Cl⁻, NH₂⁻, CH₃COO⁻, OH⁻.**
Weaker base leaves better. **Answer:** Cl⁻ > CH₃COO⁻ > OH⁻ > NH₂⁻.

**2. Which reacts fastest with NaCN in DMSO: CH₃CH₂Cl, CH₃CH₂OTs, CH₃CH₂F?**
Tosylate is the weakest base of the three. **Answer:** CH₃CH₂OTs > CH₃CH₂Cl > CH₃CH₂F.

**3. Why does an aryl diazonium salt lose its group so easily?**
The leaving group is N₂, an extremely stable neutral molecule whose escape as a gas makes the step irreversible; see [diazonium salt reactions](/mechanisms/diazonium-salt-reactions/).
