## What is the ortho effect?

The **ortho effect** is the unusual behaviour of a benzene derivative when a substituent sits **ortho** (1,2) to a –COOH or –NH₂ group. Two results are tested:

1. **Acids:** nearly every ortho-substituted benzoic acid is **stronger** than benzoic acid and usually stronger than its meta and para isomers, whether the group is electron-withdrawing or electron-releasing.
2. **Bases:** nearly every ortho-substituted aniline is a **weaker** base than aniline and than its meta and para isomers.

Both effects come mainly from steric crowding between the ortho group and the functional group, together with the usual inductive and resonance effects.

## Why ortho-substituted benzoic acids are stronger

In benzoic acid, the –COOH group lies in the plane of the ring, and the ring's π electrons are conjugated with the C=O group. This conjugation stabilises the **undissociated acid** more than the carboxylate ion, so it makes benzoic acid weaker (it is the reason benzoic acid, pKa 4.2, is weaker than formic acid, pKa 3.74).

An ortho group bumps into the –COOH group and **twists it out of the ring plane**. The p orbitals of the ring and of the carboxyl carbon are no longer parallel, conjugation is lost, and the acid becomes stronger. This twisting is an example of [steric inhibition of resonance](/concepts/steric-inhibition-of-resonance/).

### pKa data for substituted benzoic acids

| Substituent | ortho | meta | para |
|---|---|---|---|
| –H | 4.2 | 4.2 | 4.2 |
| –CH₃ | 3.9 | 4.3 | 4.4 |
| –F | 3.3 | 4.3 | 4.4 |
| –Cl | 2.9 | 3.8 | 4.0 |
| –Br | 2.8 | 3.8 | 4.0 |
| –OCH₃ | 4.1 | 4.1 | 4.5 |
| –OH | 2.98 | 4.08 | 4.58 |
| –NO₂ | 2.2 | 3.5 | 3.4 |

Even the electron-releasing –CH₃ group makes o-toluic acid stronger than benzoic acid, while in the para position it makes the acid weaker.

## Order of ortho substituents (exam rule)

<KeyPoint title="Ortho group order for acid strength">
**−M > −H > −I > IMHB > +I > +H > +R**

Example series: o-nitrobenzoic acid (−M) > o-(trichloromethyl)benzoic acid (−H) > o-chlorobenzoic acid (−I) > salicylic acid (IMHB) > o-toluic acid (+I, +H).
</KeyPoint>

Here −H means the electron-withdrawing (reverse) hyperconjugation of a group such as –CCl₃, and IMHB means **intramolecular hydrogen bonding**: in the salicylate ion, the ortho –OH forms a hydrogen bond with the carboxylate oxygen and stabilises it, so salicylic acid (pKa 2.97) is much stronger than its meta and para isomers.

<Trick>
मही इमहब इहार: read the letters as −**M**, −**H**, −**I**, **IMHB**, +**I**, +**H**, +**R**.
</Trick>

<ExamTip title="Common mistake">
The rule "every ortho group strengthens benzoic acid" has exceptions. Anthranilic acid (o-aminobenzoic acid) has carboxyl pKa 4.85, so it is **weaker** than benzoic acid, because the strong +R amino group (and zwitterion formation) outweighs the steric effect. o-Methoxybenzoic acid (4.1) is only marginally stronger than benzoic acid.
</ExamTip>

## Ortho effect in anilines

When aniline is protonated, the nitrogen changes from nearly sp² to sp³, and the –NH₃⁺ group becomes non-planar and bulkier. An ortho group clashes with the hydrogens of this –NH₃⁺ group, so the anilinium ion is destabilised and the base is weaker. This **steric inhibition of protonation** gives:

| Series | Basicity order |
|---|---|
| Toluidines | p-toluidine > m-toluidine > aniline > o-toluidine |
| Nitroanilines | aniline > m-nitroaniline > p-nitroaniline > o-nitroaniline |
| Haloanilines | aniline > p-haloaniline > m-haloaniline > o-haloaniline |
| Aminophenols (pKb) | p- (8.50) > o- (9.28) > aniline (9.38) > m- (9.80) |

With two ortho methyl groups, as in 2,6-dimethylaniline, the crowding is greater still and the base is weaker than aniline. For p-substituted anilines, the normal electronic trend holds: –OCH₃ (pKa of conjugate acid 5.34) and –CH₃ (5.08) raise basicity, while –Cl (3.98) and –NO₂ (1.00) lower it, relative to aniline (4.63).

Note the aminophenols: o-aminophenol is still more basic than aniline, so the ortho effect in amines is a strong trend rather than an absolute law.

## Solved examples

**1. Arrange in order of acid strength: benzoic acid, o-toluic acid, p-toluic acid.**
o-Toluic acid (3.9) > benzoic acid (4.2) > p-toluic acid (4.4). Ortho effect beats the +I effect at ortho; at para only the electron-releasing effect acts.

**2. Arrange: o-, m-, p-chlorobenzoic acid.**
ortho (2.9) > meta (3.8) > para (4.0). Ortho effect plus the strongest −I at the nearest position; at para the +M of Cl partly opposes −I.

**3. Arrange in order of basicity: aniline, o-toluidine, p-toluidine.**
p-Toluidine > aniline > o-toluidine. The para methyl releases electrons; the ortho methyl causes steric inhibition of protonation.

**4. Which is the strongest acid: salicylic acid, o-chlorobenzoic acid, o-nitrobenzoic acid?**
o-Nitrobenzoic acid (2.2) > o-chlorobenzoic acid (2.9) ≈ salicylic acid (2.97), following −M > −I > IMHB.
